## Core finding

The Fifth Amendment can protect **valuable contractual interests**, but a government act that merely frustrates a private contract does not necessarily take that contract. Going-concern value may be compensable when the government actually appropriates economically transferable business advantages, especially in the particular temporary-occupancy circumstances recognized in *Kimball Laundry*; that case does **not** set a universal rule that all business goodwill, lost profits, or future Starlink revenue would automatically be paid. Distinguish each property interest taken, property interests merely affected, duration, transferable value, and statutory compensation rules.

## Source and evidence index

| Evidence ID | U.S. Supreme Court authority | Supported holding relevant to asset-by-asset compensation | Limitation / counterargument |
|---|---|---|---|
| IC-01 | [*Omnia Commercial Co. v. United States*, 261 U.S. 502 (1923)](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/261/502/) | Valuable contract rights are property, but 1917 wartime government requisitioning that prevented contract performance was held to **frustrate**, not appropriate, the disappointed buyer's contract; no compensation for that loss on these facts. | No ruling on direct legislative vesting of customer/vendor contracts, orbital authorizations or a modern satellite-network enterprise. |
| IC-02 | [*United States v. General Motors Corp.*, 323 U.S. 373 (1945)](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/323/373/) | For temporary wartime occupancy of a part of leased premises, some reasonable removal/storage expenses may inform temporary rental market value rather than stand as automatically separate compensation. | Not a universal entitlement to all business disruption costs; Court distinguished excluded goodwill/injury to business on those facts. |
| IC-03 | [*United States v. Petty Motor Co.*, 327 U.S. 372 (1946)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/327/372) | When whole remaining leasehold interests were taken, compensation measured value of the property interest, with moving/reinstallation costs and business losses generally not independently added. | Distinguishes *General Motors*' shorter partial occupancy; does not decide the value of appropriated intellectual property or government radio licenses. |
| IC-04 | [*Kimball Laundry Co. v. United States*, 338 U.S. 1 (1949)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/338/1) | During temporary military occupation of a laundry, appropriation of transferable value of established trade routes warranted compensation if established by evidence, in addition to compensation for temporary plant use. | A special temporary taking of a business and its transferable customer-route advantage; not per se compensation for all goodwill in a permanent acquisition. Dissent disputed award. |
| IC-05 | [Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 71.1](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/federal_rules_of_civil_procedure_december_1_2024_1.pdf) | Condemnation court procedure can address real or personal property and calls for the **authority for the taking** to be pleaded. | Rule itself supplies **no substantive federal taking authority**; exact adopted statutory mechanism and updated applicable rules need counsel review. |

## Contradictions and adversarial review

- These opinions do not establish a general entitlement to claim all lost Starlink profits or private parent-company market capitalization in just compensation. *Petty Motor* specifically resists adding consequential business losses to value of interests taken.
- Conversely, treating the potential acquisition as mere physical hardware undervalues interests if the law and actual taking encompass transferable contracts, IP or demonstrable business advantages; *Omnia* and *Kimball Laundry* require attention to the character of what is taken.
- No legal source in this pass authorizes an acquisition. Congress's power, actual statute, compensation tribunal, congressional appropriations and licenses must be separately proven.
- No double counting between going-concern value, cash-flow valuations, customer relationships, intangible rights, parent equity or liabilities. Coordinate but **do not alter** Item 5's standalone model.
