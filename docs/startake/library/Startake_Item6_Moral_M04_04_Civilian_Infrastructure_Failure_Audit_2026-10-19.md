## Finding

Federal policy supplies targeted routes to extend communications to areas without service: the Universal Service Fund’s carrier-designation process can address a requesting unserved community under statutory conditions, high-cost support recipients can have area-specific broadband availability duties, and the BEAD program funds deployment in unserved or underserved locations. Those routes are conditional on covered services, designated carriers, particular support areas, applications, or awards. They do not create a generally applicable federal duty for every commercial satellite-broadband operator to provide an alternate connection to every community whenever terrestrial networks are absent, damaged, or overloaded.

The statutory unserved-community mechanism is the material qualification to a broad negative conclusion. Under 47 U.S.C. § 214(e)(3), when no common carrier will provide a federally supported service to a requesting unserved community, the appropriate Commission shall determine which carrier or carriers are best able and order them to provide that service, with ETC designation. The trigger is a request by an unserved community and the statute is limited to common carriers and services supported under § 254(c). It is not an automatic response to temporary damage, congestion, or overload, and the sources reviewed do not establish that a particular satellite broadband provider or offering is covered. Fixed and mobile satellite common-carriage treatment is a Commission determination under § 153(51); this audit does not resolve it.

No current provider-specific coverage, performance, outage, customer, contract, or community-use facts were established. The conclusion is limited to the public federal materials reviewed. It does not negate state, Tribal, local, grant-specific, contract-specific, or fact-dependent obligations.

### Distinguishing absence from interruption

A persistent “unserved community” can invoke the statutory process in § 214(e)(3), subject to the service and carrier conditions above. A location with a network that is temporarily damaged or overloaded presents a different fact pattern. The cited Universal Service Fund, high-cost, and BEAD materials do not establish that those conditions automatically trigger a satellite provider’s duty to furnish alternate service. Whether an existing carrier must maintain or restore a particular service, whether an emergency priority applies, and whether an outage-reporting rule covers an offering are separate questions addressed in the prior M04.02 and M04.08 audits.

Federal programs can also pay to build or improve networks in defined areas. A grant-funded deployment duty may be enforceable against its recipient under award terms; no award or Starlink recipient status was authenticated here. The existence of a deployment grant program should not be treated as proof that a particular community is served, that the service is resilient to terrestrial-network failure, or that every user is entitled to service.

**Disposition:** No Bill v1.14 or financial-workbook v0.4 change is recommended from this evidence pass. If the policy objective is an ongoing public satellite alternative for all communities whose terrestrial systems fail or lack service, that is a separate legislative choice. It would require defined eligible communities and users, qualifying triggers and duration, service and performance standards, funding, priority rules, responsibility for terminals and power, privacy and security controls, and a relationship to existing ETC, grant, and State/Tribal programs. The current Bill’s transition and planning safeguards do not make that choice implicitly.

## Contrary considerations, gaps, and status

The strongest contrary points are the § 214(e)(3) unserved-community pathway; high-cost recipients’ specific broadband-availability obligations; the BEAD deployment program; and CISA’s resilience-planning objectives. Each is relevant to access or resilience, but each is conditional or programmatic and does not establish a universal obligation for commercial satellite broadband during community-wide terrestrial absence, damage, or overload.

Before any formal acceptance or provider-specific conclusion, determine the exact service and operator classification; whether an affected place is persistently unserved, underserved, or temporarily disconnected; any applicable ETC designation, support area, grant award, contract, or public mission; and the locality’s actual service, power, terminal, and backhaul constraints. Establishing service in a particular community would require current coverage and performance evidence, availability/affordability data, outage and restoration records, and customer or public-agency requirements. None was obtained here.
