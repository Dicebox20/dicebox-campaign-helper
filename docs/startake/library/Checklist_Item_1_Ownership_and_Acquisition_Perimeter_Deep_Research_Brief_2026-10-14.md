## Research objectives — answer each with evidence and counterevidence

A. **Entity and beneficial-ownership chain.** Identify every relevant U.S./foreign Starlink operating, licensing, asset-holding, contracting and IP-owning entity; parent/subsidiary chains; public-record beneficial/economic/voting ownership; share and control rights. Carefully distinguish known persons/entities from unsupported guesses about shareholders, SpaceX or Musk ownership, and ultimate compensation recipients. Date every snapshot.

B. **Functional acquisition inventory.** Enumerate operational spacecraft and constellation assets; gateways, teleports, ground stations and backhaul; spectrum/licensing entitlements (not private ownership of frequencies); network operations/control and telemetry systems; terminals, inventory and distribution; services, subscriptions and customer data; software, firmware, source code and patents; customer and supplier contracts; labor and technical expertise; operational permits; goodwill and liabilities. Mark each item **IN / SHARED / DEPENDENCY / OUT / UNKNOWN** for the Starlink-only scope. Identify legal owner, geographic/regulatory constraints, source and confidence.

C. **Perimeter alternatives and legal ownership consequences.** Compare negotiated asset purchase, segregated subsidiary/equity purchase, tailored statutory condemnation, license/right transfer, transition-service agreements and public-utility/regulatory alternatives. Identify whose legally compensable interests are affected, what could be separated from SpaceX, and where constitutional, FCC and cross-border approvals limit transferability. Do not treat all options as equally viable by assumption.

D. **Shared SpaceX resources and separability.** Document launch, manufacturing, satellite buses, ground systems, factories, IP, cyber/security operations, mission control, personnel, insurance, supply chain, funding, cash management, facilities, tax and contractual cross-subsidies. For each dependency assess continued service through transitional contracts, licensing, independent duplication or external suppliers, with cost and failure risks. Differentiate technical separability from legal title and commercial viability.

E. **Starshield/classified separation.** Determine only publicly supportable program boundaries, shared resources, contracts and interfaces; do not expose or conjecture classified details. Record which questions require secure government/contractor diligence, and ensure no blanket Starshield inclusion.

F. **Regulatory/sovereignty chain.** Inventory FCC NGSO and earth-station authorizations, responsible licensees, transfer/change-of-control procedures and pending regulatory proceedings; national spectrum allocations, ITU coordination/filings, United Nations registration of space objects and host-country telecommunications/landing/market access. Confirm that regulatory authorization is not fee-simple spectrum property.

G. **Liabilities, encumbrances and continuity.** Map liens and secured-creditor claims, contracts with change-of-control limits, export-controls, IP licenses, litigation, warranties, orbital debris and space-liability claims, data protection, employees/benefits, tax, cybersecurity and foreign-country obligations. Distinguish publicly documented liabilities from potential categories requiring diligence.

H. **Valuation boundary only.** Identify which entity/asset rights and obligations belong in a future **Starlink-only** valuation without inventing a valuation or conflating SpaceX parent valuation, net worth, Starshield revenue, government ceiling awards, or procurement disbursements. Defer the full financial model to checklist item 5 while making a required-data handoff.

I. **Contrary case and unknowns.** Present strongest evidence **against** clean separability or beneficial-ownership certainty, arguments against public acquisition as necessary, potential sovereign/innovation/service risks, alternative remedies, and nonpublic due-diligence requirements. Clearly distinguish primary facts, reasonable inference, scenario assumptions and unknowns.

## Source and verification method

- Use primary records first: FCC Licensing and Management System / IBFS successor, ULS and Commission orders/dockets; corporate and securities filings from SEC EDGAR; appropriate state registries; ITU and UNOOSA filings; USPTO assignments; Federal Acquisition/USAspending contract records (distinguish obligation, outlay, ceiling and award); published corporate disclosures, government IG audits, procurement and court records, relevant legislative text and federal regulations. Exact authority, filing date, entity name, page/paragraph/docket/license ID and live source URL must accompany each material claim.
- **Do not assert an exact shareholder cap table or complete subsidiary tree absent verifiable documents.** Private-company records and classified contract details may not be public. State the access limits and name which records government/SpaceX diligence would require.
- Inspect material later-than-source changes with an **as-of** date; separate reported activity from authorized capacity, owned property, granted licenses, commercial service and legal rights.
- Cross-check each high-impact fact against another authoritative record when available. Preserve contradicted facts and explain relative source reliability. No unsupported inference that FCC licenses themselves constitute private ownership of radio frequencies.
- Use secondary reporting to find leads and identify disputes, not to launder disputed claims into certainty. No fabricated citations or invented numeric valuations.
