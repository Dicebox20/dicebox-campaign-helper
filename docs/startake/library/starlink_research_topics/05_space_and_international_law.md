## Research question

If the United States acquired or established public operation of Starlink's communications system, which international treaties, country-specific authorizations, international spectrum-coordination rules, space-object duties, and cross-border technology/data restrictions would govern lawful ownership, continued operation, and service continuity?

**Do not infer:** U.S. acquisition automatically makes every existing foreign permission transferable; registration proves private title to an orbit or band; a commercial authorization is a sovereign right to operate everywhere; or changing an operator extinguishes an existing launching State's treaty liability.

## Preliminary evidence register — sources checked, conclusions limited

| Area | Initial source and provisional finding | Outstanding verification |
|---|---|---|
| State responsibility/supervision | [UNOOSA, Outer Space Treaty](https://www.unoosa.org/oosa/en/ourwork/spacelaw/treaties/outerspacetreaty.html), especially Articles VI–IX. Article VI places international responsibility for national activities on States and requires authorization/continuing supervision of nongovernmental space activities. | Apply to the specific acquired entity/operator, licensing laws and possible federal-operation model; examine differences between state and private operators. |
| Jurisdiction/control and registry | [UNOOSA, Registration Convention](https://www.unoosa.org/oosa/en/ourwork/spacelaw/treaties/registration-convention.html), especially Articles I–IV. The registering launching State and registry records are legally important; with multiple launching States, they determine which registers the object. | Obtain current U.S. and UNOOSA registrations and launch-State designations for relevant Starlink objects; determine required notices/updates if ownership or control changes. |
| International liability | [UNOOSA, Liability Convention](https://www.unoosa.org/oosa/en/ourwork/spacelaw/treaties/liability-convention.html), especially Articles II–V. International liability concerns launching States and varies by where damage occurs and fault rules. | Map launching State(s), incidents, insurance, private indemnification, contractual allocation and continuing sovereign exposure. |
| ITU coordination | [ITU, Regulation of Satellite Systems](https://www.itu.int/en/mediacentre/backgrounders/Pages/Regulation-of-Satellite-Systems.aspx) and [ITU, Space Services Department](https://www.itu.int/en/ITU-R/space/Pages/Default.aspx). National administrations submit/co-ordinate/notify assignments; the MIFR records statuses. | Obtain exact U.S. administration/filings, frequency rights, notifications, ITU coordination history, deployment milestone compliance, and interference disputes; verify transition mechanics. |
| Orbital sustainability | Outer Space Treaty Article IX and applicable domestic disposal/debris requirements provide a starting framework. | Document relevant regulatory debris/mitigation licenses and orbit-specific collision-avoidance responsibilities; no blanket compliance claim. |
| Foreign authorizations | Country rules for landing rights, gateway earth stations, terminals, spectrum use and telecom services may apply independently of U.S. ownership. | For each jurisdiction: identify regulator, license holder, legal basis, expiry, transfer/change-of-control approval, national-security/foreign ownership limits, and emergency continuity options. |
| Sanctions and exports | U.S. sanctions and export controls (including ITAR/EAR where applicable) require fact- and item-specific analysis. | Get classification/jurisdiction determinations and authorization terms for hardware, code, encryption, technical data, defense-related services and recipients; avoid assuming all Starlink technology is ITAR-controlled. |
| Cybersecurity and data | Local data protection, localization, lawful interception and telecom security requirements can affect continued cross-border operations. | Identify the actual processor/controller, relevant systems and binding national rules; differentiate subscribers' states and gateway/server locations. |
| International continuity/diplomacy | Treaty continuity and national licensing are distinct from commercial contract assignment; jurisdictional obligations must be analyzed separately. | Draft notice/consent sequences and diplomatic plan including countries with conflict or humanitarian operations. |

**Assessment so far:** The primary sources establish the existence of multiple overlapping legal regimes. They do **not** establish that any particular country has approved a hypothetical public acquisition or that the U.S. already has a fully verified transition route. No definitive treaty-conflict conclusion or acquisition legality opinion is made here.
