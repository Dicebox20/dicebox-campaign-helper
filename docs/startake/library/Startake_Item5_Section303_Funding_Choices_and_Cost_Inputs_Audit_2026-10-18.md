# Item 5 — §303 funding choices and cost-input audit

**Date:** 18 October 2026  
**Status:** Bounded research and drafting support; no funding amount, fiscal year, valuation, score, or formal acceptance is established.

## Purpose and scope

This audit turns the five unresolved funding placeholders in integrated working Bill v1.12 §303 into an evidence-linked decision schedule. It covers (1) the fiscal year for due diligence, (2) the amount for due diligence, review, and transition planning, (3) the end of that appropriation's availability, (4) the acquisition/compensation/transition appropriation amount, and (5) the continuity-reserve amount. It also records the adjacent choice whether acquisition funds are available until expended or for a fixed period.

This is distinct from earlier work on the wording/interface for §303 and from the previous valuation-model review. It does not decide the transaction perimeter, set a price, estimate a federal score, update the bill or workbook, or alter any requirements-acceptance field. Item 1 remains outside this assignment. The owner of these policy choices is Congress; agency budget, acquisition, counsel, accounting, engineering, and independent valuation specialists must provide evidence within their respective roles.

## Controlling record and model status

Bill v1.12 §303 currently contains five unresolved placeholders. It separates due-diligence/review/transition-planning funds from acquisition, compensation, and transition funds; the latter are stated as available until expended. A drafting note flags the no-year versus fixed-period decision. Sections 301(b)–(c) call for a ten-year budget submission, sources-and-uses and scenarios, and cash/accrual and gross/net distinctions. Section 302 limits the fund to amounts made available by law and disclaims an independent borrowing or payment guarantee. The Judgment Fund is not automatically made applicable.

The financial workbook v0.4 is not a basis for an amount. Its selected case is “Actual — inputs missing”; Enterprise value, Equity value, Closing cash uses, ten-year nominal and discounted federal cash, and ending operator cash are all “n.a.” Its summary says commercial inputs are not authenticated and all examples are synthetic test units. It also disclaims debt issuance, a three-statement forecast, fleet-age cohorts, comparable-company analysis, and a compensation opinion. The workbook’s missing-driver count is a completeness flag, not an estimate of dollars. Historical calibration is not a forecast input.

Accordingly, the bill can responsibly specify decision authority, evidence gates, account structure, reporting, and limits now. It cannot responsibly insert numeric values or a specific fiscal year from this record.

## Appropriation architecture for the next bill draft

A practical provisional design is two separate, capped appropriations with explicit phase gates: (a) due diligence, independent review, and transition planning; and (b) acquisition, compensation, and transition, available only after Congress provides a specific amount and the statutory acquisition conditions are met. A separately stated continuity reserve may be a sublimit within (b), with release criteria, reporting, and a no-double-counting schedule. The bill should not imply that authorization alone funds either account.

The due-diligence account can use a definite availability period once a supported schedule exists. The acquisition account should remain a live policy choice: a fixed period increases time discipline but may require renewed authority if closing is delayed; a no-year term can accommodate uncertain transaction timing but requires tighter obligation gates, account controls, milestone reporting, and treatment of unobligated balances. “Until expended” must be stated expressly to create no-year availability. Select dates or periods only when the source schedule supports them.

No design in this audit creates borrowing authority, a federal credit guarantee, or automatic payment from the Judgment Fund. Any debt or credit mechanism would require separate explicit statutory authority and budget treatment. Congress should provide a specific amount and purpose; avoid treating “such sums as necessary” language in an authorization as budget authority.

## Federal budget and scorekeeping work product

The next cost package should contain (1) a provision-by-provision budget classification; (2) a year-by-year budget-authority, obligation, and outlay schedule; (3) acquisition and transition sources-and-uses; (4) ten-year and, where required, longer-term effects; (5) direct-spending, discretionary, revenue, and mandate analysis; (6) cash and accrual views; and (7) assumptions, data sources, and uncertainty/sensitivity cases. Reconcile cash payments to earned revenue, receivables, deferred revenue, working capital, and any liabilities assumed at closing so customer balances are not counted twice.

CBO’s public guidance distinguishes estimates for authorizing legislation from the budget-authority/outlay information it produces for appropriations. CBO describes the assumptions, sources, methods, and uncertainty as part of an estimate’s basis. No CBO estimate or official score was located for this draft; this audit is an input schedule, not a score. The financial workbook is a scenario tool with unauthenticated actual inputs, not a substitute for federal scorekeeping.

## Legal and accounting controls for drafting

Appropriations must be used for their enacted purposes; a law is not construed to appropriate Treasury funds or permit obligations in excess of an appropriation unless it specifically says so. A fixed-period appropriation is available for properly incurred expenses or qualifying contracts made during its period, and obligations require documentary support. Agency obligations also remain subject to apportionment and statutory limits. The draft should state the purpose, account, amount (once supported), period of availability, conditions for obligation, transfer/reprogramming rules, reporting, and handling of unobligated balances in terms the implementing agency and appropriations counsel can administer.

These controls support a cautious drafting path: authorize due diligence and preparation, but condition any acquisition obligation on a separate specific appropriation and completion of the required valuation, perimeter, title/consent, continuity, and review evidence. They do not determine what Congress should appropriate or establish that any current model output is reliable.

## Open evidence requests and drafting gates

1. Obtain a scoped agency work breakdown and calendar for diligence, independent review, and transition planning, including labor hours, procurement plan, cost basis, and annual phasing.
2. Obtain transaction-specific commercial asset/liability, title, lien, contract, and consent records and a documented valuation protocol; commission independent valuation and legal/accounting review before stating compensation.
3. Obtain operating and engineering evidence for service continuity, failover, capacity, replacement, cyber response, and transition services; cost each scenario and reconcile it against acquisition uses.
4. Have agency budget staff prepare account classification, budget authority/obligations/outlays, cash/accrual, and ten-year submissions; request CBO technical assistance at the appropriate legislative stage.
5. Have appropriations counsel review the chosen period, no-year/fixed-period wording, apportionment, obligation, reserve, transfer, and closeout provisions.

Until these inputs arrive, keep the five §303 values visibly unresolved, keep actual-model outputs unavailable, and do not mark the funding or valuation requirements accepted. The bill and workbook remain unchanged by this audit.
