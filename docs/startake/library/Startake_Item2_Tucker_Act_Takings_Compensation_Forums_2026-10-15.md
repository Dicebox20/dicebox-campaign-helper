## Executive result

The *Tucker Act* (28 U.S.C. § 1491) is a principal **jurisdiction and monetary-remedy pathway** for qualifying claims against the United States, including a Fifth Amendment just-compensation claim when a legally cognizable taking occurred. It is **not** a substantive authorization to nationalize a satellite communications company, vest shares, compel the transfer of software, or confer spectrum rights. The *Little Tucker Act*, 28 U.S.C. § 1346(a)(2), permits concurrent district-court jurisdiction for certain claims **not exceeding $10,000**, with statutory exceptions. The default Court of Federal Claims (CFC) limitations period under § 2501 is generally **six years after accrual**; § 1500 can bar CFC jurisdiction if sufficiently related claims are already pending in another court. The distinction between a **government-initiated federal condemnation action** under Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 71.1 and an **owner-initiated inverse-condemnation money claim** under § 1491 matters in designing any acquisition statute and remedy. No authority reviewed is a self-executing transfer of Starlink assets or licences.

## Interpretation, competing readings and risk

**Strongest legally defensible limited proposition:** A compensation mechanism and judicial forum exist for some federal constitutional takings claims, even when the federal act authorizes a taking and compensation follows. This makes the availability of monetary relief a necessary planning element, not a legal conclusion that Congress can take all contemplated interests on the proposed terms. *Monsanto* demonstrates the possible availability of compensation for some trade secrets in a specific federal regulatory setting; the predicate property and facts are essential.

**Adverse proposition:** A court may find that an asserted asset is not owned by the claimant, the government's conduct is not a taking, a statutory right does not mandate money, the claim accrued too long ago, or a related pending federal action triggers §1500. A statutory compensation mechanism might also channel or alter remedies. Any proposed acquisition must confront these litigation exposures and not portray an after-the-fact damages remedy as a guaranteed authorization or automatic injunction alternative. The 10,000-dollar threshold is an **upper jurisdictional bound for the Little Tucker Act**, not a ceiling on just compensation in the CFC.

**Important distinction:** Government-filed condemnation under Rule 71.1 and owner-filed inverse condemnation under the Tucker Act have different plaintiffs, purposes, pleading/jurisdiction structures and may use different tribunals. Rule 71.1 directs an authorized condemnor to *state the authority* for taking. It does not create that authority. A land declaration-of-taking statute under 40 U.S.C. §3114 likewise cannot alone be repurposed as automatic title for satellites or equity; see prior Item 2 verification report.
