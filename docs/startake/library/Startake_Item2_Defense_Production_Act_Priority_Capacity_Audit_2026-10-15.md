# STARTAKE — Item 2: Defense Production Act priority, capacity, and acquisition-boundary audit

**Research date:** 2026-10-15 UTC. **Claim:** `STARTAKE-20261015-item2-dpa-74c18e`. **Method:** one bounded direct primary-source statute and regulation review; **not** Deep Research, an implemented government order, or independent legal advice. **Scope:** possible use of Defense Production Act (DPA) authorities as alternatives to permanent, compensated federal acquisition of **commercial Starlink communications operations alone**; not SpaceX as a whole and not presumptively Starshield/classified assets. **Overlapping named topic:** Statutory acquisition procedure and property remedies (still formally unverified).

## Executive finding

**A meaningful middle ground exists in statutory design, but it is not a proved Starlink-specific mechanism.** DPA Title I, 50 U.S.C. §4511(a), permits priority performance of qualifying contracts/orders (other than employment contracts) and allocation of materials, services, and facilities for national defense. That is an order/performance and resource-allocation power, **not general statutory vesting of Starlink shares, satellites, spectrum rights, software or global business title**. Title III includes industrial financing and purchase/capacity-development mechanisms, subject to restrictions, conditions, and appropriations. The hypothetical ability to use these tools for commercial satellite connectivity needs a distinct legal and factual eligibility analysis, properly delegated authority, authorized program, a lawful order, existing capacity, procurement terms, financing, FCC/foreign licence restrictions and litigation scrutiny. Neither §4511 nor Title III establishes the necessity of federal ownership. Crucially, §4564(a), as amended in 2026, currently sets **December 11, 2026** as the general termination date for the relevant DPA subchapters (specified exceptions and preserved existing obligations), and Title III is available only to the extent/amounts provided in appropriations acts. Future reauthorizations must be checked at actual use.

## Implications, adverse evidence and distinctions

1. **Priority service** is legally different from **asset ownership**. A qualifying rated order could, subject to legal conditions, favor particular deliveries or services over another contract. It cannot be described as government seizure of satellites or automatic transfer of ownership.
2. **Capacity development** is legally different from **compulsory enterprise purchase**. Title III loan guarantees, loans, purchase commitments and production-facility support potentially offer less intrusive substitutes for ownership but require a suitable industrial resource, findings, funding and implementation.
3. **Competing considerations:** Limited capacity, emergency prioritization and national defense could favor ordered access. Civilian service interference, unjustified cost shifts, statutory limits, speech/public accountability, foreign operations and actual technical dependencies favor narrower, transparently funded contracts or competitive redundancy procurement. A legal authority to issue certain orders does **not** demonstrate they are necessary or preferable.
4. **Cannot assume telecom exceptions or suitability:** Which cabinet department has delegated jurisdiction, whether contracted broadband capacity is within rated-order category and program eligibility, how FCC Part 25 and foreign authorizations interact, and how disputes and compensation are handled require separate primary records and counsel.
5. **Expiration is material:** September 2026 OLRC §4564 reflects a congressional 2026 extension to **December 11, 2026**; check subsequent enacted legislation rather than rely on older 2025/September 2026 sunset assertions.
