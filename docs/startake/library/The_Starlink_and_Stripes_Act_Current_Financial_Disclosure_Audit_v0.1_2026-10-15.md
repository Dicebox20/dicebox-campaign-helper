## Subscriber-to-model mapping

The following are our analytical eligibility decisions, not additional issuer statements. They use the definitions in F1 without enlarging them.

| Existing model field | Permissible use of this evidence | Required bridge before a real forecast |
|---|---|---|
| opening_service_lines | A dated observed balance may anchor a historical service-line schedule | Set valuation date and ensure eligible lines fall inside the acquired commercial scope; bridge additions, churn and any omitted cohorts from observation date to forecast opening |
| monthly_net_arpu | Historical metric can calibrate an identically defined service-revenue stream | Reconcile equipment, taxes, discounts, plan/geographic mix, numerator coverage and average line definition; label any future path as an assumption |
| gross_line_additions and annual_churn | No direct replacement from a change in period-end balances | Separate gross connections, cancellations, reactivations and movements; net growth alone does not identify both gross additions and churn |

The existing model computes annual revenue from the arithmetic midpoint of beginning and ending lines multiplied by annualized monthly ARPU, plus other revenue. That midpoint is a simplifying assumption. For rapid or uneven growth it may differ from the actual time-weighted average. A historical back-test needs monthly or otherwise defensibly weighted lines; an endpoint multiplied by twelve is not a verified annual revenue observation. Equipment and managed-contract builds need explicit reconciliation before addition. This is model-design analysis based on the saved source code, not a new asserted financial result.

## Accounting-to-standalone bridge — proposed diligence schedule

1. Start with each reported revenue category and assign every included contract/stream to an acquired, excluded or unresolved perimeter. Maintain an unresolved column; do not force allocation to make totals balance.
2. Remove excluded revenue together with its attributable operating costs, assets, liabilities, working capital and capital expenditure. Removing only revenue distorts earnings. Removing all government sales can also exclude ordinary acquired commercial services; use contract/function evidence.
3. Reconcile the acquired historical cost base to continuing services. Replace parent allocations with credible standalone staffing, systems, facilities, insurance, compliance and procurement arrangements; disclose stranded parent costs separately from acquired obligations.
4. Reconcile launch/manufacturing capitalization and future market arrangements. Avoid double counting a purchased launch in both cash operating expense and capex; do not assume internally capitalized historical costs equal negotiated future capacity pricing.
5. Reconcile recurring cash generation to cash taxes, customer prepayments, collections, inventory, replacement satellites and required investment. Recognized revenue, backlog and cash receipts are distinct schedules. There is no automatic receivable or cash available merely because a sales contract exists.
6. Build enterprise value, transaction-specific equity or asset consideration, and federal fiscal costs as separate outputs. Federal customer payments remain government outlays even when received by the public operator. Compensation and past public funding require separate legal and ledger treatment.
7. Subject every bridge to source evidence, date, units, scope, responsible reviewer and sensitivity. A model may evaluate explicitly labeled assumptions; it may not label them audited actuals or conceal acquisition-critical unknowns as zero.

These are proposed financial-method requirements. The current disclosures do not supply every bridge or a defensible purchase price. The statement is limited to the reviewed public material, not a representation that private counterparties cannot provide it.
