# Item 6 / moral M04.05 — Continuity of public services

**Bounded federal-source audit | 19 October 2026 UTC**

## Executive finding

Reviewed federal authorities impose a few targeted planning or service duties relevant to public services, but they do not establish a general federal duty for a commercial satellite broadband provider to supply a backup connection to every dispatch center, utility, school, or local government when terrestrial communications are unavailable or impaired.

Two qualifications matter. First, the Safe Drinking Water Act, as amended by AWIA §2013, requires qualifying community water systems serving more than 3,300 people to maintain risk and resilience assessments and emergency response plans. EPA's current materials recommend planning for communications loss. The statutory duty is on the water system; the statute does not prescribe satellite service or place a continuity duty on a broadband provider. Second, NERC Reliability Standard CIP-012-2 requires specifically listed bulk-electric-system entities that own or operate covered control centers to plan for loss of availability and recovery of specified real-time data communications. It permits approaches such as alternate paths and carrier service-level agreements, but does not mandate a particular carrier or satellite link.

For schools and libraries, 47 U.S.C. §254(h)(1)(B) requires a telecommunications carrier serving a geographic area, upon a bona fide request for a covered universal-service service, to provide that service at discounted rates for educational purposes, subject to statutory eligibility limits. The provision addresses conditional access and price, not uninterrupted service, emergency failover, restoration time, or redundant paths. The FCC's FY2026 E-Rate eligible-services order maintains the program's annual service-eligibility mechanism; the reviewed order does not add a general continuity standard.

CISA's National Emergency Communications Plan and FEMA's Continuity Guidance Circular help public agencies plan. They are planning frameworks and guidance, not a generally applicable satellite-provider mandate. The bill's §§104, 205, 401, and 402 already address affordability/access planning, documented critical-service obligations, transition service levels, service tests, and alternative communications paths for registered critical services during a transfer. No bill or workbook amendment is recommended from this evidence pass.

### Water-system detail

The statute requires a qualifying community water system to make its own risk and resilience assessment, incorporate the assessment into an emergency response plan, include resilience strategies/resources and hazard-response procedures, and coordinate with local emergency planning committees to the extent possible. The statutory text expressly includes the resilience of electronic, computer, and other automated systems used by the system. That is relevant to communications dependencies, but it does not itself prescribe a communications technology.

EPA's 2024 drinking-water ERP template instructions add operational guidance: utilities should plan internal and external communications and consider contingency measures for communications loss. EPA separately publishes a water-sector guide intended to help utilities diversify telecommunications plans and equipment during prolonged power outages. The guidance makes the communication-continuity issue concrete; it still does not turn the water system's planning duty into a duty for a specific commercial provider. No utility assessment, ERP, procurement, or customer agreement was reviewed.

### Electric-system detail

CIP-012-2 is the strongest reviewed example of a mandatory utility communications-continuity planning rule. It applies only to the listed bulk-electric-system functions and covered control centers. Its requirement is entity-facing: a responsible entity must document and implement methods for availability loss and link recovery for specified operational data. The examples show that a carrier SLA can be one implementation method, alongside alternatives and internal procedures. The standard leaves the covered entity to determine a compliant method and does not compel any carrier to offer a service.

## Proposed bill and workbook disposition

Crosswalk v1.22 maps M04.05 to Bill v1.14 §§104, 205, and 401–402. The reviewed findings support that mapping:

- **§104(3)** makes funded, executable critical-service continuity arrangements a condition to closing. It is a transaction gate, not a permanent service guarantee.
- **§205(a), (c)–(e)** requires published terms and outage procedures, an affordability/rural-access plan and measured access baseline that expressly includes schools and emergency services, and notice/review/restoration rules for emergency restrictions. It does not promise universal availability or create an identified public-sector uptime threshold.
- **§401(a), (c)–(d)** requires transition planning for customer and emergency-service obligations, measurable contract service levels and a funded exit plan, and a register of contracted services whose interruption would foreseeably threaten life, emergency response, or essential public functions. The register is based on documented obligations and risk; customer status as a school, hospital, or government is not enough by itself.
- **§402(b), (d)–(e)** requires approved service tolerances and cutover tests for registered critical services, including tested independently supplied communications alternatives or a documented, funded interim arrangement meeting mission tolerances. These protections attach to the affected transition/cutover and registered service; they do not set a continuing, universal satellite-service obligation after transition.

No bill-text or workbook change is recommended. The federal examples support risk-based planning, conditional access rules, and sector-specific operator obligations. They do not establish a missing general provider mandate that this proposal should add without a separate policy decision, funding, classification analysis, and engineering evidence.
