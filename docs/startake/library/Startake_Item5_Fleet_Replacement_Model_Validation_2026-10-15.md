## Result

Delivered a reproducible ten-year deterministic cohort/inventory/launch/commissioning companion. Eighteen tests pass. This closes the absence of an executable fleet-age mechanism; it does not close missing actual fleet, generation, cost, engineering or supplier evidence. No actual Starlink value or continuity finding is produced. Existing enginev0.2, workbookv0.3 and Billv1.4 remain unchanged.

Retirement follows each opening cohort's specified remaining years. Unplanned operating losses are entered explicitly, allocated oldest first after scheduled retirements. New manufacturing enters inventory, available launch slots constrain loading, launch losses are explicit, and successful units enter a commissioning pipeline. Delay is measured in whole years. New units receive their specified operating life after commissioning. Year-end unit targets yield explicit deficits or surpluses; the algorithm does not silently fill a shortfall. Purchased launches are paid even when partly empty. Conservation checks include operating units, inventory, pipeline, retirements and losses.

The synthetic fixture starts with five cohorts of20, residual lives1–5,20new units annually, one launch with20slots, five-year new operating life, zero delay/losses, manufacturing cost5 per unit, launch cost50 and other capex40. All are arbitrary test assumptions in consistent monetary units, not estimates or peer calibrations. Annual capex190, ten-year1900, and year-end fleet100 are hand-reproducible controls. A one-year commissioning delay leaves20units in pipeline and a20unit year1shortfall. Reducing slots to10 leaves10units in inventory and a10unit year1shortfall. Two launch losses plus three operating losses yield95year1operating. Buying two launches still loads20, leaves20unused slots and pays100in launch cost. None of these numbers may populate the actual case.

## Validation and limitations

Tests: balanced replacement/conservation; exact useful-life boundary and repeat cohort retirement; delayed commissioning; constrained launch capacity/inventory; launch plus operating losses; paid unused launch slots; opening inventory carryforward; input nonmutation; missing late-year cost; negative count; fractional count; Boolean count; absent source; loss exceeding loaded inventory; unconfirmed payment-basis exclusion; blocked actual-missing template; target growth deficit; legitimate explicit zero cost. All18passed under Python unittest. Standard library only; code, fixtures and instructions included in the package.

Evidence completeness requires source/date/status for annual fields and cohort/policy assumptions. This does not authenticate source quality. Actual template has nulls and missing evidence, fails validation, and contains no actual fleet estimate. Units must be homogeneous service equivalents only if engineering validates that conversion: physical counts alone do not demonstrate generation capacity, geography, spectrum, bandwidth, coverage, resiliency or service continuity. Here they remain physical scenario units without generation mixing.

Annual end-point timing cannot prove service throughout the year. A cohort with one remaining year serves during year1 and retires at its end; a zero commissioning delay means available by year end. New additions are not aged in their commissioning year. The model does not optimize orders, enforce factory capacity, model payload mass/orbit/inclination, pipeline failures, stock obsolescence, launch provider independence, licensability or intra-year outages. Costs assume cash in the purchase/manufacturing year; deposits, accruals, construction-in-progress, noncash movements and accounting classification require a separate reconciliation. Opening inventory is not repurchased. Other capex must exclude manufacturing and launches already counted; deorbit/ground/terminal/insurance costs require supported separate treatment. Terminal-value replacement economics beyond year10 remain open.
