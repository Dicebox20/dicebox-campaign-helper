## Executive proposal and route choice

Congress should create a funded commercial-network acquisition program, prioritize negotiated purchase of a separable business, and authorize a court-supervised compulsory fallback over specifically identified domestic property. The public corporation's charter, continuity obligations and rights protections must be enacted with the acquisition authority. The mission is public ownership; the path must remain lawful and compensated. Missing private facts are conditions to closing, not assertions silently supplied by this draft.

The draft supplies a NEW acquisition delegation and NEW jurisdiction for defined personal-property condemnation. Existing [28 U.S.C. §1358](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/119-118/t28/s1358/) speaks to real estate. Existing [40 U.S.C. §3114](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&num=0&req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title40-section3114) supplies a land-specific declaration/deposit mechanism. Neither is presented here as a network-wide taking power. The official [2025 Federal Rules of Civil Procedure](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/document/federal-rules-of-civil-procedure.pdf), Rule 71.1, supply procedure for real and personal property, not substantive acquisition authority.

The preferred transaction is a negotiated asset acquisition or purchase of voluntarily separated commercial subsidiaries. Equity purchase requires verified carve-out and liabilities; it does not justify taking the entire parent. The fallback below excludes compulsory acquisition of stock, foreign-situs property and regulatory permissions. Satellites in orbit and disputed intangible situs require separate jurisdictional analysis; this draft does not claim they are domestic property merely because an owner is American. If these limits prevent a viable carve-out, the Secretary must return to Congress with a revised lawful mechanism rather than seize excluded property.

## Proposed operative text

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the United States of America in Congress assembled,

### SEC. 1. SHORT TITLE.

This Act may be cited as “The Starlink and Stripes Act”.

### SEC. 2. PURPOSES AND RULES OF CONSTRUCTION.

(a) PURPOSES.—The purposes of this Act are to establish publicly owned commercial satellite communications infrastructure; maintain reliable civilian, emergency and commercial connectivity; provide lawful continuity for public customers; protect communications rights; and acquire property only through voluntary agreement or constitutionally valid proceedings with just compensation.

(b) NONPUNITIVE APPLICATION.—No determination, acquisition price, service decision or enforcement action under this Act may be based on an individual's political affiliation, protected expression, disability, or personal animus.

(c) EVIDENCE.—The Secretary shall publish a dated acquisition rationale identifying dependence, service concentration, resilience objectives, costs, alternatives, contrary evidence and uncertainty. No numerical fleet share, incident allegation, private financial estimate, or disputed attribution is incorporated as a congressional factual finding by this section.

### SEC. 3. DEFINITIONS.

In this Act:

(1) “Secretary” means the Secretary of Commerce.
(2) “Corporation” means the public operator established under title II of this Act.
(3) “Covered commercial business” means the commercial Starlink communications business to the extent identified in the schedule under section 101, excluding unrelated SpaceX businesses, dedicated Starshield and classified programs, and property of customers or third parties except specifically identified interests lawfully acquired from their owners.
(4) “Covered interest” means a specifically described property, contractual, intellectual-property or equity interest proposed for acquisition under section 102; it does not mean the electromagnetic spectrum, a foreign sovereign authorization, or the right to compel personal labor.
(5) “Closing” means the transfer of a covered interest under an executed agreement or final judicial transfer order, as applicable.

## TITLE I—LAWFUL COMMERCIAL ACQUISITION

### SEC. 101. TRANSACTION SCHEDULE AND SEPARATION PLAN.

(a) Before committing to acquisition, the Secretary shall prepare an owner-by-interest schedule identifying the proposed commercial assets, obligations, liabilities, encumbrances, necessary permissions, shared services and exclusions. The schedule shall distinguish assets from nontransferable permissions, acquired ownership from licensed use, and verified facts from unresolved claims.

(b) The Secretary shall obtain independent legal, financial and engineering assessments of the schedule, separability and continuity. Commercially sensitive and security information shall be protected under applicable law; a public summary shall identify material limitations and categories withheld.

(c) Inclusion on a schedule creates no title, lien release, regulatory consent, entitlement to confidential information, or presumption against an affected owner. This Act confers no investigative subpoena power.

### SEC. 102. NEGOTIATED ACQUISITION AUTHORITY.

(a) Subject to appropriations and sections 101, 104 and 110, the Secretary is authorized to acquire, for transfer to the Corporation, covered interests through negotiated purchase, lease, license, or acquisition of equity in a voluntarily separated commercial entity.

(b) The Secretary may negotiate transition-service, launch, manufacturing, intellectual-property, software-support and access agreements necessary for independent operation. Such agreements shall specify price, term, service obligations, remedies, renewal options and exit arrangements. This subsection neither transfers excluded property nor obligates a counterparty without agreement.

(c) No contract may require acquisition of unrelated businesses as a condition of closing unless Congress expressly authorizes the expanded perimeter by a subsequent law. No officer may waive the exclusions in section 3 by administrative designation.

### SEC. 103. APPRAISAL AND FAIR NEGOTIATION.

(a) The Secretary shall retain at least two independent appraisers with relevant enterprise and asset expertise, disclose material conflicts, and reconcile divergent conclusions in writing. Valuation shall distinguish enterprise value, equity value, liabilities, required separation costs, acquired rights and retained rights, avoiding duplicate payment for the same interest.

(b) Owners shall receive an intelligible description of the interest sought and a supported written offer, with a reasonable opportunity to obtain advice, present contrary evidence and negotiate. No acquisition discount may be imposed as punishment or by equating historical government spending with property already owned by the United States.

(c) No appraisal, negotiated payment or budget estimate binds a court's constitutional compensation determination.

### SEC. 104. CONDITIONS TO CLOSING.

The Secretary may close only after certifying, on a reviewable record, that—

(1) the specific title, interests, liabilities and creditor treatment are resolved to a legally sufficient standard;
(2) all permissions required for that closing and continued operation are effective, or lawful transitional permissions cover the relevant period;
(3) critical workforce, manufacturing, launch, network control, software, security, supplier and customer continuity arrangements are funded and executable;
(4) the Corporation is legally constituted, has responsible officers and has demonstrated operational readiness through independently witnessed tests;
(5) lawful budget authority covers the obligations proposed to be incurred, with a disclosed compensation and transition contingency plan; and
(6) material unresolved risks, dissenting assessments, alternatives and mitigation measures have been reported to Congress at least 60 days before closing.

Expiration of the 60-day period does not waive any other condition. The report is notice, not a committee veto or authorization for either House to change legal obligations outside enactment of law.

### SEC. 105. LIMITED JUDICIAL CONDEMNATION AUTHORITY.

(a) NEW DELEGATION.—The Secretary, acting through the Attorney General, may institute a civil action in the name of the United States to acquire a specifically identified domestic real-property interest or domestic tangible personal-property interest indispensable to the covered commercial business, only after a supported good-faith offer and not less than 180 days of negotiations unless the owner expressly declines further negotiation.

(b) NECESSITY RECORD.—Before instituting the action the Secretary shall publish findings explaining the public use, the precise interest, the negotiations, why purchase or a less burdensome interest is unavailable, and why the taking is necessary for the public communications purposes of this Act. Political retaliation, punishment and private favoritism are prohibited purposes.

(c) EXCLUSIONS.—This section does not authorize compulsory transfer of corporate shares, foreign-situs property, satellites situated outside the territorial United States, intellectual property, customer property, classified systems, regulatory permissions, or contracts requiring third-party performance. Acquisition of such interests must proceed by lawful agreement or further specific legislation. A court shall determine any disputed territorial situs; the Secretary's designation is not conclusive.

(d) NEW JURISDICTION AND PROCEDURE.—The district courts of the United States shall have original jurisdiction over actions under this section involving covered domestic real or tangible personal property, including objections to authority, public use, compliance with this Act, and compensation. Venue shall lie in a district where the property is situated. Rule 71.1 of the Federal Rules of Civil Procedure shall apply except as expressly provided by this Act. Affected owners and lienholders shall receive notice and a meaningful opportunity to contest authority, scope and compensation.

(e) NO PERSONAL-PROPERTY QUICK TAKE.—Title or possession to personal property shall transfer only after a judicial determination sustaining authority and public use, determination and payment or court deposit of compensation, and a court order specifying the interest and possession date. This Act does not extend the declaration-of-taking procedure in section 3114 of title 40 to personal property.

(f) LAND PROCEDURE.—For qualifying real-property interests, sections 3113 through 3118 of title 40 may be used only in accordance with their terms, available appropriations, this Act and the Constitution. No occupation before lawful possession is authorized by this Act.

### SEC. 106. JUST COMPENSATION AND CLAIMANT RIGHTS.

(a) No property may be taken under this Act without just compensation. The United States shall be liable for the full amount constitutionally required, including any required interest. A payment estimate, price ceiling or deposit does not extinguish that liability.

(b) Compensation shall be determined for legally recognized interests under applicable constitutional law, with evidence of fair value and any legally compensable associated loss. No categorical statutory exclusion of legally compensable goodwill, trade secrets or going-concern value is created. No entitlement to speculative, duplicative or legally noncompensable value is created.

(c) The court shall determine entitlement among owners and encumbrance holders, protect disputed proceeds and permit withdrawal of undisputed deposits without surrender of a claim to additional lawful compensation. No claimant shall recover twice for the same taking.

(d) The United States consents to suit in the action under section 105 for compensation, interest and relief necessary to enforce that section. Other remedies and jurisdiction otherwise available for a taking are preserved, subject to applicable jurisdictional requirements. Nothing in this Act treats the Tucker Act as the source of acquisition authority or promises simultaneous jurisdiction in multiple courts.

### SEC. 107. REGULATORY AND INTERNATIONAL SAVINGS.

(a) This Act does not itself transfer a radio authorization, assign frequencies, approve control changes, establish foreign landing rights, or alter a treaty obligation. Required permissions shall be obtained from the responsible authorities before affected operations transfer.

(b) Before closing, the Secretary, the Federal Communications Commission and the National Telecommunications and Information Administration shall publish a transaction-specific domestic authorization plan, including the applicability of sections 301, 305 and 310 of the Communications Act of 1934. No assumption that federal ownership automatically resolves the FCC–federal spectrum boundary is authorized.

(c) Foreign property and permissions shall be acquired or continued only through agreements and approvals effective under the relevant jurisdiction's law. Domestic enactment creates no presumption of foreign recognition.

### SEC. 108. CONTRACTS, INTELLECTUAL PROPERTY AND WORKERS.

(a) Contract succession, software and intellectual-property access shall occur only under legally effective assignments, licenses, novations or other lawful instruments. Payment for a physical asset does not by itself convey associated intellectual property or third-party rights.

(b) No individual shall be compelled to work for the United States, the Corporation or a contractor. Employment offers, benefits, bargaining obligations, pensions and any workforce transition shall be addressed in a disclosed plan consistent with applicable labor and employment law. Existing employee rights are not waived by this Act.

### SEC. 109. ACQUISITION ACCOUNTABILITY.

The Secretary shall deliver quarterly public progress reports and protected annexes as necessary, including the current schedule, offers and transaction status, expenditures, outstanding compensation exposure, continuity readiness, denied permissions, unresolved risks and alternatives. The Comptroller General shall have access to program records sufficient to audit implementation, subject to lawful protection of information. This section creates no power to obtain records from private persons beyond existing law or contract.

### SEC. 110. EXPIRATION OF NEW ACQUISITION AUTHORITY.

Authority to enter a new acquisition agreement or initiate a new condemnation action expires five years after enactment. Existing lawful obligations, pending proceedings, compensation rights, operating authority and safeguards survive. Extension or expansion requires a subsequent law.

## TITLE III—FUNDING AND COMPENSATION ACCOUNTS

### SEC. 301. AUTHORIZATION AND BUDGET SUBMISSION.

(a) There are authorized to be appropriated such sums as may be necessary for due diligence, independent review, negotiated acquisition, lawful condemnation, compensation, transition and operation under this Act. This subsection is an authorization and does not itself make an appropriation or permit obligations in advance of appropriations.

(b) Within 180 days after enactment, the Secretary shall submit a ten-year budget with acquisition and transition sources and uses, operating receipts, replacement satellites and launch needs, cybersecurity, debt assumptions, scenarios, sensitivity ranges and independent appraisal limitations. No parent-company valuation or personal net-worth estimate may substitute for a commercial-business valuation.

### SEC. 302. ACQUISITION AND TRANSITION FUND.

(a) There is established in the Treasury a Starlink Public Acquisition and Transition Fund, consisting of amounts specifically appropriated to the Fund by law. Amounts shall be available only for purposes and periods stated in the applicable appropriation. Establishment of the Fund does not itself provide money, borrowing authority or a federal guarantee.

(b) Acquisition agreements and condemnation initiation require legally available budget authority and a written assessment of probable total liabilities and contingencies. No arbitrary ceiling shall be construed to reduce compensation constitutionally owed for property already taken. If estimated exposure exceeds available authority before a taking, the Secretary shall defer the taking and seek further legislation or appropriations.

(c) Acquisition price, judicial awards, interest, transition services and operating support shall be separately accounted for. Obligations and outlays shall be separately reported. This section neither redirects existing broadband or defense funds nor assumes that historical federal payments offset compensation.

## TITLE V—REVIEW AND EFFECTIVE DATE

### SEC. 501. JUDICIAL REVIEW AND SAVINGS.

(a) Final agency action under this Act remains subject to applicable judicial review. In addition, the United States waives sovereign immunity for nonmonetary relief sought by a person adversely affected by an alleged violation of this Act, within the jurisdiction of the district courts, except where a specific statutory review route governs the challenged action. No new restriction on constitutional claims or compensation remedies is imposed.

(b) Nothing in this Act displaces a court's authority to prevent an unlawful acquisition, alters otherwise applicable FCC review procedures, waives a lawful privilege, or authorizes a taking without constitutionally adequate compensation.

### SEC. 502. SEVERABILITY WITH OPERATIONAL SAFETY.

If a provision or application is held invalid, the remainder shall continue to the extent legally and operationally practicable. No severability determination authorizes a transfer without compensation, required operating permissions, funded continuity, or the rights safeguards of this Act. The Secretary shall suspend any affected closing until the remaining lawful transaction satisfies section 104.

### SEC. 503. EFFECTIVE DATE.

This Act takes effect on enactment. No property or operating control transfers by enactment alone.
