## Findings from original source documents

1. **SpaceX supplied unusually precise site identifiers in an original filing.** In its **December 17, 2025** application filed to ISED, **SpaceX Canada Corp.** gave the gateway locations as **Saint-Laurent, QC: 45°29′15.24″ N, 73°44′33.25″ W** and **Janet, AB: 50°59′55.80″ N, 113°54′24.23″ W**. The applicant characterized both sites as already operational and serving Ka-band users. This establishes geographic **search leads and an applicant representation**, not independently authenticated parcel IDs, deeds, leases, present beneficial ownership, operator title, maintenance rights or assets included in a prospective transfer. The single-page signed application was inspected in both extracted text and original-page visual form (CA-GW-01).
2. **Regulatory approval does not substitute for land title.** ISED's January 22, 2026 notice repeats that the two gateways were operational and licensed for Ka-band. ISED's **April 21, 2026** E-band decision authorizes **temporary no-interference/no-protection** operation with incumbent coordination and future-policy conditions. Neither document identifies underlying property owners, registered easement holders or equipment vendor/financing counterparties; they answer a spectrum-permission question, not an ownership-transfer question (CA-GW-02/03). In particular, an applicant's reference to its satellite network is not legal proof that the applicant personally owns the land or all installed hardware.
3. **Alberta's authoritative land-title path is identified, not completed.** The Alberta government states that current title results identify **current owner, mortgages, caveats, easements, builders' liens and other registered interests**, and supports SPIN2 / ARLO or registry-agent retrieval keyed to a legal description, LINC, title number, and some other parcel search approaches (CA-GW-04). The provincial registration guidance explains that **utility rights of way/easements** and **caveats** may appear on land records and are distinguishable from a land ownership transfer (CA-GW-05). The ISED coordinate does **not** alone reveal the legally registered Alberta parcel. No certified or current title, plan, lease caveat, right-of-way instrument, title number or registered owner was obtained. Alberta land-record retrieval may involve paid documents and/or registry-agent access. No payment or account use occurred.
4. **Quebec's cadastral and land-rights chain is identified, not completed.** Quebec's official **Infolot cadastre** depicts cadastral parcels and their lot identifiers; the lot number is the common key linking to land register instruments (CA-GW-06). The government's **Index des immeubles** records transfers, hypothecs and servitudes, and requires the lot number and a client account for detailed consultation, with charges for some consultations (CA-GW-07). No Saint-Laurent lot number, Index des immeubles entry, deed, lease or easement was actually retrieved. Published geographic coordinates must first be tied to the authoritative cadastral lot boundary; nearby municipal property and the operator's actual site footprint must not be conflated.
5. **Siting-consultation requirements differ from proof a permit was granted.** ISED circular **CPC-2-0-03** describes contacting land-use authorities, reviewing shared infrastructure, public consultation and applicable exclusions; it explicitly notes that a building permit **does not itself establish federal land-use authority concurrence**. Therefore neither a spectrum decision nor generic antenna rules establishes site-level local approvals, or ownership of the structure (CA-GW-08). Montreal's Saint-Laurent antenna-permit guidance provides a relevant local documentary search path, not a retrieved, site-specific issued permit (CA-GW-09). No conclusion is made about whether a particular site qualified for a consultation exemption.
6. **Equipment and fixtures have a separate security/title trail.** Alberta's **Personal Property Registry** permits searches by **corporate debtor name**, with registered security interests potentially affecting machinery and other personal property. Its official search process involves a registry agent and match evaluation (CA-GW-10). Quebec's **RDPRM** is the Ministry of Justice register for personal and movable property rights and has public consultation routes (CA-GW-11). Neither registry here produced a search-result certificate for the SpaceX legal entities, nor were purchase orders, serial inventories, installation/maintenance contracts, fixture classifications or lender release instruments obtained. **Landowner ≠ gateway equipment owner ≠ operating licence holder** without underlying documents.

## Contradictions, interpretation and limitations

- **Better site identification, not ownership verification:** 2025 source-native coordinates materially improve future records retrieval compared with only municipal place names. Neither coordinates nor Ka-band operation support a deed or lease conclusion.
- **No property register return is not a negative registry match.** The actual named owner(s) cannot be established without cadastral matching and authenticated current title documents. No title- or PPR-search negative result was obtained.
- **Authority hierarchy:** Spectrum authorizations, land title/lease, physical equipment ownership, security interests, zoning consultation and contractual operational dependencies are independently provable questions. ISED CPC may provide exemption cases; local guidance does not establish issued permits or direct land title.
- **Geographic scope:** Two identified gateways, not an exhaustive Canadian Starlink gateway inventory. No assertion about site ownership by SpaceX Canada Corp., Space Exploration Technologies Corp., landlord, utility or unrelated colocation provider.
- **Other original research:** CA-G04a–f ISED licence/site permits and CA-G05a–f customer kit-contract gaps remain intact and distinct. An acquisition of an entire enterprise or classified Starshield equipment is not inferred.
