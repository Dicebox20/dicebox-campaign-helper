**Research date:** 15 October 2026 (UTC).  
**Claim:** `STARTAKE-20261015-WORK-ITEM3-UK-OFCOM-CTRL-71b6d4`.  
**Jurisdiction and perimeter:** United Kingdom Ofcom permissions and UK spectrum-trading rules for the publicly identified Starlink NGSO network and gateway permissions covering the UK proper. This review distinguishes a change in ownership of the same licensee from transfer of licence rights to another legal person. It does not determine a proposed transaction's facts or obtain a regulator's transaction-specific view.

## Question and answer

Does an acquisition of a Starlink licensee or its parent require transfer of its UK Ofcom licence, and what changes if the licence is moved to another company?

**Short answer.** Ofcom's current Spectrum Trading Guidance Notes expressly say that where a corporate licensee is taken over, for example by share purchase, it does not need to apply to transfer the licence if the licensee's company identity/registration number stays the same. The same guidance says that moving spectrum rights from one registered company to another requires a spectrum trade, including an intra-group move. Applied to a parent acquisition, the implication is that a transaction leaving the named licensee as the same legal person does not itself trigger a licence-transfer application under this guidance. That last application to an upstream-parent acquisition is an inference; the guide's example addresses a corporate licensee takeover, and the transaction documents and exact chain of control were not reviewed. [Ofcom, *Trading Guidance Notes*, OfW513, paragraphs 3.1–3.2](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/siteassets/resources/documents/manage-your-licence/spectrum-trading/trading-guidance-notes.pdf).

A transfer to a different registered company is a different event. Ofcom lists the relevant satellite-earth-station-network and NGSO earth-station classes as eligible for outright or concurrent transfer, total only (not partial), subject to the applicable regulations, licence terms and procedure. The transfer route generally involves the prescribed application and publication on Ofcom's Transfer Notification Register; it is not accomplished merely by private corporate documents. Whether a particular permission and transaction qualify must be checked against its exact licence and the current rules. [Ofcom, OfW513, eligible classes and procedure](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/siteassets/resources/documents/manage-your-licence/spectrum-trading/trading-guidance-notes.pdf); [Wireless Telegraphy (Spectrum Trading) Regulations 2012, SI 2012/2187](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2012/2187/pdfs/uksi_20122187_en.pdf).

This conclusion is limited to the question of transfer of spectrum rights. The sources reviewed do not establish that no other notice, reporting, licence-condition or competition obligation could arise from a particular transaction, nor do they establish the actual control chain or authorization status of any proposed purchaser.

## Findings

### 1. Same licensee after a share/control transaction

Ofcom's January 2026 Trading Guidance Notes say a change of company name that leaves the company registration number unchanged is not a trade. They separately say that if a corporate licensee is taken over, for example by share purchase, it does not have to apply for transfer because the licensee's identity/registration number does not change. The contrast in the same passage is explicit: transferring rights to another registered company, even within a group, does require a spectrum trade.

The best-supported conclusion is therefore narrow: a direct share purchase of the current licensee, or an upstream acquisition that leaves the named licensee as the same legal company, is not itself a transfer of the Ofcom spectrum rights under Ofcom's stated trading guidance. This conclusion does not determine any other transaction-specific notification or consent requirement, and does not assume that the target or parent is the current holder. The actual licensee and corporate registration identifiers must be confirmed at closing.

The Ofcom NGSO applicant guidance reviewed is dated 24 March 2026. Searches of that guidance for “change of control” and “change in control” returned no match. This is limited negative evidence about that guidance only; it is not a regulator ruling, and does not displace a condition in an individual licence or another applicable rule.

### 2. Transfer to a different legal person

Wireless Telegraphy Act 2006 section 30 provides the statutory basis for spectrum-trading regulations. Section 30(4) makes a transfer of licence rights or obligations void except as permitted under those regulations or the relevant statutory route. The 2012 Regulations identify eligible licence classes and bands. Ofcom's January 2026 table lists Satellite (Earth Station Network), Satellite (Earth Station) (Non-Fixed Satellite Service), and Satellite (Earth Station) (Non-Geostationary Satellite Service) among eligible classes for outright or concurrent transfers, but total transfers only.

Ofcom describes the ordinary process as an application signed by the relevant parties, a proposed-transfer notice on the Transfer Notification Register, and Ofcom checks under the applicable rules. Once effective, the existing licence is surrendered and a new licence issued to the transferee. Ofcom says most classes do not require its separate affirmative consent, while specified classes do; that general statement does not waive the application and procedure or any condition in the actual Starlink licence. The exact class and instrument need confirmation before applying the table to a specific transfer.

The standard OfW597 licence conditions booklet says that a licence may not be transferred and requires prompt written notice of a licensee name or address change. Its footnote recognizes that certain rights and obligations may be transferred under Ofcom regulations made pursuant to Wireless Telegraphy Act section 30. Read together, the booklet's baseline condition and the statutory trading route do not create a free-standing private assignment right; the permitted regulatory trade route remains subject to eligibility, procedure and instrument-specific terms.

### 3. Temporary E-band gateway licences

Ofcom's temporary E-band page lists Starlink temporary gateway licences 1398959 (Morn Hill), 1398998 (Wherstead), and 1399007 (Woodwalton), each linked to network licence 1239247 and with a stated end date of 31 December 2028. The issued Morn Hill licence 1398959/1, dated 31 October 2025, names Irish Starlink Internet Services Limited. Its special condition 3.1 says the licence may not be transferred; it also includes OfW597 conditions.

This is a materially stricter instrument-specific term than the ordinary tradeable-class table appears to suggest. The special temporary E-band class was not located as a separately named class in the published Ofcom trading table or the 2012 Regulations Schedule 2 reviewed. The issued licence's own non-transfer term should not be treated as erased by general trading guidance. Conversely, the documents reviewed do not conclusively resolve whether a statutory trade mechanism could apply notwithstanding that condition, or how Ofcom would classify a transfer application for this temporary instrument. Treat any move of an E-band licence to a different legal person as **unresolved and requiring confirmation from Ofcom and UK regulatory counsel before closing**.

The public Ofcom E-band page says the listed licences are subject to OfW597, while the 2025 consultation materials discussed a draft with different wording about incorporating OfW597. For present purposes the issued licence and the current Ofcom page control the description of operative published terms; the draft is not substituted for the issued instrument. The discrepancy reinforces the need to inspect the final licence rather than rely on a consultation draft.

## Adverse readings and limits

- Ofcom's share-purchase example is a guidance statement about transfer of a licence, not an opinion on every direct or indirect change of control. It does not establish that separate notification, reporting, change-of-name/address, competition, national-security, contractual or other duties are absent.
- The registration-number test concerns the identity of the corporate licensee. A parent-company acquisition should have the same result only if the actual licensee remains the same legal person and no separate condition or statutory rule is triggered; that application is an inference from the guidance, not a transaction-specific decision.
- OfW597's no-transfer condition and statutory trading footnote must be read with the current Ofcom table and regulations. The standard classes have a published trade route; the temporary E-band licences have a separate express no-transfer condition and the public sources reviewed do not settle the interaction.
- The review did not obtain native case files, non-public Ofcom correspondence, a current certified corporate record, transaction documents, or formal Ofcom/counsel advice. It did not determine whether an acquisition is reportable under UK competition or other regimes.
- This assignment did not reconcile every possible current grant to an individual instrument, so the register review cannot establish that every current UK permission or condition has been identified.

## Bill v1.4 implications

No amendment is supported by this pass. Bill v1.4 §104(2) already conditions closing and continued operation on effective permissions or lawful transitional permissions. Section 403 already calls for a jurisdiction-by-jurisdiction register of the legal operator, authorization, expiry, ownership/control consent, conditions and pending matters, and bars treating a country as ready solely because a general procedure exists. Those provisions capture the distinction found here: verify that the licensee remains the same legal person for a share/control transaction, and obtain an eligible transfer when the rights move to a new grantee. They also leave the E-band transfer point pending rather than presuming a route.

No Bill text was changed. No foreign permission, transaction-specific approval, practical Item 3 completion or named-topic completion is established.
