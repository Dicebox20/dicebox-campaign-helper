## Finding

The reviewed federal framework has **targeted restoration and coordination mechanisms**, but it does not establish a universal disaster-time availability or restoration guarantee for every commercial satellite-broadband subscriber.

The clearest direct provider duties are conditional:

- The Telecommunications Service Priority system can require priority provisioning and restoration for an assigned, eligible National Security and Emergency Preparedness service. FCC rules define telecommunications transmission to include satellite, but service eligibility, provider status, user authorization, and an actual priority assignment matter. This is not a general priority or uptime entitlement for every retail broadband user.
- The Mandatory Disaster Response Initiative imposes coordination, roaming, mutual-aid, preparedness, testing, and reporting duties on **facilities-based mobile wireless providers** when the rule is activated. It is not a general rule for commercial satellite broadband in its satellite-broadband capacity.
- Stafford Act §5189e expressly includes Internet access and direct broadcast satellite services in its essential-service-provider definition, but its operative non-impediment duty is imposed on Federal agency heads, for qualifying providers contributing to response. It helps providers reach sites and restore service; it does not require the provider to restore service or meet an uptime target.
- FEMA’s ESF #2 framework coordinates public and industry support and identifies the private sector as a partner or lead for rapid restoration of its networks. That operational framework does not itself set a general enforceable satellite-broadband service level.
- FCC disaster information reporting supports situational awareness. The FCC’s 2026 order retained satellite DIRS fields on a voluntary basis and declined to make satellite reporting mandatory; reporting is not a restoration guarantee.

Accordingly, the scoped conclusion is **no general federal satellite-broadband restoration or availability mandate was identified in the reviewed authorities**. This is not a conclusion that no duty could apply to a specific eligible TSP service, mobile-wireless operation, Federal contract, or other provider-specific arrangement. No such Starlink-specific facts were reviewed or established.

### Decision

**No Bill text change is recommended from this evidence pass.** Sections 104(3), 401(d), and 402(d)–(e) can capture and test a contracted emergency-response service when its interruption meets the draft’s risk threshold; §205(d) supplies an access baseline. That is a meaningful transition path, but it is not an ongoing disaster-response service regime.

If the Act is intended to guarantee a continuing operational role during disasters after transition, that is a separate policy choice. It would need defined eligible missions/services, activation conditions, priority relationship to existing TSP/MDRI systems, measurable restoration or availability criteria, funding, interagency and State/Tribal coordination, exceptions, evidence/reporting, and oversight. This review does not supply operational, cost, coverage, or engineering facts sufficient to specify those requirements.

## Contrary considerations and limits

- TSP can impose real priority-restoration duties for an eligible, authorized, assigned NSEP service. The report therefore does not assert that satellite transmission is categorically outside Federal priority rules.
- MDRI creates concrete coordination and restoration-related duties for facilities-based mobile wireless providers. It is evidence that Congress/FCC can use targeted provider-class rules, while its feasibility and own-network safeguards limit what those duties guarantee.
- Section 5189e expressly covers Internet access service and direct broadcast satellite service in a qualifying emergency-response context. Its operative duty nevertheless binds Federal agency heads, not the service provider.
- FEMA’s ESF #2 plan expects private operators to lead or partner in restoring networks they own or operate. Practical responsibility under a response plan is not the same as a generally enforceable service-level obligation.
- A particular Federal acquisition, service contract, TSP assignment, or other binding agreement could create additional provider-specific duties. No such contract or assignment was supplied or examined.
- This audit does not decide whether a particular Starlink product or entity is eligible for TSP or falls within another regulated provider class. It makes no claim about actual Starlink disaster use, performance, coverage, outages, or restoration.
