**Research date:** 15 October 2026 UTC. **Claim:** STARTAKE-20261015-WORK-SEC303-APPROP-1b7d4c, reserved and read back in canonical tracker v179. **Method:** direct review of official preliminary U.S. Code text and a GAO decision, integrated with the already-saved Item 2 Federal Appropriations, Obligations and Payment Audit. **Scope:** Revise proposed §303 of Bill v0.5 only. Make its appropriation template explicit about purpose, amount, period of availability, documentary obligation records, and Judgment Fund limits, while retaining all financial placeholders. No new Starlink valuation, federal score, acquisition-price estimate, payment record, or source-and-uses model was produced.

## Finding

The earlier Item 2 audit remains the controlling evidence report for the legal framework. It correctly concludes that general acquisition authority, a compensation remedy, an agency program, and an appropriation are separate questions. Its claim was verified and released in tracker v81, and the report appears in the current Item 2 evidence row. No duplicate audit was needed.

The rechecked statutes support a narrow §303 drafting change. The current Bill v0.5 already says the Fund receives only amounts specifically appropriated by law and that §301's authorization does not itself provide funds. But the two proposed §303 appropriation lines were still placeholders, the first did not name a fiscal year, and neither line expressly said “there is appropriated.” Under 31 U.S.C. §1301(d), an appropriation or authority to contract beyond an appropriation must be specifically stated by law. Section 1341 generally prohibits obligations above available amounts or before an appropriation, except where law authorizes. Therefore, the revised template uses express appropriation language, distinguishes a fixed-period due-diligence line from an acquisition/transition line expressly available until expended, and preserves blank amounts and dates.

The revision does not supply budget authority unless Congress enacts text with completed figures and availability terms. It does not identify an available existing account, determine any federal score, or make a negotiated purchase price payable from the Judgment Fund. It preserves §1304's separate treatment of qualifying judgments, awards, and settlements.

## Proposed §303 text in Bill v0.6

The text below is the working draft. Bracketed amounts and the due-diligence fiscal year/date remain unresolved.

> ### SEC. 303. APPROPRIATIONS AND OBLIGATION CONTROLS.
>
> (a) DUE-DILIGENCE AND TRANSITION-PLANNING APPROPRIATION.—For fiscal year [DUE-DILIGENCE FISCAL YEAR], there is appropriated to the Fund, out of any money in the Treasury not otherwise appropriated, $[DUE-DILIGENCE AMOUNT], to remain available until [DATE], solely for preacquisition investigation, independent legal, appraisal, and engineering review, and transition planning under this Act. No amount under this subsection may be obligated for purchase consideration, a condemnation deposit, or operational control before the applicable closing conditions under section 104 are met.
>
> (b) ACQUISITION, COMPENSATION, TRANSITION, AND CONTINUITY APPROPRIATION.—For acquisition and transition under this Act, there is appropriated to the Fund, out of any money in the Treasury not otherwise appropriated, $[ACQUISITION AND TRANSITION AMOUNT], to remain available until expended solely for the purposes stated in this Act and the applicable appropriation, of which not less than $[CONTINUITY RESERVE] shall be separately reserved for funded continuity arrangements. No acquisition obligation or taking may be initiated until the Secretary certifies compliance with sections 104 and 302 and that legally available budget authority is available for the purpose, amount, and period of the obligation. Nothing in this subsection reduces final compensation or interest owed for property already taken.
>
> (c) PURPOSE, AMOUNT, AND PERIOD CONTROLS.—
> (1) Each appropriation under this section shall state its amount, purpose, and period of availability. No amount may be obligated or expended in excess of amounts legally available for that purpose and period, or before an appropriation is made, except as otherwise authorized by law.
> (2) An appropriation limited to a definite period is available only as provided under section 1502(a) of title 31, United States Code. This Act does not make an amount in a closed appropriation account available for a new obligation. Recording, adjusting, and liquidating an obligation after expiration or account closing remain governed by sections 1552 and 1553 of title 31, United States Code, and other applicable law.
>
> (d) OBLIGATION RECORDING.—The Secretary shall record and report an obligation under this Act only when supported by documentary evidence and otherwise consistent with section 1501 of title 31, United States Code, and other applicable law. This subsection does not determine the legal event that creates an obligation for a particular transaction.
>
> (e) JUDGMENT FUND.—This section does not establish that a negotiated acquisition payment or advance purchase payment is payable from the Judgment Fund solely because this Act authorizes an acquisition. Any payment sought from the Judgment Fund for a judgment, award, or compromise settlement remains subject to section 1304 of title 31, United States Code, and other applicable law. Nothing in this subsection limits compensation or interest owed for property already taken.

## Contrary readings and drafting limits

- An enacted bill can itself make a direct appropriation if it clearly states that an appropriation is made. The proposed lines use that form, but placeholders mean the current draft still supplies no amount or due-diligence availability date.
- “To remain available until expended” expressly drafts the acquisition line as no-year availability. Congress may retain that choice or replace it with a fixed period; either way, the purpose and amount still need to be settled.
- Section 1501 does not require that every valid obligation arise from a real-estate purchase agreement. It recognizes multiple documentary categories. The proposed text therefore refers to §1501 and applicable law rather than inventing a single obligating event.
- Section 1304 can fund qualifying judgments, awards, and compromise settlements under its conditions. The proposed text does not categorically exclude it; it prevents the Act from assuming that an advance or negotiated purchase price qualifies solely because acquisition is authorized.
- Section 1553 permits certain post-expiration or post-closing adjustments and liquidations. The proposed text preserves those rules and bars only the inference that this Act makes a closed balance available for a new acquisition obligation.
