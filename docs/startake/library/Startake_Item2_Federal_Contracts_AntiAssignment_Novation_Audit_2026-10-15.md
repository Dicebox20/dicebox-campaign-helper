## Executive finding

A Starlink asset sale, change of corporate ownership and transfer of an existing U.S. federal service contract are legally distinct events. **41 U.S.C. §6305(a)** generally prohibits transfer of federal contracts. **FAR 42.1204(a)** allows the government, when in its interest, to recognize a new contracting party following transfer of all of the contractor's assets or the entire portion used to perform the affected contract. Recognition depends on contracting-officer evaluation and executed novation; it is not an automatic consequence of a hypothetical acquisition statute. **FAR 42.1204(b)** generally does not require novation for a stock purchase leaving the legal contracting party, assets and performer unchanged, although other ownership-related matters may still need formal agreement. **31 U.S.C. §3727** governs assignments of claims/payment and must not be confused with assignment of performance obligations. ## Contradictions and limits

- **Incorrect:** Buying all Starlink satellites automatically transfers government broadband contracts. **Correction:** Original contracting parties and novation/consent depend on actual agreements and federal transfer rules.
- **Incorrect:** All stock acquisitions require novation. **Correction:** FAR 42.1204(b) provides a specific exception when the legal contractor remains the same and keeps performance control.
- **Incorrect:** Novation proves rights to spectrum, intellectual property, physical gateway title, foreign approvals or classified assets. **Correction:** Government contract succession is separate from each of those property and regulatory rights.
- **Incorrect:** A receivables assignment gives the assignee the right to perform the government's contract. **Correction:** 31 U.S.C. §3727 and 41 U.S.C. §6305(b) concern claims/payment assignments, not a general contract novation.
