## Executive result

A federal agency with independently established authority to condemn a particular domestic **land interest** can use federal condemnation proceedings under 40 U.S.C. §3113 and, if statutory requirements are satisfied, file a Declaration of Taking under §3114. Filing an adequate declaration **and depositing the estimated compensation with the court** immediately vests title to the described land estate, easement or right-of-way in the United States, while the owner retains the enforceable right to **final just compensation**, possible deficiency and applicable interest. Section 3115 restricts that commitment when Congress places price limits. Rule 71.1 governs the relevant federal litigation pleading, party-notice and compensation procedures; its broader scope over real *or personal* property **does not expand §3114's land-specific immediate-vesting mechanism**.

**This is not a Starlink acquisition authority.** Section 3114 specifically presupposes authority empowered by law to acquire land; it cannot automatically vest satellites in orbit, radio licences, orbital coordination filings, Starlink software/source code, contracts, corporate shares, or the entire commercial network. Nor does it prove any particular Starlink gateway is owned by SpaceX rather than a landlord, carrier, third-party gateway operator or customer. The case law makes timing fact-specific when the government physically occupies property before formally acquiring title.

## Source-supported acquisition sequence, strictly conditional

1. **Authorization and parcel selection:** Congress or existing agency law must empower acquisition of identified U.S. real estate or land interest for a sufficiently established public use. Identify the exact public agency and legal estate; do **not** equate a facility-use licence, leased roof or colocation agreement with fee-simple title.
2. **Judicial proceeding:** §3113 and Rule 71.1 require appropriate court proceedings, complaint/parties/notice and an identified interest. Identify owner, tenants, lienholders, surface/easement interests and encumbrances. Affected parties may contest authority/public use and compensation through the applicable process.
3. **Optional immediate vesting:** In a qualifying case the acquiring official may file §3114 declaration with the five specified elements and **deposit estimated compensation**. Legal title to the *specified real-property interest* can then vest before trial/ultimate valuation. **There is no verified Starlink-specific declaration or deposit.**
4. **Budget and compensation:** §3115 adds constraints concerning Congress's price limits and irreversible compensation commitment; §3114(c), §3116 address court valuation, deficiency and interest. Deposit is not final just compensation. Coordinate appropriations/Antideficiency Act with the earlier Item 2 budget report.
5. **Possession and continuity:** Courts may set surrender terms, and *Dow* warns physical entry may create an earlier taking date. Physical ownership of a gateway does not by itself confer authority to operate Starlink's space segment or FCC-licensed earth-station radio systems. Service transition, security, colocation and access agreements require separate instruments.

## Contrary cases and unresolved boundaries

- **Broad procedural rule, narrow immediate-vesting statute:** Rule 71.1 expressly allows real *and personal* property condemnation proceedings, but §3114's land/easement/right-of-way quick-take mechanism cannot be stretched to an orbiting fleet, customer equipment, proprietary software, radio licences or an equity stake. A separate asset-specific statute could create other procedures; none has been established here.
- **Existing authority versus new authority:** §3113 conditions proceeding on an **already authorized officer**; §3114's requirement to state the authorizing law likewise forecloses inferring a general Starlink nationalization power solely from 40 U.S.C. Title 40. Opponents could also challenge public use, delegation, standing, procedures and adequacy of compensation in a real case.
- **Ownership versus control:** Taking a landlord-owned gateway parcel might leave equipment and network rights with third parties; taking a carrier’s easement may not give the government a right to independently operate antenna/radio equipment. Conversely acquiring corporate stock need not entail condemnations of U.S. parcels. Original title/lease registers belong to independently owned Item 1 work; **no Item 1 claim is made here**.
- **Time-of-taking and multiple owners:** *Dow* is adverse to treating the date of deposit as necessarily the first taking when physical occupation came earlier. Identify the rightful compensation claimant based on actual chronology; do not assert a universal vesting date without facts.
- **Foreign locations:** A federal district-court mechanism for U.S. land cannot automatically transfer title to gateway sites in Canada, UK, Ireland or other jurisdictions. Separate sovereign property and regulatory rules apply.
