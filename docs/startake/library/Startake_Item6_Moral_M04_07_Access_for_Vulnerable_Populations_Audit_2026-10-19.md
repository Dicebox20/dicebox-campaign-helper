## Assessment

Existing federal law provides conditional accessibility duties for specified telecommunications services/equipment and advanced communications services. Separate universal-service programs can subsidize some eligible voice or broadband offers, but participation depends on carrier, service, territory, and household eligibility. The Universal Service Act’s affordability and rural-access principles guide federal/state policy; they do not by themselves guarantee every household a broadband line at a set price.

Bill v1.14 would add a substantial planning and transition framework: accessible and nondiscriminatory terms, a measurable affordability/rural-access plan with a required access baseline, and tested continuity controls before operating transfers. The draft still does not set a funded minimum service, affordability ceiling, eligibility rule, universal-coverage commitment, or a particular benefit for children, older adults, people with disabilities, isolated residents, or conflict-zone users. Nor does this review establish that a future Corporation or Starlink service would meet any existing rule. The proposed plan is a required planning instrument; it is not itself a funded entitlement.

## Existing law by population and service

| Population or access issue | Current federal framework reviewed | Limits and application conditions |
|---|---|---|
| **People with disabilities — telecommunications** | 47 U.S.C. §255 requires covered telecommunications-service providers and customer-premises-equipment manufacturers to make covered services/equipment accessible when readily achievable, and otherwise to provide compatibility where required. | Applies only to covered entities, equipment, and telecommunications services; it is not a general accessibility rule for every internet-access feature. Section 255(f) places §255 complaints within FCC jurisdiction and creates no additional private right of action. The FCC’s classification of a fixed/mobile satellite offering can matter. |
| **People with disabilities — advanced communications** | 47 U.S.C. §617 and FCC Part 14 require accessibility and usability of covered advanced communications services (ACS) and equipment unless requirements are not achievable; rules address compatibility and network barriers. Covered ACS categories include interconnected and non-interconnected VoIP, electronic messaging, and interoperable video conferencing. | “Satellite internet” is not itself one of the statutory ACS categories. A particular service or feature may qualify depending on what it offers and how it is classified. Section 617 contains a small-entity exemption, waiver authority, and a customized-service exclusion; §617(j) does not require every feature of every device/service to be accessible for every disability. Application to a future operator and its specific products requires facts. |
| **People who are deaf, hard of hearing, deaf-blind, or have speech disabilities** | 47 U.S.C. §225 requires common carriers providing telephone voice-transmission services to provide telecommunications relay service throughout the area in which they offer service, directly or through an arrangement. Section 618 supplies FCC complaint/enforcement and recordkeeping procedures for covered §255/§617/§619 duties. | Relay-service duties depend on carrier and voice-service classification. Neither §225 nor §618 creates a blanket entitlement to any particular satellite broadband plan. Accessibility-specific FCC process is distinct from the general service-harm procedures reviewed in M13.06. |
| **Children, older adults, and low-income households** | Lifeline is a non-transferable retail offer for qualifying low-income consumers; it can support qualifying voice or broadband service. Under 47 C.F.R. §54.409, the main routes are household income at or below 135% of the Federal Poverty Guidelines or qualifying participation by the consumer, a dependent, or the household in specified programs. Tribal eligibility has additional routes. One Lifeline benefit is allowed per household. A separate narrow survivor route exists under §54.409(a)(3). | Age alone is not a Lifeline criterion. Children may be reached through household/dependent eligibility, not a standalone age-based broadband right. Older adults may qualify through household income or listed programs. The rules do not establish that any specific service or provider participates. |
| **Rural, remote, and isolated residents; schools and health facilities** | 47 U.S.C. §254 states federal universal-service principles for affordable services and reasonably comparable access in rural, insular, high-cost, and low-income areas, and addresses schools and health-care providers. A carrier designated as an eligible telecommunications carrier (ETC) may receive support and must offer supported services in its designated area; §214(e)(3) permits a designation/order for an unserved community that requests service where no carrier will provide supported service. | Section 254(b) principles do not themselves fix a particular household’s price or assure that a given provider, broadband product, or location receives support. ETC duties and Lifeline offers are conditional on legal classification, designation, service-area scope, supported service, and program compliance. No Starlink or future-Corporation ETC designation or Lifeline participation was established in this pass. |
| **People in conflict zones** | The draft’s closing and transition provisions require the relevant permissions and continued-operation arrangements to be identified and secured. | No conflict zone, foreign jurisdiction, user group, or service authorization was specified for this audit. This pass establishes no general federal person-level right to service in every conflict zone and makes no country-specific finding on host-state law, sanctions, permissions, humanitarian arrangements, or technical feasibility. |

**Lifeline administration.** Current 47 C.F.R. §§54.401, 54.405, 54.409, and 54.410 define Lifeline as a discounted, non-transferable retail offer for qualifying consumers and require ETCs to make it available to qualifying consumers, publicize it, determine and certify eligibility, and follow enrollment/recertification rules. The eCFR Title 47 page stated it was current through 15 October 2026 when retrieved. USAC’s consumer page explains income/program qualification and the one-benefit-per-household rule. These conditions make Lifeline a potential access route, not a universal public-service requirement for every operator.

**Section 504 question.** 29 U.S.C. §794(a) covers qualifying programs receiving federal financial assistance and programs conducted by an Executive agency or the Postal Service. Bill §201(c) expressly treats the proposed Corporation as an agency for the APA, FOIA, Privacy Act, federal-records, and information-security requirements; it does not expressly name §504. This text-level comparison leaves the future Corporation’s §504 status and regulations unresolved. Whether the Corporation would be an Executive agency or covered program for this purpose, and whether a separate funding arrangement triggers recipient coverage, should be resolved by legislative counsel. This report does not infer an exemption or coverage.

## Findings, contrary considerations, and open evidence

1. **Accessibility is a real but conditional legal baseline.** Sections 255 and 617, and Part 14, can require covered communications services, equipment, support, and documentation to be accessible. The central uncertainty is application to the exact provider, service class, device, feature, and future Corporation. A general satellite-internet subscription should not be assumed to be a covered telecommunications service or ACS without an FCC/legal classification analysis.
2. **Low-income support is targeted and administratively bounded.** Lifeline can lower the price of a qualifying service for an eligible household when offered by an ETC under program rules. Its income/program criteria can reach some children and older adults but do not create a universal or age-based entitlement. Provider designation, service-area coverage, product approval, benefit level, enrollment/takeup, and any required funding remain unverified here.
3. **The Bill improves the planning record, not the funded outcome.** Section 205(d) requires useful disaggregation by population, geography, accessibility, service quality, total cost, affordability, eligibility, takeup, and data gaps. That gives future decision-makers a stronger basis to test access than nominal footprint alone. The plan still lacks an enacted price ceiling, minimum service specification, appropriation, explicit ETC commitment, or automatic benefit; its effectiveness depends on evidence, funding, later Board action, and oversight.
4. **Continuity safeguards address identified critical services, not all users.** Sections 401–402 can protect access where a service is tied to documented customer or mission obligations and is classified critical. They establish a transfer gate and testing regime, not a general queue priority or universal-access promise. Requirements for schools, health facilities, emergency services, and other users need actual obligation and service data.
5. **Conflict-zone access remains a distinct jurisdictional question.** The Bill’s foreign-permission gate and register acknowledge the issue, but this pass did not assess any country, sanction, armed conflict, permission, humanitarian exception, or actual network footprint. A later claim must specify geography, period, lawful operator, and relevant service authorization.

No actual Starlink or Corporation service was tested; no current plan, coverage map, installation price, equipment design, performance dataset, incident, discrimination claim, customer agreement, ETC status, or Lifeline enrollment was authenticated. No user-specific facts, private materials, or professional opinion were obtained. These are material limits, not findings that access is unavailable or unlawful.

## Review questions for a later verified drafting pass

- Specify the accessibility standard, testing method, and operational responsibility intended by “reasonable disability accessibility,” including whether the Act should expressly state §504 coverage and preserve all applicable §§255/617/225 duties.
- Decide whether the Corporation must seek ETC designation or participate in Lifeline where lawful and feasible; define any additional publicly funded low-income benefit only with an identified appropriation and eligibility process.
- Set measurable definitions for effective availability, acceptable quality, total installed/recurring cost, and household affordability; identify target groups and geography; disclose takeup and data gaps; reconcile objectives to lawful funding.
- Decide whether children, older adults, isolated residents, schools, health facilities, and conflict-zone users require separate service commitments, and document what current contracts, public-service obligations, and alternative paths support those commitments.
- For any conflict-zone proposal, obtain a country- and date-specific permissions, sanctions, host-state law, and operational review before making an access claim.

These are questions for a future source-backed drafting and professional-review pass, not proposed operative language or a conclusion that the current Bill is unlawful.
