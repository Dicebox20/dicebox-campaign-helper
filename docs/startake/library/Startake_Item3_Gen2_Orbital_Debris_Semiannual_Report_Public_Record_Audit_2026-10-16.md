**Date:** 16 October 2026 (UTC)

## Question and limits

This review asked whether the FCC’s official public record exposed the Starlink Gen2 orbital-debris semiannual reports and their filing metadata by 16 October 2026. It was limited to direct public FCC sources and a bounded search of the official filing systems. It does not determine whether SpaceX complied with a reporting condition, whether a report exists in a non-public or unindexed record, or whether any report is accurate.

## Primary-source baseline

FCC 22-91 set a semiannual reporting condition for Starlink, with reports due January 1 and July 1 for the preceding June–November and December–May periods, respectively. Its specified information includes conjunction events and actions, difficulties and corrective measures, satellites screened from deployment, reentries, disposal failures, and related information. [S1]

FCC DA 26-36 (January 9, 2026) identifies the Gen2 upgrade application files as SAT-LOA-20200526-00055, SAT-AMD-20210818-00105, SAT-AMD-20221216-00175, SAT-AMD-20241017-00228, and SAT-MOD-20241011-00224. It partially grants the Gen2 Upgrade Applications and states the authorization conditions discussed in the prior orbital-debris report. This confirms the relevant authorization record; it does not by itself establish whether a later report was filed or made publicly accessible. [S2]

The existing orbital-debris rules audit (Library ID `libfile_7203cf9127288191a3b01d5c174d1766`) expressly excluded inspection of underlying FCC filings and reconciliation of semiannual reports. It records a next scheduled reporting date of January 1, 2027, for June–November 2026. That report therefore supplies the rule and authorization context, while leaving actual filing-level evidence open.

## Public-record retrieval result

The search covered FCC-hosted order and filing pages using the DA 26-36 identifier, Gen2 Starlink, semiannual-report wording, and the report period/date terms. The FCC text search returned the governing orders but did not expose an authenticated filing entry or attachment for the relevant report period. A link to an FCC ICFS filing-summary page was surfaced as a retrieval lead, but the official page did not return readable content through the available page-fetch path. The underlying ICFS filing entry, its receipt metadata, and any report attachment could not be inspected.

Search results from non-FCC sites were treated only as leads and not as evidence. No filing date, accession number, attachment, report contents, or deadline compliance is certified here. In particular, an inaccessible or unindexed entry is not evidence that SpaceX failed to file. Whether any report was filed and whether it is publicly available remain unresolved.

## Bill crosswalk

The completed rules report maps the authorization and reporting duties to Bill v1.4 §107. That section already prevents the Act from itself transferring an FCC authorization and requires a pre-close authorization plan. The plan should inventory the applicable debris plan, authorization-specific reporting duties and dates, outstanding filings, record custodian, responsible successor, and any condition that could pause deployment. This follow-up found no new basis for a bill redline because the underlying filing record was not authenticated.

The previous crosswalk also found that §208 does not provide a source-backed estimate or a named deorbit-cost amount. This report adds no cost input and does not change the existing Item 5 evidence schedule.
