# Item 3 — Canada ISED Starlink authorization and change-of-control audit

**Research date:** 15 October 2026 UTC  
**Claim:** STARTAKE-20261015-WORK-ITEM3-CANADA-ISED-8c4b62  
**Status:** Bounded primary-source pass complete; practical Item 3 and the named topic remain formally unchecked.  
**Scope:** Canadian ISED foreign-satellite approvals and related space/earth-station spectrum authorization rules relevant to continued commercial Starlink service after the proposed U.S. public acquisition. This is a focused Canadian permission audit, not a global licence census, ownership opinion, or agency/counsel determination.

## Findings

### 1. Canada publishes Starlink foreign-satellite FSS entries, but not the underlying authorization records

ISED’s current Client Procedures Circular CPC-2-6-04, Issue 3 (April 2026), requires a foreign-satellite approval (FSA) before a Canadian earth station may communicate with a foreign-licensed satellite. The procedure states that after approval ISED publishes the commercial satellite name, associated ITU filing, orbital position and authorized frequencies. Unless an approval says otherwise, it covers Canadian territory. The procedure replaced Issue 2 (May 2025). ([CPC-2-6-04](https://www.ised-isde.canada.ca/site/spectrum-management-telecommunications/en/learn-more/key-documents/procedures/client-procedures-circulars-cpc/cpc-2-6-04-procedure-submission-applications-approve-use-foreign-licensed-satellites-canada))

The ISED FSS list, last modified 19 August 2026, includes Starlink entries associated with ITU filing names STEAM-1, STEAM-2, STEAM-2B, USASAT-NGSO-3B-R, USASAT-NGSO-3D, USASAT-NGSO-3M/-3N/-3O/-3P, USASAT-NGSO-3X, USASAT-NGSO-3-Y1, USASAT-NGSO-MULS and USASAT-NGSO-MULS2. The listed bands include Ku, Ka, E-band and V-band ranges. This establishes published FSS market-access entries for those named networks and frequency ranges as displayed on that date; it does not establish which legal person holds each approval, an individual approval number/date, the current underlying U.S. authorization, or all approval-specific conditions. ([ISED foreign FSS satellite list](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/spectrum-management-telecommunications/en/licences-and-certificates/list-foreign-satellites-approved-provide-fixed-satellite-services-fss-canada))

The distinction matters because CPC-2-6-04 Annex A asks for the applicant’s full name and address; if the applicant differs from the satellite operator, it also asks for the operator’s name, the relationship between them and the operator’s agreement to the application. It also calls for the services, ITU filings, frequency ranges and, when relevant, Canadian earth-station locations. Those application fields are not shown in the published FSS list. The public list therefore cannot, by itself, establish the FSA holder or the effect of a change in that holder’s ownership or control.

### 2. The general FSA rules require ISED approval for operational changes, but do not state a general change-of-control trigger

For ordinary foreign-satellite approvals, CPC-2-6-04 says holders are expected to inform ISED of changes in satellite operations. Changes to satellite operation—including orbital parameters or frequency assignments—must be approved through an amendment for Canadian market access to continue. Approval conditions can also be added case by case; noncompliance can lead to revocation. Ordinary FSA terms have no expiry date, unlike temporary, developmental and SMCS approvals. ([CPC-2-6-04, §§7–8](https://www.ised-isde.canada.ca/site/spectrum-management-telecommunications/en/learn-more/key-documents/procedures/client-procedures-circulars-cpc/cpc-2-6-04-procedure-submission-applications-approve-use-foreign-licensed-satellites-canada))

The reviewed general FSA provisions do not expressly say that a transfer of shares, a change in ultimate parent, or a change in control of the satellite operator automatically transfers, terminates or requires a new FSA. Nor do they establish that a change in control is irrelevant. The underlying approval, its case-specific conditions and the applicant/operator relationship are not available in the public FSS list.

Radiocommunication Act §5(1) authorizes the Minister to issue radio/spectrum licences and other radiocommunication authorizations and to set or amend their terms. Paragraphs 5(1)(h)–(i) permit the Minister to require information about the present/proposed use of radio apparatus and to require notice of material changes in that information. These provisions are not, on their face, an automatic rule that every corporate acquisition requires prior consent; whether an ownership or control change is reportable for a particular authorization depends on the information required and the authorization’s actual conditions. ([Radiocommunication Act, §5](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/R-2/page-1.html))

### 3. Do not extend the special SMCS non-transferability term to every Starlink FSS entry

CPC-2-6-04 Annex C makes an FSA for supplemental mobile coverage by satellite (SMCS) non-transferable in whole or in part. It also requires a valid SMCS agreement with a Canadian mobile-service provider and limits service to the areas and frequency blocks supported by that agreement. The text places that non-transferability rule in the SMCS-specific annex; Annex B contains the general FSA conditions. The published Starlink rows and the reviewed 2026 SpaceX Canada E-band decision concern fixed-satellite service (FSS). No Starlink-specific SMCS FSA was identified in the reviewed public material, so this pass does not apply the SMCS-only restriction to Starlink’s listed FSS entries. A separate SMCS authorization, if any, must be checked in the native ISED record. ([CPC-2-6-04, Annexes B–C](https://www.ised-isde.canada.ca/site/spectrum-management-telecommunications/en/learn-more/key-documents/procedures/client-procedures-circulars-cpc/cpc-2-6-04-procedure-submission-applications-approve-use-foreign-licensed-satellites-canada))

### 4. Canadian station licences are separate instruments and have express transfer-approval rules

ISED treats foreign-satellite approval, Canadian space-station spectrum licences, site-approved earth-station spectrum licences and generic earth-station spectrum licences as distinct authorizations. For Canadian space-station licences, CPC-2-6-02 says a licence may not be transferred or assigned without Minister authorization; ISED reviews the new licensee’s eligibility and Canadian direction/control and whether technical parameters and customer service remain unchanged. For both site-approved and generic earth-station licences, CPC-2-6-01 and CPC-2-6-03 also require Minister authorization for transfer or assignment and review the new licensee’s eligibility, operating/technical parameters and customer service. ([CPC-2-6-02](https://www.ised-isde.canada.ca/site/spectrum-management-telecommunications/en/learn-more/key-documents/procedures/client-procedures-circulars-cpc/cpc-2-6-02-licensing-space-stations); [CPC-2-6-01](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/spectrum-management-telecommunications/en/learn-more/key-documents/procedures/client-procedures-circulars-cpc/cpc-2-6-01-procedure-submission-applications-license-fixed-earth-stations-and-approve-use-foreign); [CPC-2-6-03](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/spectrum-management-telecommunications/en/learn-more/key-documents/procedures/client-procedures-circulars-cpc/cpc-2-6-03-procedure-submission-applications-generic-earth-station-spectrum-licences))

Those express transfer rules concern the relevant licence. They do not, without the actual licence conditions and transaction structure, answer whether acquisition of the shares or control of a Canadian licensee is itself a licence transfer. They also do not turn a Canadian earth-station licence into the foreign-satellite approval for the constellation.

### 5. The 2026 E-band decision is a narrow, site-specific FSS record, not a complete authorization file

ISED’s 21 April 2026 decision identifies SpaceX Canada Corp. as the requesting company for 71–76 GHz downlink and 81–86 GHz uplink communication between Starlink filing USASAT-NGSO-3X and two gateway sites: Janet, Alberta, and Saint-Laurent, Quebec. Decision D1 states that ISED will temporarily authorize that use on a no-interference/no-protection basis, subject to coordination with fixed-service licensees and future Canadian decisions, including possible WRC-27 outcomes. It is evidence of a specific FSS request and policy decision; it is not an individual licence/search extract, does not identify the holder of every Starlink FSA, and does not establish a complete list of Canadian gateway rights. ([ISED E-band decision](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/spectrum-management-telecommunications/en/learn-more/key-documents/consultations/decision-request-received-spacex-canada-corp-use-e-band-frequencies-canada))

ISED’s satellite licensing page provides a public Licence Search for viewing licences and approvals without signing in. No matched native ISED licence record or approval instrument was obtained for this pass. The prior Item 1 E-band audit likewise left the actual SMS licence/site-approval extract and foreign-satellite approval for USASAT-NGSO-3X unresolved. The public approval list and policy decision must not be substituted for those records.

**Drafting disposition:** No bill amendment is supported by this pass. Sections 104 and 403 already require the transaction-specific authorization and consent evidence that the public sources do not provide. The gap is the missing underlying ISED file and any specific holder/conditions, not a demonstrated omission in Bill v1.4.
