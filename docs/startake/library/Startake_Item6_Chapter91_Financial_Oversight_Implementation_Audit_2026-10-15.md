# Item 6 — Chapter 91 financial oversight and implementation audit

**Research date:** 15 October 2026 UTC. **Claim:** `STARTAKE-20261015-WORK-ITEM6-CH91-IMPLEMENTATION-8f2d6c`. **Status:** Research pass complete; formal verification pending. **Scope:** 31 U.S.C. §§9103–9109 and Integrated Working Bill v1.3 §§201(c)–(d), 206, 208, and 301–303. This pass assumes the prior classification audit’s conclusion that §201(c), now accompanied by §201(d), applies Chapter 91 to the proposed Corporation; it does not repeat the §§9101–9102 classification question.

## Determination

Under the bill as drafted, the Chapter 91 requirements in §§9103–9108 attach to the Corporation through Act §201(c), with §201(d) adding the Corporation to §9101(3)’s list. The bill’s express annual audit clause, Treasury operating account, and no-debt-beyond-available-authority language align with §§9105, 9107, and 9108. Section 301(a) is only an authorization of appropriations; §9104 is not an operating appropriation; §208(a)(1) separately and expressly appropriates only service receipts actually collected and credited to the Treasury account. The existing bill does not authorize empty-account spending, debt beyond available budget authority, or off-Treasury banking.

Two narrow clarifications are warranted and are integrated in **Bill v1.4**. First, §301(b)’s ten-year budget to Congress is not the annual business-type budget that §9103 requires the Corporation to submit to the President. Second, §9109 creates a later exclusion path: when the President considers it practicable and in the public interest, the President shall include a recommendation in the §1105 budget; congressional approval would remove the Corporation from Chapter 91 for later fiscal years while leaving the corporation intact. Although current §206(b) separately commands an annual audit “under chapter 91,” its operation after §9109 is not explicit, and §9106’s annual management report is not separately stated in the Act. Bill v1.4 makes the §§9105–9106 audit and management-report requirements Act-specific and continuing after a §9109 exclusion. It does not prevent §9109’s change to Chapter 11 and fiscal treatment or preserve every Chapter 91 fiscal rule.

This result does not establish an appropriation amount, actual budget classification, revenue forecast, CBO score, procurement/receipt total, or professional legal review.

## Integrated drafting changes

### Section 301(b): separate the two budgets

Bill v1.4 appends:

> The submission under this subsection is in addition to, and does not satisfy, the annual business-type budget required of the Corporation by section 9103 of title 31, United States Code.

This states the existing consequence of §201(c) clearly; it does not create a second ten-year submission or replace the Corporation’s annual responsibility under §9103.

### Section 206(b): preserve audits and management reports after §9109

Bill v1.4 replaces the opening sentence of §206(b) with:

> The Corporation shall comply with section 9105 of title 31, United States Code, for annual audits of its financial statements and shall submit annual management reports in accordance with section 9106 of that title. Those requirements shall continue to apply notwithstanding any exclusion of the Corporation from chapter 91 of that title under section 9109 of that title.

The remaining §206(b) sentences—Comptroller General and Commerce Inspector General access and Commerce IG oversight—are preserved. This does not designate the Commerce IG as the Corporation’s Inspector General, prohibit a §9109 exclusion, or retain all budget/account rules of Chapter 91 after exclusion. The clarification makes the Act’s own audit/report command unambiguous and allows §9109’s move to Chapter 11 for fiscal matters.

## Contrary readings and limits

1. **No present Chapter 91 gap:** The strongest reading of §201(c), as amended by §201(d), is that it applies §§9103–9108 now. On that reading, §206(b)’s current audit clause and §301(b)’s ten-year report are supplements. The proposed edits are clarifying and continuity-protective, not a claim that the existing text fails to incorporate Chapter 91.
2. **Existing §206(b) may already survive §9109:** A reader could view its direct “shall be audited” command as an independent Act duty that remains effective after exclusion. Another reader could treat “under chapter 91” as dependent on Chapter 91 coverage and conclude that the cross-reference no longer supplies the auditor/report machinery after §9109. The new “notwithstanding” sentence resolves this without choosing the broad result that all of Chapter 91 must continue.
3. **Future fiscal substitution:** After an approved §9109 recommendation, Chapter 11/fiscal treatment attaches for later fiscal years. This report does not assess those separate requirements or opine on a hypothetical recommendation. The proposed preservation is limited to §§9105–9106, consistent with §206’s audit/report objective.
4. **Inspector General assignment remains fact- and text-dependent:** Section 9105 allows a Corporation IG or an external auditor selected by the IG—or, if there is no Corporation IG, by the Corporation head. Section 206(b)’s Commerce IG component-oversight language does not itself clearly settle whether that officer is the §9105 IG. Bill v1.4 points directly to §9105’s selection rules rather than silently designating an office. Congress may choose to state the relationship expressly.
5. **No automatic funds or score:** §9104 and an authorization of appropriations do not supply this project’s amount, valuation, cash-flow assumption, or official score. The acquisition and continuity appropriations in §§303(a)–(b) remain placeholders. An OMB Circular A-11 publication page lists the 2025 circular, but the §9103(a) submission form/date remains regulation-dependent; no corporation-specific instruction, CBO estimate, or OMB/GAO opinion is claimed.
