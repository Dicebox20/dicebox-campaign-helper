# Item 5 — Section 208 operating-account receipts and federal scorekeeping audit

**Research date:** 15 October 2026 UTC. **Assignment:** STARTAKE-20261015-WORK-ITEM5-SEC208-SCOREKEEPING-1-8c2f7a, reserved and independently read back at tracker v192. **Question:** whether proposed §§208(a), 301(c), and 303 clearly authorize, limit, classify, and report operating-account receipts and appropriated deposits.

## Finding

Proposed §208 can lawfully establish a Treasury operating account and an express permanent appropriation of specified receipts if Congress chooses that policy. GAO describes revolving funds as appropriations that Congress must create expressly, with the statute defining receipts, purposes, and availability. Section 208 already identifies the account, service receipts, operating purposes, no-year language, and no-borrowing controls; the basic structure is therefore retained.

The v0.8 text did not separate actual service receipts from deposits made under a separate appropriation. It made “amounts in the Account” available until expended and described both actual receipts and appropriated deposits as permanently appropriated. That wording could convert a time-limited operating appropriation into no-year authority when transferred to the account. Section 303(c)(2), however, says this Act does not make an amount in a closed appropriation account available for a new obligation and otherwise preserves the governing period-of-availability rules. The interaction is a drafting ambiguity, not a conclusion that either clause is invalid. The v0.9 text clarifies that service receipts expressly appropriated by §208 may be available without fiscal-year limitation, while separately appropriated deposits keep the purpose, amount, and period stated in their source appropriation.

Budget classification also turns on the nature and source of a collection and the law authorizing its credit. OMB Circular A-11 distinguishes governmental receipts from offsetting collections and offsetting receipts. A collection authorized to be credited to an expenditure account is generally treated as an offsetting collection; OMB says those accounts report budget authority and outlays both gross and net. A service-receipt label alone does not establish the final classification. Proposed §301(c) already bars treating enterprise/equity value as the federal score and leaves classification/scoring subject to law; v0.9 adds source, authority, classification-assumption, receivable, and gross/net reporting detail. No CBO estimate or official score is produced here.

## Governing sources and application

- **Treasury deposit rule.** 31 U.S.C. §3302(b) generally requires money received for the Government to be deposited in the Treasury, except as otherwise provided by law. Proposed §208 places the account in the Treasury and directs deposits there; no separate bank account or off-Treasury authority is proposed.
- **Purpose and amount controls.** 31 U.S.C. §§1301(a) and 1341(a)(1) restrict use to the authorized purpose and bar obligations or expenditures exceeding available appropriations or fund amounts, subject to express legal authority. Proposed §§208(b), 303(c), and 303(d) already address purpose, no-borrowing, available authority, and documentary obligation recording.
- **Documentary obligation support.** 31 U.S.C. §1501 requires documentary evidence before recording a federal obligation. Proposed §303(d) cites that rule.
- **Period of availability.** 31 U.S.C. §1502(a) limits a definite-period appropriation to expenses incurred or contracts properly made during that period. Proposed §303(c)(2) preserves that limit. The new §208(a)(2) ensures that depositing such money in the operating account does not itself extend the period or reopen a closed account.
- **Explicit receipt and revolving-fund authority.** GAO’s B-335844 (2024) explains that revolving funds remain appropriations; their statutes should specify receipts, authorized purposes, and availability, and they remain subject to purpose, Antideficiency Act, and obligation-recording controls. The operating account resembles this structure; this audit does not decide its formal fund classification.
- **Budget terminology and scoring.** CBO explains that budget authority is authority provided by law, obligations are binding commitments, and outlays generally occur when cash is disbursed. OMB Circular A-11 (2025), §20.7, says collection treatment depends on the transaction and law; offsetting collections are credited to expenditure accounts and budget authority/outlays are shown gross and net. Section 21.5 states Appendix A’s scorekeeping guidelines are used by the House and Senate Budget Committees, CBO, and OMB. The legally appropriate classification and score for this proposed corporation remain for official budget review.

## Legislative revision

The [Integrated Working Bill v0.9](The_Starlink_and_Stripes_Act_Integrated_Working_Bill_v0.9_2026-10-15.md) changes only §§208(a) and 301(c) from v0.8:

1. Section 208(a) now appropriates actual service receipts credited to the Treasury account for the specified operating purposes without fiscal-year limitation. A separate appropriation deposited in the account retains its source law’s purpose, amount, and availability period. The account may not obligate or expend more than amounts legally available.
2. Section 301(c) requires the ten-year budget submission to distinguish public service collections, intragovernmental collections, appropriated deposits, and uncollected receivables; identify credit/availability authority and classification assumptions; and show gross and net budget authority and outlays for offsetting collections where applicable.

The other 29 numbered sections are byte-identical to v0.8. Section 303(c)(2) remains unchanged and now reads consistently with section 208(a)(2). No revenue, price, appropriation, financing assumption, or score has been supplied. A counsel/CBO/OMB review remains necessary to determine entity/account classification, treatment of service receipts, PAYGO or other budget enforcement effects, apportionment, and official scorekeeping.

## Contrary interpretation and remaining questions

Congress could intend §208’s words “until expended” to make all deposits, including amounts separately appropriated for operating purposes, available on a no-year basis. If so, Congress should state that choice expressly and reconcile it with §303(c)(2), rather than leave it to inference. The v0.9 draft takes the conservative continuity-of-appropriation approach: a transfer into the operating account does not change another appropriation’s period unless the source law says otherwise.

This bounded pass did not conduct a public-source search for commercial service receipts; it therefore supplies no actual revenue, Starlink-only operating forecast, or official federal budget score. Existing model inputs for actual revenue, costs, cash balances, and valuation remain missing where not authenticated. This pass supplies a cleaner legal and budget-reporting structure but does not complete Item 5 or formal acceptance.
