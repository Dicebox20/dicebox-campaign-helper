# FCC Debris-Authorization Handoff and Section 107(b) Drafting Audit

**Research date:** 16 October 2026 UTC  
**Claim:** STARTAKE-20261016-WORK-ITEM3-DEBRIS-107-DRAFT-bd4e8a  
**Status:** Bounded primary-source verification and drafting pass complete; formal acceptance pending.  
**Scope:** Proposed acquisition transition plan for U.S. FCC satellite authorizations, with orbital-debris conditions and continuing duties.  
**Disposition:** Bill v1.5 adds a procedural handoff inventory in §107(b). No FCC authorization is transferred by the draft and no operational compliance or cost conclusion is made.

## Question and conclusion

Does Bill v1.4 §107(b), together with §107(a), adequately identify the authorization-specific information and successor arrangements needed to maintain FCC orbital-debris duties through a proposed public acquisition? The prior transfer audit found that §107(a) preserves FCC authority and requires permissions before affected operations transfer, while §107(b) requires a transaction-specific domestic authorization plan. The text does not expressly require an authorization-by-authorization inventory of applicable debris plans and conditions, reporting duties and deadlines, outstanding reports, responsible records custodians, or proposed successor officials.

A narrow amendment is warranted as a transaction-planning and public-accountability control. Bill v1.5 requires the plan to identify those items for each affected Commission authorization, the filing/action path required for the proposed transaction and its status, the post-closing entity and designated official proposed to carry each continuing duty, and records that remain unavailable or unverified. It expressly leaves all Commission action required by law in force and says the plan itself does not effect a transfer, satisfy a condition, or prove that a report was filed.

This is a new proposed statutory checklist, not a claim that current FCC law independently requires this specific public handoff plan or assigns every existing duty automatically to a successor. The change does not alter the bounded disposal rule, create a new orbital-debris performance standard, or establish a real transaction, license status, report filing, spacecraft performance, compliance, or disposal cost.

## Method and strongest counterpoint

The review compared the official Code and CFR provisions identified below, the cited FCC orders and authorization, the released primary-source orbital-debris and transfer reports, Bill v1.4 §§107 and 208, and the exact original crosswalk rows 05.09 and M16.01. The public ICFS transaction record and a complete current authorization-by-authorization condition set were not available in the preceding audits, so this is a proposed plan-content control and does not claim to populate the plan with current license data.

The strongest argument for leaving §107(b) unchanged is that §107(a) already disclaims automatic transfer and requires applicable permissions before operations move, while §107(b) already requires a transaction-specific plan. Existing FCC rules and orders separately govern the underlying authorizations and conditions. The answer is limited: those provisions do not specify the minimum handoff fields in the published plan, and a transaction must distinguish prior-approval, notification, and modification routes. The amendment supplies an oversight checklist but creates no new FCC transfer path or substantive disposal duty.


## Primary-source findings

### Transfer, control, and modification paths are transaction-specific

47 U.S.C. §310(d) governs transfers, assignments, or dispositions of a construction permit, station license, or rights under it, including indirect disposition and transfer of control, and requires an application and public-interest finding for covered transactions. 47 C.F.R. §25.119 supplies the satellite-authorization procedures and exceptions. It distinguishes assignments from changes in control; it also provides pro-forma and receive-only paths that do not uniformly require the same prior approval. 47 C.F.R. §25.117(a) generally requires Commission application and grant for a modification affecting authorization parameters or terms, subject to the exceptions in §25.118. Accordingly, the Act should require a plan to identify the filing or Commission action that actually applies and its status, rather than declaring that every ownership or operator change requires an identical prior approval.

### Disposal and Gen2 duties are conditional and authorization-specific

The official 2025 CFR text of 47 C.F.R. §25.283(e) states the five-year outer limit for the defined LEO uncontrolled-reentry cohort: spacecraft ending their mission in or passing through the LEO region below 2,000 km and planning uncontrolled atmospheric re-entry must be disposed of as soon as practicable and no later than five years after end of mission. The prior orbital-debris audit separately documents the rule’s legacy transition categories and stored-energy duty. Those bounded categories are preserved; this amendment does not broaden them.

FCC 22-91 imposed separate Gen2 authorization conditions, including semiannual reporting on launches and disposal failures with remaining-orbital-lifetime projections, and a deployment pause when cumulative failed-satellite object-years exceed 100 pending review of failure sources and mitigation. FCC DA 24-1193 continued semiannual Gen2 reporting, and DA 26-36 contains a later partial Gen2 authorization and deferral. These are not interchangeable with the generally applicable disposal rule. FCC 26-51 states that the newly adopted Part 100 rules were not yet in effect as of that Commission action; the materials reviewed here do not establish a later effective-date notice. The amendment therefore refers to the applicable rule and authorization conditions, rather than hard-coding Part 25. The plan should identify applicable plans, conditions, report periods and deadlines, filing status, and deployment restrictions without presuming that any filing occurred or any condition was met.

### Bill text gap and limited drafting response

Existing §107(a) says the Act does not itself transfer a radio authorization or approve a control change and that required permissions must precede affected operations transferring. Existing §107(b) calls for a transaction-specific domestic authorization plan addressing Communications Act §§301, 305, and 310, but does not state the minimum debris-continuity inventory. Section 208 contains general lawful-operation and maintenance language; it supplies neither this pre-close plan nor a verified disposal-cost amount.

Because the plan is published, the revised text also makes publication subject to applicable law protecting classified, privileged, or confidential commercial information. The new §107(b) text adds only that missing plan-content checklist. Its fields support traceability: identify the affected authorization and holder; identify the filing or action and status; list the applicable mitigation plan, conditions, report duties/deadlines, filing status, and deployment restrictions; identify records custody and transfer/retention method; and name the proposed successor entity and responsible official subject to Commission action. Unknown or unavailable records must remain visible. The text does not deem filing, compliance, transfer, or approval. It creates no power to compel private records outside otherwise applicable authority.

## Bill disposition

Bill v1.5 replaces only §107(b). The other 30 numbered operative sections remain byte-identical to v1.4, and the Act retains 31 numbered sections. No changes are made to §107(a), the five-year disposal rule, legacy treatment, §208 cost provisions, appropriations, acquisition perimeter, or operational authorization. A future transaction plan must use current source-native FCC records; this bill revision is not evidence that such a plan or handoff exists.
