## Executive proposal

Create a wholly public satellite communications operator and authorize a lawful, compensated acquisition of the separable commercial Starlink business. Negotiate first, preserve service throughout the transition and secure essential launch, manufacturing, software and foreign permissions. Use the narrowly drafted domestic-property condemnation fallback only with explicit statutory authority, judicial process and full compensation. Protect all customers against political discrimination and unauthorized surveillance, and make core service and privacy duties enforceable in court.

The 31 sections below form five integrated titles: acquisition (101–110); operator and rights (201–208); finance (301–303); transition and review (401–404); judicial review and effectiveness (501–503), plus sections 1–3. These are substantive proposed provisions. Missing asset schedules, valuation, dollar appropriations, factual findings and professional redlines remain blocking inputs. A draft is progress, not evidence that its conditions can be met.

## Legal anchors and design choices

**Government action and expression.** In [Lebron v. National Railroad Passenger Corp., 513 U.S. 374 (21 February 1995)](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/ll/usrep/usrep513/usrep513374/usrep513374.pdf), the Court held that a specially created corporation serving governmental objectives with permanent government power to appoint most directors is governmental for First Amendment purposes. It did not decide the ultimate advertisement dispute. The proposed charter expressly accepts public duties rather than relying on corporate form to avoid them.

**Records and privacy.** [5 U.S.C. §552(f)](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/119-118/t5/s552/) includes government corporations in FOIA's agency definition, with statutory exemptions. [5 U.S.C. §552a(a)](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/119-118/t5/s552a/) defines individuals and systems of records with limits; it is not a worldwide customer privacy code. Sections 201 and 204 therefore expressly apply agency duties and create additional all-customer statutory protections. Those new protections and the section 207 cause of action are policy proposals, not existing customer remedies.

**Corporate finances.** [31 U.S.C. chapter 91](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/119-120/t31/) sets the government-corporation framework; the current title index identifies §§9101–9108 on definition, creation, budgets, audits, reports, accounts and obligations. Section 201 explicitly applies that framework rather than assuming an unformed entity is already listed. Section 208 is a NEW permanent appropriation of actual operating-account receipts; section 301 merely authorizes later appropriations. Independent fiscal counsel/CBO must assess the interaction, scorekeeping, revenue treatment, apportionment and implementation. No borrowing power or empty-account spending authority is proposed.

**Security and cost.** [NIST CSF 2.0, released 26 February 2024](https://www.nist.gov/news-events/news/2024/02/nist-releases-version-20-landmark-cybersecurity-framework) emphasizes governance and supply-chain risk. [GAO-20-195G, 12 March 2020](https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-20-195g) calls for documented technical baselines, assumptions and cost-risk analysis. These frameworks inform proposed gates; neither is evidence the Starlink network passed a test or a binding acquisition-specific approval.

**Acquisition limits.** The [prior acquisition-title report](The_Starlink_and_Stripes_Act_Acquisition_Title_Working_Draft_v0.1_2026-10-15.md) contains externally checked statutory sources, adverse interpretations and caveats incorporated into this bill. In particular, [40 U.S.C. §3114](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&num=0&req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title40-section3114) is land-specific, and [47 U.S.C. §310(d)](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=%28title%3A47+section%3A310+edition%3Aprelim%29) separately governs relevant transfer permissions. The new text is not a determination that orbital assets, patents, foreign sites or the complete commercial network can be compelled under existing law.

## Proposed bill text

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the United States of America in Congress assembled,

### SEC. 1. SHORT TITLE.

This Act may be cited as “The Starlink and Stripes Act”.

### SEC. 2. PURPOSES AND RULES OF CONSTRUCTION.

(a) PURPOSES.—The purposes of this Act are to establish publicly owned commercial satellite communications infrastructure; maintain reliable civilian, emergency and commercial connectivity; provide lawful continuity for public customers; protect communications rights; and acquire property only through voluntary agreement or constitutionally valid proceedings with just compensation.

(b) NONPUNITIVE APPLICATION.—No determination, acquisition price, service decision or enforcement action under this Act may be based on an individual's political affiliation, protected expression, disability, or personal animus.

(c) EVIDENCE.—The Secretary shall publish a dated acquisition rationale identifying dependence, service concentration, resilience objectives, costs, alternatives, contrary evidence and uncertainty. No numerical fleet share, incident allegation, private financial estimate, or disputed attribution is incorporated as a congressional factual finding by this section.

### SEC. 3. DEFINITIONS.

In this Act:

(1) “Secretary” means the Secretary of Commerce.
(2) “Corporation” means the public operator established under title II of this Act.
(3) “Covered commercial business” means the commercial Starlink communications business to the extent identified in the schedule under section 101, excluding unrelated SpaceX businesses, dedicated Starshield and classified programs, and property of customers or third parties except specifically identified interests lawfully acquired from their owners.
(4) “Covered interest” means a specifically described property, contractual, intellectual-property or equity interest proposed for acquisition under section 102; it does not mean the electromagnetic spectrum, a foreign sovereign authorization, or the right to compel personal labor.
(5) “Closing” means the transfer of a covered interest under an executed agreement or final judicial transfer order, as applicable.

## TITLE I—LAWFUL COMMERCIAL ACQUISITION

### SEC. 101. TRANSACTION SCHEDULE AND SEPARATION PLAN.

(a) Before committing to acquisition, the Secretary shall prepare an owner-by-interest schedule identifying the proposed commercial assets, obligations, liabilities, encumbrances, necessary permissions, shared services and exclusions. The schedule shall distinguish assets from nontransferable permissions, acquired ownership from licensed use, and verified facts from unresolved claims.

(b) The Secretary shall obtain independent legal, financial and engineering assessments of the schedule, separability and continuity. Commercially sensitive and security information shall be protected under applicable law; a public summary shall identify material limitations and categories withheld.

(c) Inclusion on a schedule creates no title, lien release, regulatory consent, entitlement to confidential information, or presumption against an affected owner. This Act confers no investigative subpoena power.

### SEC. 102. NEGOTIATED ACQUISITION AUTHORITY.

(a) Subject to appropriations and sections 101, 104 and 110, the Secretary is authorized to acquire, for transfer to the Corporation, covered interests through negotiated purchase, lease, license, or acquisition of equity in a voluntarily separated commercial entity.

(b) The Secretary may negotiate transition-service, launch, manufacturing, intellectual-property, software-support and access agreements necessary for independent operation. Such agreements shall specify price, term, service obligations, remedies, renewal options and exit arrangements. This subsection neither transfers excluded property nor obligates a counterparty without agreement.

(c) No contract may require acquisition of unrelated businesses as a condition of closing unless Congress expressly authorizes the expanded perimeter by a subsequent law. No officer may waive the exclusions in section 3 by administrative designation.

### SEC. 103. APPRAISAL AND FAIR NEGOTIATION.

(a) The Secretary shall retain at least two independent appraisers with relevant enterprise and asset expertise, disclose material conflicts, and reconcile divergent conclusions in writing. Valuation shall distinguish enterprise value, equity value, liabilities, required separation costs, acquired rights and retained rights, avoiding duplicate payment for the same interest.

(b) Owners shall receive an intelligible description of the interest sought and a supported written offer, with a reasonable opportunity to obtain advice, present contrary evidence and negotiate. No acquisition discount may be imposed as punishment or by equating historical government spending with property already owned by the United States.

(c) No appraisal, negotiated payment or budget estimate binds a court's constitutional compensation determination.

### SEC. 104. CONDITIONS TO CLOSING.

The Secretary may close only after certifying, on a reviewable record, that—

(1) the specific title, interests, liabilities and creditor treatment are resolved to a legally sufficient standard;
(2) all permissions required for that closing and continued operation are effective, or lawful transitional permissions cover the relevant period;
(3) critical workforce, manufacturing, launch, network control, software, security, supplier and customer continuity arrangements are funded and executable;
(4) the Corporation is legally constituted, has responsible officers and has demonstrated operational readiness through independently witnessed tests;
(5) lawful budget authority covers the obligations proposed to be incurred, with a disclosed compensation and transition contingency plan; and
(6) material unresolved risks, dissenting assessments, alternatives and mitigation measures have been reported to Congress at least 60 days before closing.

Expiration of the 60-day period does not waive any other condition. The report is notice, not a committee veto or authorization for either House to change legal obligations outside enactment of law.

### SEC. 105. LIMITED JUDICIAL CONDEMNATION AUTHORITY.

(a) NEW DELEGATION.—The Secretary, acting through the Attorney General, may institute a civil action in the name of the United States to acquire a specifically identified domestic real-property interest or domestic tangible personal-property interest indispensable to the covered commercial business, only after a supported good-faith offer and not less than 180 days of negotiations unless the owner expressly declines further negotiation.

(b) NECESSITY RECORD.—Before instituting the action the Secretary shall publish findings explaining the public use, the precise interest, the negotiations, why purchase or a less burdensome interest is unavailable, and why the taking is necessary for the public communications purposes of this Act. Political retaliation, punishment and private favoritism are prohibited purposes.

(c) EXCLUSIONS.—This section does not authorize compulsory transfer of corporate shares, foreign-situs property, satellites situated outside the territorial United States, intellectual property, customer property, classified systems, regulatory permissions, or contracts requiring third-party performance. Acquisition of such interests must proceed by lawful agreement or further specific legislation. A court shall determine any disputed territorial situs; the Secretary's designation is not conclusive.

(d) NEW JURISDICTION AND PROCEDURE.—The district courts of the United States shall have original jurisdiction over actions under this section involving covered domestic real or tangible personal property, including objections to authority, public use, compliance with this Act, and compensation. Venue shall lie in a district where the property is situated. Rule 71.1 of the Federal Rules of Civil Procedure shall apply except as expressly provided by this Act. Affected owners and lienholders shall receive notice and a meaningful opportunity to contest authority, scope and compensation.

(e) NO PERSONAL-PROPERTY QUICK TAKE.—Title or possession to personal property shall transfer only after a judicial determination sustaining authority and public use, determination and payment or court deposit of compensation, and a court order specifying the interest and possession date. This Act does not extend the declaration-of-taking procedure in section 3114 of title 40 to personal property.

(f) LAND PROCEDURE.—For qualifying real-property interests, sections 3113 through 3118 of title 40 may be used only in accordance with their terms, available appropriations, this Act and the Constitution. No occupation before lawful possession is authorized by this Act.

### SEC. 106. JUST COMPENSATION AND CLAIMANT RIGHTS.

(a) No property may be taken under this Act without just compensation. The United States shall be liable for the full amount constitutionally required, including any required interest. A payment estimate, price ceiling or deposit does not extinguish that liability.

(b) Compensation shall be determined for legally recognized interests under applicable constitutional law, with evidence of fair value and any legally compensable associated loss. No categorical statutory exclusion of legally compensable goodwill, trade secrets or going-concern value is created. No entitlement to speculative, duplicative or legally noncompensable value is created.

(c) The court shall determine entitlement among owners and encumbrance holders, protect disputed proceeds and permit withdrawal of undisputed deposits without surrender of a claim to additional lawful compensation. No claimant shall recover twice for the same taking.

(d) The United States consents to suit in the action under section 105 for compensation, interest and relief necessary to enforce that section. Other remedies and jurisdiction otherwise available for a taking are preserved, subject to applicable jurisdictional requirements. Nothing in this Act treats the Tucker Act as the source of acquisition authority or promises simultaneous jurisdiction in multiple courts.

### SEC. 107. REGULATORY AND INTERNATIONAL SAVINGS.

(a) This Act does not itself transfer a radio authorization, assign frequencies, approve control changes, establish foreign landing rights, or alter a treaty obligation. Required permissions shall be obtained from the responsible authorities before affected operations transfer.

(b) Before closing, the Secretary, the Federal Communications Commission and the National Telecommunications and Information Administration shall publish a transaction-specific domestic authorization plan, including the applicability of sections 301, 305 and 310 of the Communications Act of 1934. No assumption that federal ownership automatically resolves the FCC–federal spectrum boundary is authorized.

(c) Foreign property and permissions shall be acquired or continued only through agreements and approvals effective under the relevant jurisdiction's law. Domestic enactment creates no presumption of foreign recognition.

### SEC. 108. CONTRACTS, INTELLECTUAL PROPERTY AND WORKERS.

(a) Contract succession, software and intellectual-property access shall occur only under legally effective assignments, licenses, novations or other lawful instruments. Payment for a physical asset does not by itself convey associated intellectual property or third-party rights.

(b) No individual shall be compelled to work for the United States, the Corporation or a contractor. Employment offers, benefits, bargaining obligations, pensions and any workforce transition shall be addressed in a disclosed plan consistent with applicable labor and employment law. Existing employee rights are not waived by this Act.

### SEC. 109. ACQUISITION ACCOUNTABILITY.

The Secretary shall deliver quarterly public progress reports and protected annexes as necessary, including the current schedule, offers and transaction status, expenditures, outstanding compensation exposure, continuity readiness, denied permissions, unresolved risks and alternatives. The Comptroller General shall have access to program records sufficient to audit implementation, subject to lawful protection of information. This section creates no power to obtain records from private persons beyond existing law or contract.

### SEC. 110. EXPIRATION OF NEW ACQUISITION AUTHORITY.

Authority to enter a new acquisition agreement or initiate a new condemnation action expires five years after enactment. Existing lawful obligations, pending proceedings, compensation rights, operating authority and safeguards survive. Extension or expansion requires a subsequent law.

## TITLE II—PUBLIC OPERATOR AND COMMUNICATIONS RIGHTS

### SEC. 201. ESTABLISHMENT AND PUBLIC OWNERSHIP.

(a) There is established a federally chartered, wholly owned Government corporation, the United States Public Satellite Communications Corporation. All ownership interests shall be held by the United States. No ownership interest or control may be sold, pledged or privatized without subsequent express statutory authority.

(b) The Corporation shall operate the acquired commercial business in the public interest, subject to this Act, applicable federal law and necessary domestic and foreign authorizations. It may hold acquired property, make lawful contracts, employ personnel, provide commercial communications and collect service charges. It has no independent power to condemn property, regulate competitors, compel labor, issue debt, guarantee obligations or exempt itself from law.

(c) For purposes of chapter 91 of title 31, United States Code, the Corporation shall be treated as a wholly owned Government corporation. For purposes of the Administrative Procedure Act, the Freedom of Information Act, the Privacy Act, federal records law, and federal information-security requirements, the Corporation shall be treated as an agency. No corporate label shall limit constitutional rights otherwise applicable to its conduct. No subsidiary may be used to evade these duties.

### SEC. 202. BOARD AND MANAGEMENT.

(a) The Corporation shall be governed by a seven-member Board appointed by the President, by and with the advice and consent of the Senate. The President shall designate a Chair from among confirmed members. Members shall have demonstrated expertise collectively covering satellite engineering, network security, public finance, communications law, consumer and rural service, emergency operations and civil liberties. Appointments shall be made without requiring representation of a political party or private provider.

(b) Members shall serve six-year terms, initially staggered as designated at appointment, subject to removal by the President. No holdover tenure or removal-for-cause protection is created. The President shall provide Congress a written explanation of a removal, without delaying or conditioning its effectiveness.

(c) Four serving members constitute a quorum. The Board shall appoint a chief executive officer and may remove that officer at any time. The chief executive shall be subordinate to Board direction and shall appoint subordinate personnel consistent with applicable law and funded staffing plans. No operating control may transfer before a lawful Board and management are in place.

(d) Members and senior officers shall disclose financial interests and material relationships, recuse from conflicting transactions, and comply with applicable federal ethics rules. No regulated or contracting entity may appoint a voting director. An advisory council representing employees, customers, emergency users and affected communities may advise but may not exercise executive or regulatory authority.

### SEC. 203. PROTECTION AGAINST POLITICAL INTERFERENCE.

(a) No officer, employee, director or contractor acting for the Corporation may deny, throttle, prioritize, terminate or price service to reward or punish a political viewpoint, lawful association, protected expression or electoral activity.

(b) Material government directions concerning a service restriction or customer-data disclosure shall identify the directing official, written legal authority, scope, duration and review procedure. The Corporation shall preserve an auditable record, refer apparently unlawful directions to counsel and the Inspector General, and refuse directions prohibited by law. A lawful order may be implemented immediately where delay is unlawful; documentation shall follow promptly where contemporaneous recording is infeasible.

(c) Nothing in this Act enlarges emergency, intelligence, surveillance, sanctions or military authority. Lawful restrictions shall be no broader or longer than authorized and reasonably necessary, with protected oversight where public disclosure is forbidden.

### SEC. 204. PRIVACY, CUSTOMER DATA AND LAWFUL ACCESS.

(a) The Corporation shall collect, retain, use and disclose customer content, traffic and identifying data only to provide and secure service, fulfill a lawful customer instruction, or comply with independently applicable law. It shall publish a specific purpose and retention schedule for each data class, minimize collection, segregate access, log disclosures, enforce least-privilege access and securely delete data when retention is no longer lawful or necessary, subject to lawful preservation duties.

(b) Customer data may not be sold for advertising or used to profile political opinion, lawful association or protected expression. Public ownership creates no new entitlement for an agency to obtain customer communications. No new interception, search, compelled decryption, bulk-collection or law-enforcement power is created.

(c) These statutory privacy duties protect all customers and natural persons whose data the Corporation processes, regardless of citizenship or residence; they supplement, and do not reduce, other applicable rights. Conflicts with foreign law shall be documented and referred to qualified counsel before market entry or transfer. A foreign demand shall not itself be treated as U.S. legal authority.

(d) The Corporation shall establish a privacy office reporting directly to the Board, conduct privacy impact assessments before material processing changes, and publish aggregate lawful-access and breach reports to the extent lawful. An affected person may seek access, correction or deletion consistent with lawful retention and protection of others' rights. An independent internal appeal shall be decided in writing.

### SEC. 205. FAIR ACCESS, SERVICE TERMS AND CUSTOMER PROCESS.

(a) Service terms, eligibility, prices, capacity-management rules, outage notices and complaint procedures shall be accessible and published. The Corporation shall provide service without unlawful discrimination and with reasonable disability accessibility. Capacity limits and safety controls shall be applied under published, technically justified rules.

(b) Suspension or termination shall ordinarily require notice, a reason and an opportunity to challenge the decision. Immediate action may address an imminent threat to network safety, fraud, a binding legal obligation or an emergency supported by documented facts, followed by prompt notice when lawful and an expedited independent review.

(c) Within one year of establishment, the Board shall publish an affordability and rural-access plan with measurable objectives, costs and funding needs after public consultation. No universal-coverage or below-cost-service promise may be treated as funded without identified lawful resources. Cross-subsidies and appropriated public-service support shall be separately disclosed.

### SEC. 206. TRANSPARENCY, RECORDS AND INDEPENDENT AUDIT.

(a) The Corporation shall publish quarterly service, outage, access-equity, financial, procurement and conflict-of-interest reports, Board minutes and material policy changes, with lawful protections for customer privacy, trade secrets, operational security and classified information. Redactions shall identify their legal basis where lawful. No blanket corporate exemption from public records duties is created.

(b) Annual financial statements shall be independently audited under chapter 91 of title 31. The Comptroller General and the Inspector General of the Department of Commerce shall have access to Corporation records and applicable contractor records as provided by law and enforceable contract. The Commerce Inspector General shall exercise oversight of the Corporation as a component for purposes of this Act; no new protected-tenure office is created.

(c) Personnel and contractor personnel shall have protected channels to report suspected illegality, political interference, fraud and security defects. Retaliation for a lawful protected disclosure is prohibited. This provision does not authorize public release of protected information or diminish existing whistleblower rights.

### SEC. 207. ENFORCEMENT AND REMEDIES.

(a) A person adversely affected by a violation of sections 203 through 205 may bring a civil action against the Corporation in a United States district court for declaratory or injunctive relief. The Corporation's immunity from that relief is expressly waived. Venue shall lie where the plaintiff resides within the United States, where the violation occurred, or in the District of Columbia where no other domestic venue is available.

(b) An administrative complaint may be filed but is not required before seeking judicial relief from imminent or ongoing harm. The court may award reasonable attorney fees to a prevailing plaintiff. This section creates no damages remedy and does not reduce any remedy otherwise available.

(c) No immunity waiver in this section authorizes compelled disclosure of privileged or classified information contrary to law. Courts shall use lawful protective procedures rather than treating security assertions as proof that the challenged conduct was lawful.

### SEC. 208. PUBLIC OPERATING ACCOUNT AND FINANCIAL DISCIPLINE.

(a) A Public Satellite Communications Operating Account is established in the Treasury. Service receipts and amounts specifically appropriated for operating purposes shall be deposited in that Account. Amounts in the Account are hereby made available to the Corporation, without further appropriation and until expended, solely for lawful operating, maintenance, replenishment and public-service costs under this Act, subject to applicable budget execution, apportionment, audit and reporting requirements. This paragraph is a proposed permanent appropriation of actual account receipts and appropriated deposits; it provides no borrowing authority or amount in excess of the balance.

(b) Acquisition consideration and condemnation awards shall be accounted for through title III, not concealed as routine operating expenditure. No obligation of the Corporation shall be guaranteed by the United States unless expressly authorized by subsequent law. No authority to issue bonds or incur debt beyond available budget authority is created.

(c) The annual budget shall identify launch and replacement costs, shared-service charges, affordability support, security costs, depreciation, cash reserves, actual obligations and outlays, and any unfunded requirement. The Board shall publish corrective plans for projected liquidity shortfalls before service continuity is threatened.


## TITLE III—FUNDING AND COMPENSATION ACCOUNTS

### SEC. 301. AUTHORIZATION AND BUDGET SUBMISSION.

(a) There are authorized to be appropriated such sums as may be necessary for due diligence, independent review, negotiated acquisition, lawful condemnation, compensation, transition and operation under this Act. This subsection is an authorization and does not itself make an appropriation or permit obligations in advance of appropriations.

(b) Within 180 days after enactment, the Secretary shall submit a ten-year budget with acquisition and transition sources and uses, operating receipts, replacement satellites and launch needs, cybersecurity, debt assumptions, scenarios, sensitivity ranges and independent appraisal limitations. No parent-company valuation or personal net-worth estimate may substitute for a commercial-business valuation.

### SEC. 302. ACQUISITION AND TRANSITION FUND.

(a) There is established in the Treasury a Starlink Public Acquisition and Transition Fund, consisting of amounts specifically appropriated to the Fund by law. Amounts shall be available only for purposes and periods stated in the applicable appropriation. Establishment of the Fund does not itself provide money, borrowing authority or a federal guarantee.

(b) Acquisition agreements and condemnation initiation require legally available budget authority and a written assessment of probable total liabilities and contingencies. No arbitrary ceiling shall be construed to reduce compensation constitutionally owed for property already taken. If estimated exposure exceeds available authority before a taking, the Secretary shall defer the taking and seek further legislation or appropriations.

(c) Acquisition price, judicial awards, interest, transition services and operating support shall be separately accounted for. Obligations and outlays shall be separately reported. This section neither redirects existing broadband or defense funds nor assumes that historical federal payments offset compensation.

## TITLE IV—CONTINUITY, SEPARATION AND INDEPENDENT REVIEW

### SEC. 401. STAGED TRANSITION PLAN.

(a) Within 180 days after enactment, the Secretary shall submit a staged transition plan and ten-year technical baseline. The plan shall identify negotiated launch and manufacturing capacity; satellite replenishment and disposal; tooling, suppliers and spares; software and source access; workforce and benefits; network and spacecraft control; customer, defense and emergency service obligations; foreign permissions; insurance and liabilities; and alternatives if a dependency cannot be secured.

(b) The plan shall exclude dedicated classified and Starshield systems from acquisition and require appropriately cleared review of necessary interfaces. No classified information or command credential shall be presumed transferable through a commercial sale.

(c) A transition-services agreement shall specify measurable service levels, price, duration, minimum staffing, support access, enforceable remedies and a funded exit plan. No deadline alone establishes technical independence or authorizes takeover of a supplier.

### SEC. 402. SECURITY AND CUTOVER GATES.

(a) Before each material transfer of operating control, independent qualified assessors shall witness tests of command integrity, identity and key separation, software-update authority, billing and customer support, emergency coordination, network monitoring, backup restoration, supplier failure and safe rollback. Tests shall use approved safety controls; live spacecraft actions require lawful operating authority.

(b) The Board and Secretary shall approve documented service tolerances, recovery objectives and measurable test criteria before testing, based on mission requirements and customer obligations. Failed criteria shall block the affected cutover until corrected and retested. No certification may be based solely on a vendor assertion or absence of observed incidents.

(c) A failed cutover shall trigger a funded, contractually and legally authorized recovery arrangement. Rollback may not be presumed to restore expired permits, return title automatically or compel a former owner's labor. If safe recovery is unavailable, the affected transfer shall not proceed.

### SEC. 403. FOREIGN PERMISSIONS AND CONTINUITY REGISTER.

The Secretary, in consultation with the Secretary of State and responsible regulators, shall maintain a jurisdiction-by-jurisdiction register identifying the legal operator, authorization, expiry, ownership/control consent, data duties, spectrum coordination, launch and registration obligations and outstanding conditions. The register shall distinguish verified approvals from pending requests and research leads. No country shall be marked ready solely because a U.S. statute has been enacted or a general foreign procedure exists. No operating transfer may precede the permissions required for that phase.

### SEC. 404. ALTERNATIVES, PROFESSIONAL REVIEW AND IMPLEMENTATION TIMETABLE.

(a) Within 270 days after enactment and before a binding acquisition commitment, the Secretary shall publish an independent comparison of public acquisition, negotiated long-term capacity and continuity contracts, multivendor redundancy, lawful regulatory conditions, competition measures and public construction alternatives. The comparison shall use common mission requirements, lifecycle costs, public rights, technical risk, international permissions and sensitivity analysis; disclose conflicts and dissent; and explain the route recommended to accomplish the public-ownership purposes of this Act lawfully and prudently. No independent reviewer is required to endorse acquisition.

(b) Before closing, independent constitutional, fiscal, compensation, telecom, foreign-law, engineering, privacy and financial reviewers shall provide written assessments and unresolved objections. Their opinions shall inform, but not replace, the Secretary's legal responsibility and judicial review. Classified and confidential annexes may accompany public conclusions under applicable law.

(c) Within 90 days after enactment the President shall submit Board nominations and the Secretary shall publish a due-diligence plan; within 180 days the budget and transition plan are due; within 270 days the alternatives report is due. Negotiation and permission applications may proceed concurrently where lawful. The section 104 closing conditions, 60-day congressional notice and section 105 negotiation minimum remain controlling. No calendar date requires a closing before those conditions are met.

(d) Annual reports shall assess public outcomes, costs, competition effects, rights complaints, service continuity and whether additional legislation is needed. Missing evidence and failed assumptions shall be reported expressly.

## TITLE V—REVIEW AND EFFECTIVE DATE

### SEC. 501. JUDICIAL REVIEW AND SAVINGS.

(a) Final agency action under this Act remains subject to applicable judicial review. In addition, the United States waives sovereign immunity for nonmonetary relief sought by a person adversely affected by an alleged violation of this Act, within the jurisdiction of the district courts, except where a specific statutory review route governs the challenged action. No new restriction on constitutional claims or compensation remedies is imposed.

(b) Nothing in this Act displaces a court's authority to prevent an unlawful acquisition, alters otherwise applicable FCC review procedures, waives a lawful privilege, or authorizes a taking without constitutionally adequate compensation.

### SEC. 502. SEVERABILITY WITH OPERATIONAL SAFETY.

If a provision or application is held invalid, the remainder shall continue to the extent legally and operationally practicable. No severability determination authorizes a transfer without compensation, required operating permissions, funded continuity, or the rights safeguards of this Act. The Secretary shall suspend any affected closing until the remaining lawful transaction satisfies section 104.

### SEC. 503. EFFECTIVE DATE.

This Act takes effect on enactment. No property or operating control transfers by enactment alone.
