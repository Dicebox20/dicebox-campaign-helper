## Finding

The public record distinguishes two instruments that the earlier gap report had not fully reconciled:

1. The 2 March 2026, $20 billion unsecured Bridge Loan agreement was repaid in full in June 2026 and reported as extinguished. Its covenant should not be presented as the presently outstanding bridge-loan restriction.
2. A separate revolving credit facility was amended and restated on 19 May 2026. The SEC-filed execution version is Exhibit 10.10 to the 1 June 2026 S-1/A. It contains a specific covenant restricting dispositions of Starlink and certain material Starlink assets, licenses and transactions that give a third party ownership or control. The issuer reported that this separate facility remained available at $5 billion, had no balance drawn at 30 June, and was due to mature on 19 May 2031.

Accordingly, the best public-source conclusion is that the Bridge Loan restriction is no longer the operative question, while the live revolving-facility text presents a material contractual issue for a transfer of Starlink or assets within its definitions. The text does not itself establish that a federal statutory acquisition would be legally impossible, identify the exact lender-consent path for that transaction, or determine which individual assets qualify. No transaction-specific consent, waiver or termination for a public acquisition was located in this bounded filing set.

## Current revolving-facility language relevant to the perimeter

The 19 May agreement defines “Starlink” to include borrower/subsidiary operations and assets related to developing, manufacturing and operating satellite constellations for high-speed broadband, and performance of government contracts related to satellite and satellite-infrastructure development.

“Starlink Financing Assets” are defined by reference to the borrower’s and subsidiaries’ rights, title, interests and obligations related to consumer broadband delivery. The enumerated classes include user terminals and related hardware (expressly excluding satellites), related accounts and fees, payment rights under royalty/licensing/subscription/servicing arrangements, Starlink intellectual property and reasonably necessary related IP, necessary regulatory approvals and licenses, related contracts, grants, subsidies and government awards, associated records and customer lists, and proceeds.

“Material Starlink Asset” reaches Starlink-related assets, including financing assets and IP, but excludes launch services. The definition uses an alternative test: the assets either generate more than 10% of consolidated borrower-and-subsidiary revenue for the specified measurement period or are otherwise material to Starlink’s operation. This is a lender-covenant definition; it is not an asset-title schedule, a legal finding that every listed asset is owned by SpaceX, or the statutory commercial-only acquisition perimeter.

Section 7.03(c) restricts, among other things:

- disposing of Starlink or a Material Starlink Asset to anyone other than the borrower or a subsidiary;
- disposing of, licensing or granting development rights in qualifying Starlink IP to such a third party;
- a Starlink spin-off unless Starlink and the Material Starlink Assets remain owned by the borrower or a subsidiary; and
- another transaction resulting in a third party owning or controlling Starlink or a Material Starlink Asset.

The clause states an exception for a transaction of a type permitted by §§7.02(s) and/or (t). Those provisions concern specified debt connected with wireless-spectrum assets and data centers/GPU assets. The public text reviewed does not establish that the exception covers a statutory public acquisition, and no such application is assumed here.

## What the record supports about consent and present status

The May 2026 restatement is executed in form and textually contains a restriction directed at third-party ownership or control of Starlink/material Starlink assets. The Form 10-Q reports the separate revolver continued to be available, with $5 billion of borrowing capacity and no amount drawn at 30 June. Zero drawn principal is not evidence that the commitment was terminated or that its covenants were waived. The 10-Q says the company was in compliance with the facility covenants at that date.

For transaction planning, the draft Act’s implementation record should therefore require a closing-stage schedule of outstanding credit commitments, covenant text, exceptions, amendments and waivers, together with a documented path to resolve any restriction before transfer. Depending on transaction structure and the governing documents, that may require a negotiated amendment/waiver, termination or repayment of the facility, or a legally reviewed conclusion that the covenant does not reach the transaction. This report does not select among those paths.

The contract provision does not, by itself, answer:

- whether a particular statutory taking or congressional transfer is a “transaction,” “Disposition,” or transfer of control under the full agreement;
- whether the United States or a public corporation is treated as a covered third-party “Person” for each relevant definition;
- which lender or agent approvals, if any, would be required for a specific acquisition structure;
- whether separate asset-level securitizations, receivables arrangements, liens, leases, licenses, government contracts, or regulatory approvals affect the same asset; or
- what remedies would apply or whether federal acquisition law supersedes, modifies, or compensates contractual rights.

The section’s broad third-party-control wording makes these issues material; resolving them requires the complete current loan-document set and qualified transaction counsel. No inference about lien perfection, ownership, fair value, or a constitutional remedy follows from this review.
