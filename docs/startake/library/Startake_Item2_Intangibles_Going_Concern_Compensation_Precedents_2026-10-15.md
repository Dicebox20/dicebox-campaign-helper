## Legal question and bounded findings

Which federal compensation precedents caution against treating every anticipated profit, lost business opportunity, contract right, and transferable going-concern asset as a single Starlink purchase price? The question of whether Congress could authorize an acquisition, and the separate task of proving asset-specific title, are **not resolved** by these valuation decisions.

| Evidence ID and judgment | Supported proposition and specific holding | Limitation for hypothetical Starlink acquisition |
|---|---|---|
| C-01 [*Omnia Commercial Co. v. United States*, 261 U.S. 502 (1923)](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/261/502/) | Contractual rights can constitute Fifth Amendment property. But the government requisitioned steel production; the buyer's separate supply contract became impossible to perform without the government **appropriating that contract**. Resulting economic frustration was not automatically compensable as a taking. | Need contract-by-contract identification of the exact right taken versus consequential loss. Do not presume every unfulfilled Starlink customer/vendor contract or reduced expected profit is directly compensable. This decision does not establish Starlink contract valuations. |
| C-02 [*United States v. General Motors Corp.*, 323 U.S. 373 (1945)](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/323/373/) | Temporary wartime government possession of leased warehouse premises calls for market rental value of the particular **temporary** occupancy. Necessary moving/preparation expenses could be evidence affecting its market value; destroyed fixtures could be separately compensable, without treating general business loss or goodwill as automatic additional awards. | Distinguish temporary transition seizure, permanent asset acquisition, fixtures/equipment and consequential business losses. A temporary warehouse occupancy is not a direct pricing formula for orbital communications or licenses. |
| C-03 [*United States v. Petty Motor Co.*, 327 U.S. 372 (1946)](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/327/372/) | In the relevant lease condemnation, the Court distinguished temporary partial occupancy in General Motors from taking the entire remaining lease term; relocation expenses were not automatically compensable as valuation evidence in the latter situation. A lease condemnation-termination clause affected whether the tenant retained any compensable interest. | Specific terms of customer, lease, supply and financing rights matter. Neither a blanket moving-cost claim nor a blanket exclusion is justified for an unformed Starlink transaction. |
| C-04 [*Kimball Laundry Co. v. United States*, 338 U.S. 1 (1949)](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/338/1/) | The Court required examination of **transferable going-concern value of temporarily preempted trade routes** in a government occupancy, and expressly discussed public utility acquisitions where the taker acquires going-concern value. It remanded for proof of presence and value, **not** an assumed dollar award. | Strong caveat against saying goodwill can never be compensable; equally, past revenue and subscribers do not prove transferable goodwill. Need to distinguish business continued by the taker, temporary disruptions, ownership of customer relationships, exclusivity, market alternatives and double counting. |

**Cross-case rule of caution:** A recognized property interest and a governmental taking must both be proven before constitutional compensation analysis; market value of the interest actually taken generally differs from all losses suffered. Intangibles/going-concern value may matter in particular settings, while merely frustrated contracts and consequential business losses may not. No case above specifically authorizes taking Starlink or establishes a present-day just-compensation sum.

## Source index, method, and opposing considerations

The four citations point to published full U.S. Supreme Court opinions reproduced by Justia; the Court, docket, reporter citations, and holdings are identified for reproducibility. These are judicial primary-source **texts as reproduced**, not a certified federal reporter pull. Two particularly important opposing inferences: *Omnia* recognizes contract property but denied recovery on indirect frustration; *Kimball Laundry* permits valuation of demonstrable transferable going-concern value in a particular temporary seizure rather than excluding it categorically. No case-specific review of all subsequent controlling Federal Circuit precedents, statutory compensation supplements, sovereign licensing, or current telecom franchise rules was undertaken. No privileged counsel analysis obtained.
