## Preliminary findings extracted from the completed legal report

All findings below are **report-level syntheses**, with official source leads where available. They are **not marked independently checked** by this intake. Citations and source conclusions require an item-by-item primary-source audit before satisfying a checklist completion gate.

| Research domain | Preliminary synthesis from report | Needed verification / disposition |
|---|---|---|
| Congressional authorization | A permanent compelled acquisition most defensibly rests on Starlink-specific legislation, budget authority, acquisition procedures, compensation, and operational governance, rather than a unilateral presidential nationalization order. | Counsel to review congressional power, separation of powers, and remedies in the controlling legal text and cases. |
| Just compensation and affected interests | The owner of each property interest, not an assumed individual owner, is the potential compensation claimant; asset acquisitions, subsidiary equity, and rights acquisitions differ. | Obtain entity chart, title chains, lien and security interests, intercompany licenses, shareholder/creditor data, and audited valuation evidence. |
| Condemnation procedure | Report cautions against treating 40 U.S.C. § 3113, generally directed to real-property acquisition, as blanket authorization for satellite-business assets. | Verify text and relevant statutory history; analyze bespoke legislation and judicial procedures. |
| FCC and satellite licensing | Spectrum is regulated, not a privately owned frequency asset. Transfer or change of control of relevant satellite authorizations would need FCC process and other continuing obligations. | Build license-by-license FCC inventory, Part 25 review, public-interest transfer process, and foreign licensing crosswalk. |
| Emergency authorities | Communications Act emergency authority and Defense Production Act tools are not interchangeable with permanent, compensated public acquisition. | Analyze 47 U.S.C. § 606, applicable Defense Production Act authorities, thresholds, duration, due process and remedies. |
| Assets vs shared dependencies | A separable Starlink perimeter likely involves spacecraft, gateways, terminals, software, network operations, employees, and contractual rights, alongside shared SpaceX launch/manufacturing resources. | No complete public asset schedule recovered. Verify entities, IP and leases, data rights, shared staff, suppliers, and continuity contracts before valuation. |
| Starshield and security | Military/classified systems have distinct national-security, export-control, service and operating boundaries. | Do not assume inclusion; verify interfaces and separation under appropriate access controls. |
| Public support and procurement | The report warns not to equate government procurement or award ceilings with cash subsidies, or attribute all SpaceX support to Starlink. | Build transaction-by-transaction ledger segregating Starlink and wider SpaceX, grants/awards/obligations/disbursements, and multi-vendor ceilings. |
| International continuity | Outer Space Treaty state responsibility, registration, space-object liability, ITU obligations, and individual host-state approvals remain relevant after any U.S. ownership change. | Map treaties, administrations, foreign market-access and registrations, cross-border privacy and diplomacy. |
| Ukraine and critical communications | The report describes simultaneous civilian/humanitarian and military reliance and a potential continuity risk where a private operator can change service parameters. | Independently verify each reported incident, timeline, service scope, named decision maker, government contract, and counterevidence. |
| Public governance and alternatives | Suggested structures include independent public authority, transparent service rules and review, with an assessment of public-utility regulation, government contracts and redundancy as alternatives. | Formally compare necessity, fiscal burden, political-abuse risk, cybersecurity, innovation, diplomatic and constitutional downsides. |
