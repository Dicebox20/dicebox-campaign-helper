## Principal finding

The Uniform Relocation Assistance and Real Property Acquisition Policies Act (URAA), 42 USC chapter 61, provides conditional **real-property acquisition policies** and **assistance to displaced people and businesses** in covered federal/federally assisted projects. The relevant action and property interest must first be identified. The Act **does not create a new power to acquire Starlink**, does not automatically vest ground-station leases, and does not set compensation for all satellites, data, patents, shares, radio authorizations or customer contracts. A proposed purchase of a corporation's equity or an existing operation without displacement is **not automatically the same event** as federal acquisition of land that displaces tenants or businesses.

The law matters because a commercial-Starlink carve-out could implicate **gateway real estate, leased antenna pads, shelters/buildings, tenants' improvements, access easements, owners, relocation of a functioning business and independent site landlords**. But there is **no reliable Starlink parcel/lease schedule, transaction-specific displacing agency, U.S. site selection, notice, appraisal or relocation plan** in this pass. No conclusion is reached that an identifiable Starlink owner or tenant has a statutory payment claim now.

## Transaction decision matrix / contrary interpretations

| Illustrative transaction | Potential URAA gate | Contrary case / what must be proven |
|---|---|---|
| Congress-authorized direct purchase/condemnation of U.S. gateway land | Identify acquiring federal agency, §4651 policy, title/interests, offer/appraisal; if occupants are displaced evaluate 42 USC §§4622/4625 and 49 CFR Part 24. | Ownership of site and any private landlord/tenant rights unknown; a gateway license identifies a regulatory holder, **not necessarily owner of land**. |
| Purchase of stock in Starlink-related operating entity with no site move | URAA rights are not automatically triggered merely by a change in equity ownership. | A contemporaneous federal project that actually acquires real estate or displaces occupants may separately trigger the law; fact-specific review required. |
| Assign existing gateway lease and continue operation in place | Negotiate landlord consent and check actual real-property acquisition and displacement consequences. | Assignment of leases or contracts does not itself prove anyone displaced; private lease terms and location-specific law remain material. |
| Move operating gateway antenna/shelter, evict tenants or force site closure | Possible acquisition of tenant improvements under §4652 and business moving/reestablishment benefits if eligibility is satisfied. | Government must establish causal covered displacement, correctly identify each claimant, and avoid double-counting asset value and relocation costs. |
| Acquire overseas gateway land or foreign operating permissions | Need host-country property, expropriation, lease and relocation rules. | **Do not export U.S. URAA obligations or assume U.S. legislation vests foreign land/permits**; not researched under this domestic pass. |

**Adverse/limiting interpretation preserved:** URAA's §4651 is phrased as policies guiding agencies **to the greatest extent practicable**, whereas eligibility/benefit rules are statute- and fact-specific. Do not present every §4651 policy as an automatic independent damages remedy. Conversely, the limited statutory language is **not** grounds to ignore actual displaced businesses, landlords or tenant-owned improvements if the transaction involves federal land acquisition. A federal condemnation procedure still requires separate substantive acquisition authority and Fifth Amendment compensation.
