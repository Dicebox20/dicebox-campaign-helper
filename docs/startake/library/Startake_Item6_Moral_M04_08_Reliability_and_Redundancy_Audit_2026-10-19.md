## Executive result

The federal framework reviewed contains a conditional satellite-outage reporting regime, not a general uptime, recovery-time, or backup-route guarantee for every commercial satellite-broadband customer. Under 47 C.F.R. § 4.9(c), some satellite-operator failures and satellite-communications-provider outages trigger rapid FCC notice plus initial and final outage reports. The covered event definitions are specific, and § 4.9(c)(5) excludes listed satellite facilities and networks used only for private, video, or other non-covered services that are never used to carry common-carrier voice or paging. The interaction between the rule's broad references to paid two-way data and that voice/paging carve-out makes application to any particular broadband-only system fact-dependent. No Starlink service, network use, or classification was established here.

Sections 214(a) and 214(d) of the Communications Act create conditional controls over a carrier's discontinuance, reduction, or impairment of service to a community and permit the FCC, after process and findings, to order adequate facilities. They do not state a generally applicable uptime percentage or a standing diverse-route obligation. Section 254(b)(1)'s quality-services principle informs universal-service policy; it is not itself a per-provider technical reliability specification.

Bill v1.14 adds meaningful transition safeguards: funded and executable continuity arrangements at closing, transition planning, measurable transition service schedules, and tested restoration and independently supplied alternatives for registered critical services before a material control transfer. These controls are tied to closing, a transition-services agreement, or cutover gates. They do not on their face set ongoing minimum reliability or redundancy duties for every retail service after transfer. Section 205(a) requires published service terms and outage notices, but does not prescribe a service-availability target or require an independent backup path. This distinction is an inference from the provisions' text and temporal triggers.

No bill edit or formal-acceptance change is recommended from this bounded pass. Any future choice to create a standing reliability duty should define covered services and entities, measurable service conditions, outage and restoration reporting, independent-path criteria, exceptions, oversight, and lawful funding. No numeric target can be supported by this record.

## Current federal framework

### FCC Part 4: conditional satellite outage reporting

The current eCFR text displayed Title 47 as current through 15 October 2026 when retrieved on 19 October. The eCFR is the continuously updated online version and is authoritative but unofficial; the official legal publication is the CFR.

Part 4 defines a “communications provider” to include an entity that provides fee-based two-way voice and/or data communications to unaffiliated entities by radio, wire, cable, satellite, or lightguide. It separately describes satellite communications providers and satellite operators, including operators that do not directly provide service to end users. “Outage” is a significant degradation in an end user's ability to establish and maintain a communications channel caused by a provider-network failure or degraded performance. These definitions make the identity of the operator, provider, facilities, and service use relevant. [47 C.F.R. §§ 4.3, 4.5](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/section-4.3); [§ 4.5](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/section-4.5).

For satellite systems within the rule's covered scope:

- A failure lasting at least 30 minutes of a satellite transponder, satellite beam, inter-satellite link, or entire satellite triggers an electronic Commission notification within 120 minutes after discovery. Mobile-satellite operators have a corresponding gateway-earth-station trigger, with an exception where another gateway station restores operations within 30 minutes.
- A satellite communications provider has additional 30-minute outage triggers: complete loss of access to at least one satellite or transponder; a satellite-link loss potentially affecting at least 900,000 user-minutes of telephony or paging; or an outage potentially affecting a 911 or 988 special facility. The rules provide for notification of affected 911/988 facilities in the specified circumstances.
- The operator/provider must submit an initial FCC outage report within 72 hours and a final report within 30 days. Reports are electronic, and § 4.11 requires pertinent information then available; the provider-authorized final-report signatory must be empowered to bind the provider to the report's truth, completeness, and accuracy.
- Section 4.9(c)(5) excludes identified satellite equipment and networks used exclusively for private intra-organizational communications, one-way video/audio distribution, or other non-covered services—described in the rule as services never used to carry common-carrier voice or paging communications.

[47 C.F.R. § 4.9(c)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/section-4.9); [§ 4.11](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/section-4.11).

There is a scope question for an internet-only satellite network. Section 4.3(b) facially includes paid two-way data communications, while § 4.9(c)(5) defines the stated non-covered-services carve-out by reference to whether facilities ever carry common-carrier voice or paging. This audit does not resolve that interaction for a real provider. The exact services carried, facilities used, and legal classification require fact-specific review; no assumption of either coverage or exemption is made here.

Part 4's satellite provisions establish reporting triggers and deadlines. They do not themselves prescribe a customer-facing uptime percentage, a general restoration deadline, a minimum number of spare satellites or gateways, or a diverse backup path. That conclusion is an inference from the cited rule text; a specific license, program, contract, or other rule could add obligations not examined in this bounded pass.

### Confidentiality and public-evidence limits

Outage reports filed under Part 4 are presumed confidential under § 4.2, subject to specified government-agency access and safeguards. The FCC explains that NORS reports help staff identify communications vulnerabilities and coordinate with providers on reliability, security, resiliency, and readiness. This is an agency oversight and learning mechanism; it is not itself an enforceable per-customer service-level or restoration requirement. [47 C.F.R. § 4.2](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/section-4.2); FCC, [FCC 12-22](https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/FCC-12-22A1.pdf).

Because the underlying NORS filings are presumed confidential, public-source research cannot independently establish whether a provider filed a specific report, what its root cause was, or whether its restoration work was adequate. No absence of a located public report is treated as evidence that an outage or filing did not exist.

### Communications Act provisions

Section 214(a) provides that a covered carrier may not discontinue, reduce, or impair service to a community or part of a community without first obtaining the required FCC certificate, subject to the statute's exceptions. Section 214(d), after a proceeding and opportunity for hearing, authorizes the FCC to order a carrier to provide adequate facilities for expeditious and efficient common-carrier service when statutory findings are met. These provisions can support case-specific continuity oversight for a covered carrier; they do not specify a universal uptime floor or automatic technical recovery plan. Applicability depends on the covered carrier/service and facts, and is not resolved here. [47 U.S.C. § 214(a), (d)](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/119-120/t47/s214/).

Section 254(b)(1) states that quality services should be available at just, reasonable, and affordable rates as a principle for universal-service policies. That policy informs Commission and Joint Board action under § 254; it does not set a specific provider-level satellite uptime, redundancy, restoration, or failover standard. The M04.07 report separately analyzes § 254's access and affordability dimensions. [47 U.S.C. § 254(b)(1)](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/119-120/t47/s254/).

## Findings, contrary considerations, and unresolved evidence

1. **Part 4 supplies a narrow but meaningful satellite reporting duty.** Covered failures of major satellite elements receive short notification windows. The rules also permit the FCC to collect a more detailed initial and final account and notify certain covered emergency facilities. They are not merely voluntary industry practices.
2. **Coverage is conditional and the data-only edge is unresolved here.** Part 4's definitions and the satellite carve-out should be applied to the actual provider, communications offered, facilities, and uses. The FCC's specific service/entity classification or an authoritative interpretation may be needed; the M04.09 report's separate fixed/mobile satellite common-carriage analysis is not repeated.
3. **Reporting is not reliability performance.** The reviewed federal rules do not set a standing customer uptime, recovery-time, or independent-network-path obligation for every commercial satellite broadband offer. Section 214 may control service discontinuance or support a Commission facilities order in a covered case; § 254's quality principle is programmatic. Neither supplies an all-purpose technical standard.
4. **The Bill is strongest during acquisition and transition.** Sections 104, 401, and 402 require funding, readiness, measures, tests, backup restoration, alternatives, and correction gates. Their express triggers and covered population are transition-linked. Section 205(a) improves publication and outage notices without defining minimum reliability. Whether that is sufficient is a legislative choice requiring service/mission data, not a conclusion this report can make.
5. **Public evidence cannot establish actual performance.** No private NORS data, provider network telemetry, outage/root-cause history, restoration records, failover tests, supplier-dependency map, customer contracts, mission tolerances, FCC license conditions, or independent engineering opinion was reviewed. No current Starlink outage, reliability level, redundancy, noncompliance, or service harm is asserted.
