## Executive finding

A negotiated sale of business assets, acquisition of corporate stock and involuntary condemnation can result in different U.S. income-tax consequences. **Section 1001** supplies general amount-realized/adjusted-basis gain and loss concepts; **section 1033** permits specified conditional nonrecognition for property involuntarily converted through seizure, condemnation or related threats when replacement requirements are satisfied; **section 1060** imposes purchase-price allocation for an *applicable asset acquisition*, with reporting under Treasury regulations and IRS Form 8594. None is substantive authority for federal nationalization, an automatic tax exemption for government purchases, or proof of the tax treatment of any actual Starlink transaction.

## Adverse and alternative readings

A bare statement that 'government condemnation is tax free' is false: §1033 provides conditions for nonrecognition and may still recognize gain above replacement cost. Likewise, 'the entire purchase price is goodwill' lacks support when tangible satellites, terminals, gateways, intangibles, liabilities and rights require separate identification and potentially §1060 allocations. Conversely, do not assume §1060 or Form 8594 mechanically applies to every condemnation or statutory vesting; the applicability test must be established. Whether government compensation is constitutionally sufficient and whether sellers owe income taxes are analytically separate questions. Tax costs can affect negotiation, but no verified acquisition price or tax estimate follows from the source set.
