## Model-input eligibility and request exhibit

The following are proposed due-diligence requirements, not assertions that records exist or have been supplied. An accountable public transaction team would need to obtain and have qualified reviewers authenticate them before actual model population or cutover.

| Model/transition field | Missing evidence | Required reconciliation or test |
|---|---|---|
| Opening cohorts and remaining life | Object/vintage/generation register, commissioned status, forecast failure/retirement, maintenance history | Match serial/object IDs to operational status; engineering-supported remaining life per cohort; separate test, spare, failed and excluded assets |
| Target operating units | Service geography/capacity obligations by generation, orbit/inclination and season | Convert capacity to justified service-equivalent units or use separate generation submodels; test regional and intra-year coverage rather than only year-end counts |
| Manufactured units and unit cost | Executed build/supply agreement, bill of materials, yields, constrained components, tooling/software rights, production slots and staffing | Reconcile cash payments/deposits versus capitalized costs and opex; allocate commercial-only shared functions; audit lead times and acceptance rights |
| Purchased launches and slots | Priced capacity reservations, vehicle/version, mass/volume/dispenser/interface constraints, orbit, schedule, insurance and remedies | Qualified payload integration and deployment tests; distinguish nominal capacity, flight-test deployment, commissioned service and legally enforceable availability |
| Launch losses and commissioning delay | Actual failure/insurance history, orbit-raising and acceptance records, contingency ranges | Sensitivity tests at monthly/mission level; avoid treating one successful flight as a reliability distribution |
| Alternate supply and resilience | Technically qualified substitute provider/design and executable commercial terms, spares and continuity plan | Demonstrate compatibility, permits and contracted fallback; do not infer substitution from another provider's advertised payload mass |
| Other capex and operating costs | Ground/terminal refresh, retirement/deorbit, software, training and support costs | Exclude launch/manufacturing double counting, distinguish cash/accrual and accounting classifications, reconcile to approved perimeter |
| Terminal state | Sustainable replacement cohorts and capacity/cost economics beyond year10 | Test steady-state continuation and severe supply interruption; no assumed permanent cash generation from finite-life hardware |

Analytical inference: retaining operational contracts with an existing supplier could support a transition without buying its launch business. The reviewed sources neither prove that arrangement attainable nor rule it out. Conversely, an integrated operator's demonstrated technical success does not prove that separable rights, staff, production and future capacity can be conveyed on acceptable terms. A public-ownership decision needs enforceable evidence at the proposed transaction boundary.
