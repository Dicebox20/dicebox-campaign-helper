## Question and authenticated scope

The authenticated M04.03 scope is **Humanitarian relief operations**: “Aid organizations, displaced people, logistics, and field coordination.” It is in the moral topic “Civilian Life, Emergency Response, and Humanitarian Dependence.” The source is member `starlink_moral_research_topics/04_civilian_life_emergency_response_and_humanitarian_dependence.md` in `Starlink_Moral_Foundation_Research_Topics_19.zip`; archive Library ID `libfile_d51e095c82b88191807ddb3d6f4bdcfd`, 35,389 bytes, SHA-256 `26ab27f27c0099f19ee0ac32d02e3245ba32555196d001fd4b60756fac66c8e0`; member SHA-256 `c86af941a2c78f1ae163f7b3c502bf9d250c514a59cb687c0b5cb15a32de10fd`.

The question is what U.S. federal programs and authorities coordinate or support voluntary relief organizations, displaced-person assistance, relief logistics, and field coordination; whether reviewed law or policy creates an enforceable communications-continuity, access, or restoration duty for a commercial satellite-broadband provider serving those operations; and how the evidence compares with proposed Bill v1.14 §§104(3), 205(d), 401(d), and 402(d)–(e).

The review is limited to public federal statutes, FEMA response-framework and training materials, and the cited proposed Bill text. It does not assess a particular provider, relief organization, emergency, service contract, site, subscriber, service classification, TSP assignment, network performance, outage, or restoration. It does not establish actual Starlink use or coverage. No particular State, Tribal, local, or foreign jurisdiction is assessed.

## Finding

The reviewed federal framework contains **targeted humanitarian-assistance, relief-organization coordination, and damaged-facility support authorities**. In the bounded source set, I did not identify a generally applicable federal communications-continuity, uptime, priority, or restoration duty that applies to a commercial satellite-broadband provider merely because a voluntary relief organization or displaced population depends on connectivity during a disaster.

The authorities principally authorize or coordinate government resources and voluntary participation. Stafford Act §5152 allows the President to use specified relief organizations’ personnel and facilities **with their consent** and permits agreements under which a Federal coordinating officer may coordinate their relief activities. Section 5170b authorizes Federal agencies, on the President’s direction, to provide emergency assistance through relief organizations and to State/local governments and qualifying private nonprofit facilities. These are not general commands to communications providers.

Two narrower mechanisms matter. First, Stafford Act §5172 permits contributions toward repair or restoration of certain disaster-damaged public or private nonprofit facilities; qualifying critical services include communications, broadcast, and telecommunications. This is conditional facility assistance, not a provider service-level obligation. Second, §5174 authorizes specified financial or direct housing assistance to eligible individuals and households, including some who are displaced. Its temporary-rental utility clause expressly excludes telephone service, but it does not address Internet access by name; this review does not extend that wording to satellite broadband or infer that other programs cannot support connectivity.

FEMA’s National Response Framework and mass-care materials describe coordination among governments, voluntary organizations, nonprofits, and the private sector. They supply an operational coordination model, not a generally enforceable uptime or restoration standard for a commercial satellite provider. A FEMA-hosted detailed ESF #6 annex reviewed here is marked January 2008; its current operational currency was not independently confirmed. FEMA’s IS-405.A course, dated 17 December 2025, describes whole-community collaboration and coordination as training guidance, not binding law.

The prior M04.02 Disaster Response audit remains the source for general disaster communications-restoration mechanisms, including Stafford Act §5189e and FEMA ESF #2. Section 5189e’s operative no-impediment/access duty is on Federal agency heads for qualifying essential-service providers contributing to a response; it does not require the provider to meet an uptime or restoration target. This report does not repeat the M04.02 TSP, MDRI, DIRS, or general restoration analysis.

### Disposition

**No Bill text change is recommended from this evidence pass.** The existing draft can register and test a contracted humanitarian-relief communication service where the risk test is met, and its access baseline can measure emergency-service and other rural/remote customer conditions. Neither mechanism creates a general continuing service regime for voluntary relief groups or displaced people after cutover.

If the policy goal is to guarantee an ongoing corporate role in disaster relief, legislative counsel and emergency-management operators should decide whether to name qualifying humanitarian organizations, shelters, distribution/logistics hubs, or displaced-person service sites; define activation, eligibility, priority, measurable service/restoration standards, funding, interagency coordination, exceptions, and oversight. This report supplies no operational dataset or cost estimate to set those terms. The question is a policy-design choice, not a conclusion that current law is unlawful or that any provider has failed.

## Contrary considerations and material limits

- Stafford Act §§5152 and 5170b expressly recognize relief organizations as participants or channels for Federal assistance; §5172 includes communications among critical services for certain repair contributions. These provisions support relief coordination and repair but do not require service by every commercial provider.
- A relief organization may have a specific Federal, State, Tribal, local, or donor contract, grant, mission assignment, or service agreement that changes the analysis. No such instrument or actual operation was supplied.
- TSP may impose restoration priority for a qualifying, authorized, assigned NSEP service, and M04.02 separately reviewed TSP and other communications mechanisms. No humanitarian organization or service was shown to hold such an assignment.
- A FEMA resource-typing search excerpt for a pedestrian distribution team mentions considering satellite phones according to mission/team needs. The full resource page was not retrievable during this pass, and a satellite phone is not satellite broadband; that excerpt is not relied on as a provider duty or universal operational practice.
- No specific Starlink service classification, customer, incident, disaster, outage, location, subscription, coverage, field use, terminal, or contract is established. No Legal Data Hunter verification or legal, emergency-management, public-safety, engineering, provider, or legislative-counsel review is represented.
