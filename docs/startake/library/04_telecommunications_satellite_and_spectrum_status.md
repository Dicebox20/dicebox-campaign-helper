## Initial findings and status (October 14, 2026)

This document incorporates the findings reported in the Topic Four Deep Research session and links to relevant primary source records. **This synchronization is not a second independent audit of every cited filing, license identifier or court interpretation.** Distinguish reported documentary findings from complete, verified inventories before legal drafting.

- **No single transferable Starlink license:** constellation authorizations, gateways, wireless/D2D spectrum, STAs, ITU coordination and foreign market permission form a multi-layered regulatory portfolio.
- **Transition issue:** 47 USC §310(d) commercial-license control review must be reconciled with the federal-station regime under 47 USC §305 and NTIA authority. The optimal successor legal form is not yet settled.
- **International issue:** U.S. jurisdiction and FCC authority do not themselves secure country-level permissions or ITU rights held through other notifying administrations.
- **Open evidence:** complete FCC ICFS/ULS authorization inventory, ITU network/priority register and country-by-country operating rights register.
- **Scope:** Starlink communications services alone; SpaceX's unrelated operations and Starshield remain excluded except for documented shared dependencies.

### 04.01 — Satellite space-station licensing

FCC authorizations for orbital spacecraft networks and changes in operation or ownership.

**Evidence to develop:** Legal instruments, factual records, attributable figures, relevant technical information, and competing interpretations specific to this subject.

**First Deep Research pass — findings (2026-10-14):** The retrieved FCC orders identify separate satellite authorizations rather than a single transferable “Starlink license.” The original Ku/Ka constellation was approved in 2018; an important Gen1 modification followed in 2021; an initial 7,500 Gen2 satellites were partially authorized in 2022; and the January 2026 decision approved a further 7,500 Gen2 satellites. Core satellite authorizations are associated with Space Exploration Holdings, LLC. The complete active authorization inventory, grant conditions, renewals, milestones and successors are **not yet assembled**. Sources: [FCC 18-38](https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/FCC-18-38A1_Rcd.pdf); [FCC 21-48 announcement](https://www.fcc.gov/document/fcc-grants-spacexs-satellite-broadband-modification-application); [FCC 22-91](https://www.fcc.gov/document/fcc-partially-grants-spacex-gen2-broadband-satellite-application); [January 2026 FCC action](https://www.fcc.gov/document/fcc-approves-next-gen-satellite-constellation).


### 04.02 — Earth stations and gateways

Ground radio licenses, gateway filings, terrestrial stations and operational continuity.

**Evidence to develop:** Legal instruments, factual records, attributable figures, relevant technical information, and competing interpretations specific to this subject.

### 04.03 — Radiofrequency spectrum rights

Bands, sharing arrangements, interference obligations and spectrum-related contract rights.

**Evidence to develop:** Legal instruments, factual records, attributable figures, relevant technical information, and competing interpretations specific to this subject.

**First Deep Research pass — findings (2026-10-14):** FCC materials identify Starlink Ku user links approximately at 10.7–12.7 GHz down / 14.0–14.5 GHz up; Ka gateway links including 17.8–18.6 and 18.8–19.3 GHz down and 27.5–29.1 / 29.5–30.0 GHz up; V-band around 37.5–42.0 and 47.2–50.2 GHz; and Gen2 E-band 71–76 GHz down / 81–86 GHz up. These ranges should be checked against the precise grants and waivers, not treated as blanket exclusive rights. Radiofrequencies themselves are **not private fee-simple property**. Sources: [FCC 18-38](https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/FCC-18-38A1_Rcd.pdf); [DA 24-222](https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/DA-24-222A1.pdf); [47 USC §§304, 316](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&path=%2Fprelim%40title47%2Fchapter5%2Fsubchapter3).


### 04.04 — FCC ownership and transfer controls

Assignment, transfer of control and applicable public-interest findings.

**Evidence to develop:** Legal instruments, factual records, attributable figures, relevant technical information, and competing interpretations specific to this subject.

**First Deep Research pass — findings (2026-10-14):** 47 USC §310(d) generally requires FCC consent and a public-interest assessment before an assignment or transfer of control of station licenses. 47 CFR §25.119 governs most satellite-station transactions; some wireless permits have separate Part 1 procedures. Section 310(a) addresses *foreign governments*, not a general ban on ownership by the U.S. government. A purchase of satellites alone would not transfer communications permission automatically. Sources: [47 USC §310](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&num=0&req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title47-section310); [47 CFR §25.119](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-25/subpart-B/subject-group-ECFRe2bd70fc6b2eea0/section-25.119).


### 04.05 — Federal spectrum administration

NTIA authority, federal radio operations and interactions with FCC commercial licensing.

**Evidence to develop:** Legal instruments, factual records, attributable figures, relevant technical information, and competing interpretations specific to this subject.

**First Deep Research pass — findings (2026-10-14):** Government stations “belonging to and operated by the United States” invoke 47 USC §305 and federal frequency administration through the President/NTIA, potentially changing how a directly federally operated Starlink would receive spectrum authority. The application of §305 to a specially chartered federally owned corporation with commercial subsidiaries is **legally unresolved for this hypothetical structure**. Congress would need a carefully designed FCC/NTIA transition framework. Sources: [47 USC §305](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&num=0&req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title47-section305); [NTIA: Who Regulates the Spectrum](https://www.ntia.gov/book-page/who-regulates-spectrum).

### 04.08 — Mobile, direct-to-cell and partner spectrum

**Evidence to develop:** Legal instruments, factual records, attributable figures, relevant technical information, and competing interpretations specific to this subject.

### 04.12 — Government-owned station conversion under 47 USC §305

Examine the precise point at which acquired radio stations would “belong to and [be] operated by the United States,” which assets/operators would become federal spectrum users, how NTIA assignments could be issued, and whether a congressionally chartered corporation can preserve commercial operations.

**First Deep Research pass:** The §305/FCC–NTIA interface is a core structural issue. Treat hybrid-corporation treatment as an **inference requiring legal memorandum**, not as settled law. ### 04.13 — License succession, interim continuity and transaction mechanics

Analyze corporate share transfers, asset assignments, transfer-of-control applications, FCC Form 312/Schedule A as appropriate, Part 25 vs wireless licensing, closing conditions, consummation, special temporary authorizations and successor arrangements.

**First Deep Research pass:** An ordinary corporate control transaction and a direct agency acquisition may have different regulatory consequences. Statutory rule waivers cannot be presumed to override §305 or §310. Preserve operation of the existing licensees until valid successor authorities exist. Sources: [47 USC §310(d)](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&num=0&req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title47-section310); [47 CFR §25.119](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-25/subpart-B/subject-group-ECFRe2bd70fc6b2eea0/section-25.119).
