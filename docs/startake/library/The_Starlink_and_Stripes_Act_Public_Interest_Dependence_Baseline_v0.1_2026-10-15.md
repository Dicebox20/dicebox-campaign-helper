## Executive proposal

Congress should pursue a fairly compensated acquisition of the commercial Starlink communications undertaking into a wholly public operator, subject to a proved commercial perimeter, funded transaction, valid permissions and tested continuity. The public-interest objective is durable access under accountable rules: transparent interruption decisions, enforceable privacy and speech safeguards, affordable service for underserved users and continuity across political administrations. This is the project's proposed policy judgment, not a conclusion demonstrated by satellite counts alone.

The evidence supports substantial global use and identifiable emergency, medical and transport applications. The company reported 12.0 million Starlink subscribers at 30 June 2026 [S2]. An independent orbital census dated 12 October reports 11,114 active Starlinks, roughly 65.4% of its global active-payload count [S1]. Neither measure is a US broadband market share, a count of unique people, proof of monopoly over all communications, or a title schedule for assets Congress can acquire.

Public ownership can change the authority making service decisions; it cannot itself eliminate equipment failures, orbital hazards, inadequate local power, foreign restrictions or executive political interference. The proposal therefore combines ownership with a constrained charter, remedies, multivendor continuity, independent audit and democratic oversight. Existing integrated bill §§201–208 and 401–404 supply working mechanisms; this report proposes evidence-backed refinements below. Acquisition necessity and cost effectiveness remain open until comparison on equivalent service objectives, scope, timing and lifecycle cost is complete.

## Sector evidence and limits

**Remote communities and education.** UK BDUK records Starlink trials at the Ogwen Valley rescue base and Cornel Scout Centre, supporting remote safety and educational activities. It also records OneWeb at Papa Stour and Lundy Island [S7]. These are specific locations, not a census of dependent households or regular schools. The same government's school connectivity evaluation concerns a wider gigabit-hub program; it reports enabled digital services but does not establish direct pupil-outcome effects [S8]. It is useful contrary evidence that public connectivity benefits can be pursued through other infrastructure. No Starlink attribution of that entire program is made.

**Health and clinical continuity.** NHS England describes Starlink connectivity for school-nursing access to the NHS network at a Lancaster special-education school, a speech/language centre and a remote user. During a November 2024 primary-network failure, satellite access supported the trust command room. Deployment required site surveys, secure tunnels and roof access; planned mobile-unit work did not proceed because of space and stakeholder constraints [S6]. These support use-specific transition testing rather than a presumption of instant installation. The source's anomalous trust staff figure is not relied on. Ufopa's original CTIC report records a maritime Starlink terminal for the Abaré hospital-school vessel's remote telehealth needs [S9]. This confirms a particular installation purpose, not that every consultation depended on Starlink or that a health benefit size was measured.

**Emergency response.** NSW's August 2026 release expressly includes Starlink within unit connectivity upgrades [S5]. A separate July 2026 parliamentary answer says NSW Ambulance uses layered communications and cautions that geographic/infrastructure conditions affect satellite as well as other systems [S10]. The correct inference is resilience value and a need for failover, not universal suitability or sole reliance. US FEMA/Helene indexed material was located but the underlying document failed live retrieval in this pass; ambiguous “satellites” versus ground terminals was not adopted as an inventory fact [L1].

**Transport.** United's installed aircraft count and Carnival's historical fleet rollout demonstrate an established commercial service footprint [S3–S4]. Neither demonstrates that Starlink is a certified sole safety-of-life link. Carnival's explicit multivendor architecture is material counterevidence to an assumption that installation means no substitute. Ordinary Starlink terms found in indexed versions warn against mission-critical or safety-of-life reliance [S14]; the applicable national account contract still needs authentication.

**Conflict and humanitarian operations.** Incorporate the saved Ukraine Incident Evidence Dossier v0.1, stable libfile_69d273ec8ec481918d583775c130f1a5. It separates Crimea activation refusal, reported Kherson shutdown, company explanations, government requests and the October 2026 military StarShield-package restriction. No casualty, intent or commercial-control conclusion is inferred beyond that dossier. Global aid/refugee terminal counts, backup capability, procurement conditions and service-outcome denominators remain unavailable in this pass. Military StarShield dependence cannot automatically justify acquisition of the distinct commercial perimeter.

## Alternatives and necessity test

The Eutelsat original company overview describes a constellation exceeding 600 LEO satellites combined with GEO services [S11]. Its existence and BDUK's actual OneWeb trials rebut a claim that there is no alternative LEO technology anywhere [S7,S11]. They do not show equivalent household retail access, capacity in a given cell, portable terminal stocks, foreign permissions, installation timing or emergency priority. GEO/MEO, fiber, fixed wireless and cellular alternatives must likewise be evaluated at the service site and required date, with written offers and witnessed tests.

| Option | Public-interest mechanism | Material limitation to measure before deciding |
|---|---|---|
| Fairly compensated public acquisition | Public decision authority, charter duties, public audit and remedies | Purchase and separation costs; title/permissions; public surveillance and political misuse; lifecycle capex and launch dependence |
| Regulation plus enforceable service duties | Notice, nondiscrimination, complaints and privacy obligations without buying all assets | Authority/litigation, enforcement delay, ability to compel deployment, private residual decision rights |
| Long-term public procurement and continuity covenants | Specified priority, restoration times, reserve terminals and audited failover | Contract duration/termination; supplier/capacity concentration; actual enforceability; funding |
| Public competitor or mixed network | Alternative public capacity and bargaining power | Build time, spectrum, launch, capital and workforce; incumbent service during construction |
| Targeted subsidy plus infrastructure diversity | Affordable local service and terrestrial or competing satellite backup | Supplier availability, household takeup, risk of paying for unserved or already served premises |

No lifecycle cost ranking or numerical benefit-cost ratio is claimed. Obtain equivalent geographic coverage, busy-hour upload/download, latency, outage-duration distribution, power autonomy, security, accessibility and recovery commitments for each option. Price the public service obligations equally; otherwise an apparent cheap alternative may simply provide less service. Public ownership remains the objective; the evidence must still support why the chosen perimeter and safeguards serve that objective at a defensible cost.

## Affordability, rights and international effects

Company indexed residential offers describe location-dependent tiers; the official help page says an address is needed to see equipment and recurring charges [S12]. No invented national bill, global affordability result or universal hardware discount is used. A comparable household burden requires total installed cost, tax, energy, maintenance, data/priority terms, disposable income, accessibility and substitute prices in a defined geography. Lower prices promised without a funded operating model are not established benefits.

The indexed Global Privacy Policy, updated 15 January 2026, identifies subscriber/technical information, affiliate/service-provider sharing, government-request handling and business-transfer treatment [S13]. Live page text was script-limited. This is evidence of published policy text, not verification of actual collection practices, legality, government access or technical content inspection. Account-specific enterprise agreements and complete country terms require separate authentication.

Foreign civilian users may benefit from continuity, notice and enforceable safeguards; they may also face a new US state-control risk or host-government resistance. Public ownership should preserve existing valid services while obtaining necessary foreign consents and applying rights safeguards to all customers, with country-specific legal analysis. It should not promise foreign approvals, transfer another sovereign's spectrum rights by statute, or treat foreign patients/students as bargaining instruments. Low-income users and employees should have transition protections; owners receive lawful fair compensation. No group-specific incidence or compensation amount has been established here.

## Proposed Act implementation objectives — new policy design, not measured achievements

| Objective | Proposed measurable requirement | Baseline and acceptance evidence still needed |
|---|---|---|
| Continuity | Before each transferred critical-service cohort, inventory every contracted site, agree recovery-time/priority standards, and perform witnessed primary/failover tests | Actual contracts, mission risk classes, baseline outages, independent engineer's test records; disclose exceptions |
| Affordable access | Within 180 days, publish location-specific total-cost and income-burden methodology plus a costed access program; report quarterly takeup and denial reasons | Representative household/enterprise samples, energy/device costs, validated income data and appropriations; no invented price cap |
| Democratic decisions | Keep an auditable log of service restrictions with decision authority, lawful reason, geographic scope, duration and review route; publish safe aggregate statistics quarterly | Existing restrictions baseline, emergency/security redaction rules, actual complaint records |
| Privacy | Before migration, map data, purposes, recipients, retention and cross-border flows; independent privacy/security assessment; separate public oversight from content access | Actual architecture/processor agreements; enforceable country-specific rules and remediation |
| Diversity | At every genuinely critical site, test an independently supplied path or record why none is feasible, interim mitigation and funded remediation deadline | Mission/site-specific supplier quotes, terminal inventory, separate power/network dependencies |
| Accountability | Publish quarterly service availability, restoration, complaints and access metrics, disaggregated by geography and plan; independent annual audit | Definitions, audit access, fraud controls, confidentiality and statistically valid samples |

These objectives refine §§203–207 and 401–404; they are not already law, not regulatory powers established by this research and not a substitute for engineering standards. Proposed 180-day period is a drafting choice requiring implementation review. Legislators should receive a common-basis alternatives assessment before closing an acquisition, not after irreversible integration.
