## 1. Exact decision question and guardrails

**Question:** For what verifiable public-interest requirements, if any, would a **congressionally authorized and justly compensated acquisition of Starlink's commercial communications infrastructure and operations only** be more lawful, reliable, proportionate and cost-effective than a less intrusive instrument? Congress's possible legislative authority, the Fifth Amendment's **public-use** requirement, and the **policy/economic necessity** question are related but **distinct**. An acquisition serving a broadly accepted public purpose does **not** prove that public ownership is the best means of achieving it. Likewise, a policy preference for acquisition is not itself a statutory grant of authority.

The project perimeter excludes unrelated SpaceX launch/AI divisions and presumptively excludes separate Starshield/classified programs. Joint technology, procurement vehicles and infrastructure remain dependency and contract-separation questions, not automatic acquisitions. The term *necessity test* in this report means a **recommended evidentiary policy decision framework**, **not** a blanket statement of a separate constitutional strict-necessity requirement for every taking. Any proposed special statute may impose its own findings/standards.

## 3. Neutral decision requirements (define before choosing the option)

An expert panel should first adopt measurable mission outcomes, with separate civilian, disaster/emergency, defense-government, allied and international populations. Suggested evidence requirements, **not claimed existing conditions**:

1. **Availability and resilience:** mission-specific uptime, fallback activation time, regional outage duration and multi-operator failure correlations.
2. **Independent control and permissible uses:** ability to guarantee lawful continuity during emergencies or wartime under contracts/charter; documented ability to prevent arbitrary discriminatory denial without undermining provider safety/export law.
3. **Security and privacy:** adversarial cyber assessment, key/data custody, incident response, lawful access/privacy restrictions, oversight and classification boundaries.
4. **Coverage and capacity:** verified geography, terminals, supported throughput/latency under load, inter-satellite architecture, indoor/direct-to-device vs fixed terminal service distinction.
5. **Legal and diplomatic continuity:** U.S. and country-level licenses; spectrum/ITU coordination; treaty obligations; change-of-control consent; data/export rules.
6. **Total public expense:** acquisition price/just compensation, satellite replacement, launch capacity, space debris/insurance, ongoing O&M, staff/IP, litigation, indemnities, infrastructure separation, plus residual SpaceX service agreements. Compare on a common time horizon using probability-weighted scenarios.
7. **Rights and legitimacy:** procedural fairness and due process for owners, workforce rights, public/customer speech and privacy rights, transparent oversight, congressional authorization and independent evaluation.

Use these criteria to compare every option; do not pre-select acquisition in the mission-need definition.

**Comparative inference:** Primary sources establish viable *categories* of alternatives, notably hybrid architectures and subsidy/procurement programs. They **do not** prove any alternative satisfies all missions or that full acquisition is unjustified. An honest result is **"necessity not demonstrated; alternatives not yet disqualified"**, not **"acquisition illegal"** or **"privatization unequivocally best"**.

## 5. Legal assessment — what must be independently adjudicated

**Separation of powers:** Identify specifically which congressional authority and appropriation supports the defined target and mechanism. *Youngstown* weighs against an unsupported executive seizure, not against every legislated purchase. Historical wartime telecom control is a limited comparison, not a current standing authorization.

**Public purpose vs policy necessity:** Assess Fifth Amendment public use and compensation; do not recast an economic alternatives test as a universal constitutional requirement to prove no less intrusive method exists. Nevertheless, for a bill intended to support a sweeping and costly taking, a transparent empirical record of alternatives and proportionate public benefit is desirable and may reduce political and litigation risk.

**Property, process and compensation:** Require independent valuation of physical satellites, earth stations/terminals, customer and vendor contracts, software/IP, securities/encumbrances and going-concern effects on the actual legal owners. Distinguish a negotiated purchase from condemnation/compulsory vesting. Evaluate compensation to affected property holders and the required tribunal, notice, discovery and appeal rights. This is *not* resolved by applying real-estate-only 40 U.S.C. condemnation provisions to orbital assets.

**Regulatory and international law:** Under 47 U.S.C. §310(d), consent to covered FCC license/control transfers is a material issue; foreign market access and UN/ITU filings present distinct constraints. The reviewer must analyze whether any specially enacted vesting procedure interacts with these regimes, rather than assuming one overrides another.

**Constitutional/civil liberties:** Analyze future federal operator's constraints on customer speech, association, privacy and law-enforcement access; independent commission/board design, audits, data minimization and judicial remedies. Public ownership introduces government-actor obligations and misuse risks rather than automatically guaranteeing neutrality.

**National security and classified carve-out:** Do not presume Starshield, classified contracts or shared defense technology transfer. Evaluate separation/continuity and supply dependencies under classified counsel review where needed; keep public analysis limited to unclassified conclusions.

**Independent panel:** At a minimum, retain (i) constitutional and communications lawyers not advocating for one outcome; (ii) federal procurement/appropriations counsel; (iii) international telecom/space licensing counsel; (iv) valuation and restructuring specialists; (v) satellite engineers/flight safety and cyber specialists; (vi) civil-liberties and consumer representatives; (vii) procurement competition/antitrust specialists; and (viii) a responsible government audit body. Establish conflicts disclosures, rights to dissent, reproducible datasets and privileged handling of sensitive information. This is a **proposal**; no such review was commissioned or completed by Startake.
