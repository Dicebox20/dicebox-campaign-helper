## Legal/transaction distinctions and counterarguments

1. **Service access is not executable-control transfer.** A customer may have connectivity service access without code, firmware signing keys, network-management tools, space-ground command privileges, data formats or sublicensing rights. Licensing alone does not establish tangible asset legal title.
2. **Purchase structure matters.** An asset sale can require assignment/novation of software and third-party IP licenses, plus separate firmware source escrow and transition services; equity purchase may leave some licenses with original company subject to change-of-control terms; a compulsory federal statute would need a specific rights/remedy framework rather than claiming FAR clauses themselves vest IP. Coordinate with prior FAR 42.1204 novation and Item 1 owner/asset registers.
3. **Commercial versus noncommercial matters.** DFARS 252.227-7014 mixed-funding government-purpose logic pertains to the clause's other-than-commercial software. FAR 12.212 and DFARS 227.7202-3 instead require particular license/negotiated rights for commercial software. No conclusion is supportable without development history and actual executed clauses.
4. **Adverse implication:** Strong commercial license protections can impair independent federal ownership/operation if software or keys remain exclusive to retained SpaceX. **Competing possibility:** a transaction could negotiate irrevocable, transferable, security-cleared operational licenses, source/code access or escrow, maintenance support, and replacement licenses subject to owner, contractor, cybersecurity, export and classification constraints. These are feasible contractual design elements, not current Starlink facts or guaranteed approvals.
5. **Separate commercial Starlink from Starshield:** Shared code and infrastructure require allocation of ownership, access, license-backs and security restrictions without presuming transfer of classified software or contracts.
