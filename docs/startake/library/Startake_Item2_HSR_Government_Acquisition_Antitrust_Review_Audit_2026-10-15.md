## Central finding

**15 U.S.C. §18a(c)(4) expressly exempts “transfers to or from a Federal agency or a State or political subdivision thereof” from the HSR premerger notice requirements.** This is a **specific HSR procedural exemption**, not independent congressional authority to purchase or condemn commercial Starlink property, an automatic immunity from all antitrust rules, or a finding that every federally owned vehicle is a “Federal agency.” The transaction structure matters: a transfer directly to an authentic federal agency may fit (c)(4), whereas a stock or asset acquisition by a separate government-controlled but non-agency corporation can require distinct HSR entity and transaction analysis. A specially enacted public operator's charter and applicable entity law must be reviewed before calling it exempt. No proposed enacted Starlink acquisition charter or sale instrument was identified.

## Competing structural outcomes

1. **Direct federal department/agency purchases defined commercial property:** §18a(c)(4) is a strong specific exemption candidate because the transferee is an agency. **Separate authority**, available appropriation, compensation, transferability, creditor and FCC/foreign permissions remain necessary.
2. **New government corporation acquires Starlink commercial stock/assets:** outcome turns on whether the entity is itself a *Federal agency* for HSR purposes, not simply whether federal officers appoint directors or government supplies funding. The enabling law must specify legal status, governance, anti-trust treatment, assets and how acquisition occurs. FTC staff's state-corporation analogy warns against blanket inference.
3. **A privately controlled transition company or contractor acquires commercial Starlink property:** ordinary HSR thresholds, potential exemptions and waiting periods may matter. Do not use the Federal-agency exemption solely because a federal customer benefits from the purchase.
4. **Congressional special statute vests property directly:** explicit statutory vesting is not equivalent to ordinary private negotiated merger; study the actual statute and any explicit antitrust treatment. §18a(c)(5) or (6) requires its **own specific conditions** and is not inferred from a vague public-interest purpose.
5. **Ongoing acquisitions by the public operator:** even if a founding transfer were exempt, later purchases from third parties and competition impacts depend on the later entity, assets and law. HSR nonreportability is no substitute for substantive competition and public-service safeguards.
