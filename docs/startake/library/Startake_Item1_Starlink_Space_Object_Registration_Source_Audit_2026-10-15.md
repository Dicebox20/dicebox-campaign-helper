## Executive conclusion

The U.S.-submitted UN space-object registry document **ST/SG/SER.E/1227**, issued **6 December 2024** and reporting **August 2024** launches, provides direct primary evidence identifying individual **Starlink** spacecraft by name, international designator, launch date/location codes and orbital data. The U.S. diplomatic note reports these objects entered the UN Register on **31 October 2024**, while **explicitly qualifying** that the United States is *not necessarily a launching State for every object it registers*. This is valuable spacecraft-level identity, registration and responsibility evidence, **not a private-company ownership deed, evidence of full asset separability, or an assignment of property rights**. Neither the entire current Starlink constellation nor the legal owner/secured interests of the individual spacecraft have been certified here. **Terminological protection:** The UN **State of registry** is not by itself the Starlink corporation that owns or operates an object. An international designator identifies a space object; it does not establish title. The official U.S. historical practice includes objects it **owns or operates**, which is a broader test than ownership alone. A registration filing is not a bill of sale, financial encumbrance search, FCC spectrum grant or security clearance.

## Contradictions, alternative interpretations and unresolved evidence gaps

1. **Treaty text vs U.S. filing qualification.** Registration Convention Articles I–II define State of registry with reference to launching States. The U.S. 2024 note states the U.S. is *not necessarily a launching State* for each registered object. Document both original statements without deciding the validity of particular U.S. registrations, supplying an unrequested legal opinion, or suppressing contrary evidence.
2. **Registry does not answer beneficial-owner chain.** The 2004 State Department statement uses an **owned-or-operated** basis. A named Starlink satellite on a U.S. report cannot be asserted to be owned by Starlink Internet Services UK Limited, a particular SpaceX affiliate, Elon Musk personally or the U.S. government. An acquisition claimant/compensation map must come from asset-title and financial records.
3. **Official UN document access remains incomplete.** Although 2024 original English ST/SG/SER.E/1227 textual table and U.S. note were retrieved, PDF screenshot inspection yielded cache misses, other requested original documents sometimes did not render, and UNOOSA has acknowledged ongoing registration-document availability issues. Treat unavailable records as **unknown**, not zero.
4. **Historical snapshot vs current status.** The 2024 submitted orbit states and names are not a current operational census, not comprehensive fleet counts, and not evidence of a 2026 transfer to government or anyone else.
5. **Next critical evidence requests.** (i) Original U.S. national registry object-entry extracts and UN submission crosswalk for each current and retired constellation spacecraft; (ii) signed entity-by-entity spacecraft title and intercompany arrangements; (iii) recorded secured interests/waivers; (iv) commercial Starlink/Starshield classification; (v) launch, operation, maintenance, collision-liability and debris-disposal obligations; (vi) authenticated original nine-item subcategory list.
