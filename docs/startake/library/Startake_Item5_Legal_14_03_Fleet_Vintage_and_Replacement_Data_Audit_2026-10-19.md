## Executive finding

Official and issuer materials provide useful but incomplete evidence. SpaceX’s Q2 2026 Form 10-Q reports that its Connectivity segment operates a network powered by **over 10,200 Starlink broadband and mobile satellites in low Earth orbit as of 30 June 2026**. SpaceX’s launch pages give dated mission-level records, including a page reporting a 29-satellite launch on 25 August 2026. SEC launch metrics separately count launches and internal launches, but combine Starlink deployments with other internal activity and do not count individual satellites by vintage.

FCC authorizations require semiannual operator reports with lists of satellites removed from operation or screened from future deployment, reentries, and disposal failures. The Gen1 condition asks whether a satellite was removed within five years of beginning regular operations or was available as an on-orbit replacement; Gen2 reporting also covers launches and estimated orbital lifetime for disposal failures. FCC decisions confirm that SpaceX has filed reports and the Commission has used them to monitor disposal failures. These are valuable records to obtain and audit. They are SpaceX-reported compliance information, not a complete regulator-certified active-fleet census.

The verified primary-source materials available for this pass do **not** yield a reproducible spacecraft-by-spacecraft table joining launch/deployment date, start of regular service, generation, active status, removal, and reentry. The FCC orders define the reporting fields and cite prior reports, but the underlying report attachments were not retrieved here as authenticated FCC-hosted copies; no row-level report data is treated as verified. Public launch pages supply mission dates and some payload counts but not each satellite’s unique identity or operating start date. One SpaceX launch page showed conflicting date information and was excluded from data construction.

The FCC’s 2021 record discusses a five-to-seven-year nominal mission-life range and different replenishment assumptions. Those are historical design/planning statements and estimates, not observed service lives for current cohorts. A debris-disposal condition or a reentry does not itself establish that a satellite failed or provided service for five years. A full active-vintage record therefore remains unavailable from the verified sources in this audit.

**Disposition:** Public records can anchor an aggregate fleet-size observation and define the structure of a potentially useful retirement dataset. They cannot populate the workbook’s actual cohort-age or replacement-timing inputs. The proposed Bill already calls for generation, condition, remaining service life, failure/retirement assumptions, capacity, replacement expenditure, and source limitations. No Bill or workbook edit is recommended or made. The next evidence step is to obtain and authenticate the underlying FCC reports and build a source-record crosswalk to dated launch, commissioning, active-status, and retirement records.

## Bill and workbook fit

Proposed §103(f) already requires a replacement estimate to identify satellite generation and condition; expected remaining service life; failure and retirement assumptions; capacity and service requirements; manufacturing and launch; ground infrastructure, terminals, software, spares, disposal obligations, and permission constraints. It also requires continuing replacement expenditure to be reflected consistently in terminal cash flows and bars treating whole-program budgets divided by satellite counts as verified unit prices. Section 103(g) distinguishes observed amounts, forecasts, independent estimates, scenarios, and unavailable data, and requires updates when material information changes.

Proposed §208(c) requires an annual budget to identify launch and replacement costs, shared-service charges, security costs, depreciation, reserves, actual obligations and outlays, and any unfunded requirement. Proposed §301(b) requires a ten-year budget with replacement satellites and launch needs, cybersecurity, scenarios, sensitivity ranges, and appraisal limitations. Section 301(c) requires common valuation and forecast bases, distinct federal obligations and outlays, and traceable financing, transition, operations, and replenishment flows without duplicate counting. Sections 302–303 govern the use of actual appropriations and obligation controls. These are proposed provisions, not current law.

The Actual case remains “inputs missing”; the satellite/replacement-related driver groups—satellites manufactured, manufacturing unit cost, launches purchased, launch unit cost, other capital expenditure, and depreciation—remain missing or invalid. The model therefore cannot translate an aggregate satellite count, a design-life statement, or launch cadence into Actual replacement timing or cash requirements. The workbook has not been changed.

## Interpretation and limits

1. **Design life is not realized operating life.** FCC 21-48 records five-to-seven-year statements and alternative planning assumptions; it does not establish that each satellite lasts that long in productive service. Service duration can differ from time in orbit, and the Commission’s early-mission-termination discussion separates continued orbiting from continued service.
2. **Reentry is not necessarily a failure.** FCC reporting separates satellites removed from operation, screened from future deployment, reentered, and subject to disposal failure. Reentry is an end-of-orbit event; it does not alone show when commercial service ended or why.
3. **Launch cadence is not replacement need.** A dated launch or aggregate internal-launch count does not identify whether a payload was expansion, replenishment, an on-orbit spare, a mobile satellite, or another internal mission. Gross launches cannot be subtracted from an aggregate fleet count to infer retirements without object identity, dates, and scope definitions.
4. **Regulatory data are operator reports.** FCC orders make the reports a useful source of SpaceX’s stated events and the Commission’s monitoring framework. They do not convert the underlying operator tables into independent engineering or accounting assurance.
5. **Source retrieval is incomplete.** FCC orders cite and describe submitted status reports, including reports filed in 2023 and a combined Gen1/Gen2 report filed 1 July 2025. The row-level attachments needed for this assignment were not retrieved in an authenticated FCC-hosted form during the review; therefore this report does not quote or count satellite rows from them, and does not conclude that such reports or data do not exist.
6. **Financial translation remains outside the observed record.** An aggregate fleet count and consolidated PP&E category do not supply commercial Starlink unit cost, generation-specific remaining life, launch cost, engineering capacity needs, or actual replacement expenditure. The SEC Financial-Input Eligibility Reconciliation and Comparable/Replacement-Cost report remain the records for their broader assigned limits; this pass does not re-audit costs or company/segment allocation.

- Obtain the original FCC Gen1 and Gen2 semiannual report filings and attachments for each reporting period, including the FCC filing record, attachment metadata, report date, and original bytes/hash. Extract the rows for satellites removed or screened, reentered, and disposal failures while preserving the report’s stated definitions and reporting-period cutoff.
- Obtain a SpaceX launch manifest that links each launch to individual satellite identifiers, generation/model, deployment date, and any available first-regular-operation date. Preserve unsuccessful deployment, commissioning, replacement-spare, and mobile-system classifications as separate states.
- Obtain the source-native fleet-status register or equivalent dated record showing each object’s active, degraded, screened, removed-for-disposal, and reentered status with effective dates. Reconcile operator-reported status to authoritative orbital-catalog records where available, documenting catalog timing and identity-link limitations.
- Maintain an explicit source-record crosswalk: source system, persistent object and mission identifiers, joining key and method, exact/nonexact/unresolved link, duplicate or one-to-many link, unmatched records, cutoff date, and the distinction between launch, orbit insertion, regular operations, removal, and reentry.
- Only after the satellite-level schedule is authenticated should the business perimeter and engineering service-capacity requirements be mapped to model cohorts. Unit costs, launch cost, ground/terminal/cyber investment, supplier capacity, and title/perimeter are separately scoped evidence requirements and are excluded from this audit.
