## Clause and acquisition relevance matrix

| Clause | Source-text scope / trigger | Supported conclusion and limitation |
|---|---|---|
| **(a) Priority communications** | During war in which the United States is engaged, presidential national-defense necessity finding permits priority communications orders to covered carriers | Priority-routing authority, not automatic purchase, title vesting, transfer of all assets, or permanent Starlink nationalization; carrier exemption is specific to compliant prioritization |
| **(b) Anti-obstruction** | During war, criminalizes willful physical-force or threatened-force interference with interstate/foreign radio/wire communication; allows armed forces to prevent obstruction in public interest | Protective interference power, not acquisition of companies or technology |
| **(c) Emissions/radio** | Presidential proclamation of war, threat of war, public peril/disaster/other national emergency, or neutrality context; presidential security/defense necessity finding; temporary regulatory adjustment, closing, apparatus removal or government use/control of stations or specified electromagnetic devices within U.S. jurisdiction | Can allow use/control **upon just compensation** for covered owners; its scope is bounded by triggering facts, station/device coverage and territorial jurisdiction; does not automatically vest satellites, patents, foreign operating permissions or business equity |
| **(d) Wire facilities** | Presidential proclamation of war or threat of war *involving the United States*; security/defense necessity; wire-communications regulation, closure or government use/control of facilities within U.S. jurisdiction | **Express temporal limit:** power period ends no later than six months after war/threat ends, or earlier date Congress designates by concurrent resolution in statutory text. Separate from (c)'s triggers and from permanent acquisition; just compensation for use/control |
| **(e) Compensation** | President certifies just compensation for such use/control to Congress for appropriation/payment; dissatisfied owner paid 75% of certified amount, may sue U.S. for additional sum to yield just compensation under 28 U.S.C. §§1346 or 1491 | Identifiable statutory monetary remedy for government use/control, **not** an asset-level compensation schedule for purchasing an ongoing satellite enterprise; do not call the 75% payment full satisfaction |
| **(f) State powers** | Preserves state taxation and lawful police powers subject to express exceptions for government communications and securities issuance | Federal use/control should not be described as wholesale displacement of state law |
| **(g) Regulatory bounds** | Clauses (c)/(d) do not authorize FCC rule revisions the FCC itself could not make; (d) action effects must not continue past the statutorily permitted date | Additional limits on overreading extraordinary executive authority |

## Distinct legal conclusions and contrary views

1. A national emergency **alone** does not equal permanent congressional conveyance of a private commercial satellite provider. Whether an actual contemporary proclamation/order satisfies each trigger is an evidence question not resolved by statutory wording alone.
2. Government temporary use/control is distinguishable from acquiring legal title, equity, foreign licenses, IP, launch manufacturing capacity, dedicated Starshield systems and broadband subscriber contracts. A contemplated permanent Starlink-only acquisition requires separate substantive authority, just compensation and associated license/contract consents.
3. Clause (c) covers radio stations/devices with specific jurisdiction/technical qualifiers; clause (d) covers wire facilities with narrower war/threat trigger and express duration ceiling. A hybrid satellite/ground-fiber network cannot be deemed comprehensively covered without an entity, device and jurisdiction inventory.
4. Counterargument to absolute conclusions: §606 is genuine extraordinary control authority under specified conditions, so it is wrong to say the executive never can temporarily direct use of qualifying communications facilities. It is equally wrong to treat this as a blank check to nationalize all Starlink.
