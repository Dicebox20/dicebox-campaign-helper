## Evidence reviewed and results

SEC Form 10-Q of Space Exploration Technologies Corp., filed August 4, 2026, reporting period ended June 30, 2026; primary filing https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1181412/000162828026052535/spcx-20260630.htm and SEC filing index https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1181412/000162828026052535/0001628280-26-052535-index.htm . **Material finding:** The company reports three consolidated segments (Space, Connectivity, AI). Connectivity covers Starlink broadband/mobile service but SEC filing also explicitly discusses Starshield in its Connectivity revenue recognition policies. Therefore Connectivity results are an **upper/perimeter-mixed accounting proxy**, not an audited carve-out Starlink-only valuation. This is a structural limitation, not proof the whole segment is transferable.
2. Same SEC filing, segment results section, Connectivity. **Reported** H1 2026 segment revenue $7,548 million vs H1 2025 $5,062 million; H1 2026 operating income $2,844 million vs H1 2025 $1,956 million; H1 2026 cost of revenue $3,711 million, R&D $499 million, SG&A $494 million. Q2 2026 revenue $4,291 million and operating income $1,656 million. All dollar amounts from reported tables, not model estimates. 3. Same SEC filing, key operating metrics: end-June 2026 **12.0 million Starlink subscriber service lines**, up from 6.0 million at end-June 2025; subscriber ARPU **$66/month** for six months ending June 2026 vs $85 a year earlier. Metric expressly *excludes* certain managed enterprise/government customers; subscribers are service lines, not people. Subscriber figures must not be multiplied by period-end counts to misstate total realized recurring revenue.
4. Same SEC filing, Space/Connectivity accounting: internal launches are not recorded as intersegment revenue; Starlink satellite launch costs are capitalized within satellites in property, plant and equipment. Thus a separately owned Starlink business must forecast launch purchases/transfer pricing, satellite replacement and maintenance costs as independent cash obligations; parent launch division revenue is not additive to Starlink revenue without elimination review.

This is context, not independently audited segment revenue. As of June 2026 the later SEC metric above is preferable for comparable service-line counts.

FCC August 18, 2026 correction to 2026 Section 706 Report, https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/DOC-424235A1.pdf : FCC corrected a prior count to describe SpaceX report of over 10,200 on-orbit broadband/mobile satellites and global Starlink subscriber base rising from 6M to 12M June 2025 to June 2026. The FCC explicitly cites the SEC 10-Q. This is independent regulatory corroboration of the filing reference, not standalone audit of asset condition.

## Ten-year pro forma design (not falsely populated)

**Required variable ledger, each variable to carry source, date, definition, confidence and scenario:**
- Subscribers by tier and geography, beginning balances, gross additions/churn, average active service lines, ARPU and realized discounts.
- Managed enterprise, aviation, maritime, government *excluding Starshield* revenue, terminal revenue and subsidies/returns.
- Operating expenditures by class, segment allocations and stand-alone overhead increment.
- Satellite additions, retirements, orbital replenishment cadence and unit manufacturing cost.
- Internal-to-market launch price bridge, launch cadence and ground/terminal installation capex.
- Tax, working capital, insurance, cybersecurity, spectrum/license costs, contingencies and debt service.
- Discount rates, market comps (public satellite/telecom comparables only with justified structural adjustments), transaction comparables, and replacement-cost evidence.

**DCF:** `FCFF_t = EBIT_t × (1-tax_t) + D&A_t − capex_t − change-in-net-working-capital_t`; `EV = Σ(FCFF_t/(1+WACC)^t) + TV/(1+WACC)^10`; `equity value = EV + separable cash − assigned debt ± other claims/adjustments`. Terminal assumptions are scenarios, not observed facts. Compensation for property, public fiscal costs and negotiated purchase price require separate analyses.

**Three scenario dimensions:** downside (low ARPU, high churn, increased satellite replacement, market-priced external launches, delayed licenses), base (filing-anchored operating margins but adjusted for carve-out/capex), upside (enterprise traction, scale and more favorable replacement costs). No numerical valuation range is asserted without an audited carve-out balance sheet, Starshield split, attributable free cash flow, reasonable independent launch pricing, and sensitivity-backed terminal value.
