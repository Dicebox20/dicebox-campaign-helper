## Core finding

The APA, Hobbs Act and Communications Act provide **distinct channels for contesting qualifying final agency decisions**; they **do not create a substantive power to acquire Starlink assets**, and do not collapse compensation, federal contract disputes, FCC licensing appeals and a direct statutory vesting challenge into a single case. Under the APA a qualifying claimant generally must identify reviewable final agency action, standing, redressability, the absence of a preclusive statute or another adequate court remedy, and a legally cognizable defect. Many final FCC orders under 47 U.S.C. §402(a) instead enter the special **court-of-appeals Hobbs Act** pathway, whereas the categories in §402(b) require distinct **D.C. Circuit appeals**. A §402(a) petition governed by 28 U.S.C. §2344 generally has a **60-day** period; §402(b) generally requires a **30-day** notice under §402(c). 47 U.S.C. §405 imposes a separate **30-day FCC reconsideration** petition and particular preservation rules, but reconsideration is *not universally a condition of judicial review*. None of these clocks should be applied to an invented Starlink acquisition decision.

## Interpretive boundaries, adverse views and options

1. **Executive implementing act vs congressional text.** The APA primarily concerns agency conduct, not enactment of a statute by Congress. If a future statute directly vests defined property, the APA cannot automatically be assumed to supply review of the statute itself. An agency-administered purchase or order may entail APA review, subject to §701/§704 and applicable special statute; constitutional challenges and compensation remedies need separate analysis.
2. **Alternative adequate remedy and specialized forum.** An injury seeking monetary compensation under the Fifth Amendment may implicate the Tucker Act and Court of Federal Claims; a procurement contract dispute implicates the Contract Disputes Act. An FCC grant's validity generally cannot be relitigated as a generic district-court APA matter if §402/§2342 supplies the governing exclusive appellate route.
3. **Finality, standing and record.** An announced commercial agreement, proposed nationalization bill, informal agency statement or pending licence request is not automatically final agency action. Actual injury, party status, record, administrative presentation of objections, order issuance and petitioner identity control review.
4. **Review period collision.** The Hobbs Act's 60-day route for §402(a) orders, the §402(b)/(c) 30-day appeal and FCC §405 reconsideration's 30-day window are not interchangeable. Reconsideration may be needed for issues not presented, but is not universally required and does not automatically stay implementation. Independent counsel must calendar specific orders and any judicial tolling rules.
5. **Adverse and supportive interpretations.** Affected carriers, leaseholders, creditors, property owners and rivals may have distinct arguments about legally protected rights, improper agency procedure and public interest. Conversely, properly enacted legislation and a well-supported agency order may survive review; neither side can assume success without the actual statute, agency record and applicable review standard.
