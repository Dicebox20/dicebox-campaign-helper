## Question, method, and scope

This pass asks whether §303(b)'s existing continuity-reserve placeholder has clear statutory purposes and administrable controls for when the Secretary may obligate the reserved amount, what evidence must support that decision, how duplicate charges are prevented, and what Congress and the public should see afterward.

The assignment is limited to Bill v1.12 §303(b). It integrates the recently completed Item 5 funding-choice decision schedule, the exact original legal scope 14.01 (*Acquisition financing and appropriation*) at the appropriation/disbursement interface, and §401(c)/§401(d)/§402 continuity gates. Original scope 17.04 (*Valuation, compensation and financing*) is reviewed only as an integration reference. It does not repeat the prior SEC input map or Q2 historical calibration work. Item 1 remains excluded.

I compared the complete §303 and dependency language in Bill v1.12, Crosswalk v1.8 and workbook v0.4, reviewed the prior §303 funding-choice audit and prior appropriation-controls work, resolved relevant Title 31 citations through Legal Data Hunter, and compared the 2024 code snapshot with the House Office of the Law Revision Counsel text current through Public Law 119-120 (7 October 2026). The House text is treated as the current source; the LDH 2024 edition is recorded only as a separately dated cross-check.

## Finding and drafting choice

Bill v1.12 §303(b) already appropriates an unresolved acquisition/transition amount and requires that no acquisition obligation or taking begin until the Secretary certifies compliance with §§104 and 302 and availability of budget authority. It also sets an unresolved minimum continuity reserve. The subsection did not define eligible continuity uses, the evidence for drawing on the reserve, how charges would be separated from other costs, or how the reserved balance and use would be reported.

The selected approach designates the reserve as a portion of the same appropriation, gives it a narrow continuity purpose tied to the existing critical-service register and transition/cutover machinery, requires a written cost/risk/alternatives and no-duplication record for each obligation, and requires quarterly accounting and public reporting with the lawful protections already built into §206(a). It expressly prevents a diversion to other purposes without later law and preserves the appropriation's existing availability and account-closing rules. The amount, acquisition price, due-diligence amount and period, and all other placeholders stay unresolved.

The proposed text is integrated in Bill v1.13 §303(b). No borrowing or guarantee authority is created; no money is made available beyond the appropriation's unresolved stated amount; and no compensation or interest owed for property already taken is reduced.

## Proposed operative text

The following is the complete proposed replacement for §303(b); all other operative bill text is preserved:

> **(b) ACQUISITION, COMPENSATION, TRANSITION, AND CONTINUITY APPROPRIATION.—**For acquisition and transition under this Act, there is appropriated to the Fund, out of any money in the Treasury not otherwise appropriated, $[ACQUISITION AND TRANSITION AMOUNT], to remain available until expended solely for the purposes stated in this Act and the applicable appropriation, of which not less than $[CONTINUITY RESERVE] shall be reserved within the Fund solely for the purposes described in paragraphs (1) through (4). The amount so reserved is an allocation within, and not an addition to, the amount appropriated by this subsection. No acquisition obligation or taking may be initiated until the Secretary certifies compliance with sections 104 and 302 and that legally available budget authority is available for the purpose, amount, and period of the obligation.
>
> **(1) PERMITTED USE.—**An amount reserved under this subsection may be obligated only for a written arrangement or other measure reasonably necessary to preserve a service identified in the critical-service register under section 401(d) during a transition or cutover under this Act, including—
>
> (A) a transition-services arrangement satisfying section 401(c); or
>
> (B) alternative capacity, restoration, failover, rollback, or another continuity measure identified in the transition plan under section 401 and tied to a cutover or test under section 402.
>
> An amount reserved under this subsection may not be obligated for purchase consideration or for a purpose not stated in this paragraph.
>
> **(2) DETERMINATION BEFORE OBLIGATION.—**Before obligating an amount reserved under this subsection, the Secretary shall make and retain a written determination that—
>
> (A) identifies the affected service, transition phase, documented risk of interruption, and proposed arrangement or measure;
>
> (B) describes the cost basis and reasonably available alternatives considered;
>
> (C) states how a transition-services arrangement satisfies section 401(c), or how another measure is identified in the transition plan and tied to the tests or cutover under section 402; and
>
> (D) identifies the appropriation and account records against which the obligation will be charged and explains how the same cost will not be charged or reported more than once.
>
> **(3) ACCOUNTING AND REPORTING.—**The Secretary shall maintain records that separately identify the amount appropriated under this subsection, the amount designated for continuity, obligations, outlays, and the unobligated balance. For each fiscal quarter, the Secretary shall transmit to the Committees on Appropriations and the Committees on Commerce, Science, and Transportation of the Senate and House of Representatives, and publish a summary consistent with the protections in section 206(a), identifying the purposes, services, measures, performance status, obligations, outlays, and remaining balance of the amount designated for continuity. The Secretary shall identify the legal basis for a redaction where lawful and shall provide supporting records to oversight bodies as permitted by law.
>
> **(4) AVAILABILITY AND NONDIVERSION.—**An amount designated under this subsection remains subject to the purpose, amount, period of availability, apportionment, and account-closing rules that otherwise apply to the appropriation under this subsection. The amount may not be transferred or obligated for another purpose except as specifically provided by a subsequent Act of Congress. This paragraph does not extend the period of availability or create additional budget authority.
>
> Nothing in this subsection reduces final compensation or interest owed for property already taken.

## Legal fit and contrary considerations

Current [31 U.S.C. §1512(c)(1)](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/119-120/t31/s1512/) limits a reserve established in apportioning or reapportioning an appropriation to the specified circumstances, including when it is specifically provided by law. The draft makes the continuity designation part of the enacted appropriation itself and preserves applicable apportionment rules; it does not direct an administrator to hold funds outside the appropriation's statutory terms. Whether the Fund's internal designation is also an administrative apportionment reserve, and how any applicable Impoundment Control Act reporting applies, remains for appropriations counsel and agency budget officials.

The purposes and no-diversion controls implement the rule that appropriations remain limited to their enacted objects and that diversion to another purpose is treated as a new appropriation under [31 U.S.C. §1301(a)–(b)](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/119-118/t31/s1301/). The existing bill's available-amount certification is retained; [31 U.S.C. §1341(a)](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/119-120/t31/s1341/) prohibits obligations beyond available appropriations or before an appropriation absent legal authorization, and [31 U.S.C. §1517(a)](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/119-120/t31/s1517/) separately limits obligations by the apportionment.

The written determination and retained source records make the reserve decision reviewable while respecting the documentary obligation rules in [31 U.S.C. §1501](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/119-120/t31/s1501/). The new language expressly preserves the appropriation's existing availability terms; it neither creates a new period nor overrides fixed-period balances rule in [31 U.S.C. §1502](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/119-120/t31/s1502/).

The strongest operational concern is that a documentation or publication gate could slow a response to a looming service interruption or expose sensitive dependencies. The draft requires an internal decision record before obligation, rather than advance congressional permission, and its public summary uses §206(a)'s privacy, trade-secret, operational-security and classified-information protections. Underlying support is to be provided only through lawful access. A further emergency exception could be considered by counsel, but none is added here because it would create a separate trigger and reporting policy without evidence in the present record.

A second concern is that a reserve may later prove excessive or unnecessary. This amendment does not authorize administrative diversion to another purpose; any change must follow the law governing the appropriation and any later Act of Congress. The enacted reserve amount and its fiscal/accounting treatment remain for Congress and appropriations counsel to decide.
