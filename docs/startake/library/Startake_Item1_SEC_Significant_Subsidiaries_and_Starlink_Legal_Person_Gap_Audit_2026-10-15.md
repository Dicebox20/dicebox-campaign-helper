# Item 1 — SEC Significant-Subsidiary Schedule and Starlink Legal-Person Gap Audit

**Research checkpoint:** 15 October 2026 (UTC)  
**Assignment:** `STARTAKE-20261015-WORK-I1-ENTITY-3d8f72`  
**Status:** Public-source crosswalk complete; verification and formal acceptance remain open.  
**Purpose:** Compare filed significant-subsidiary schedules with the SEC’s subsequent group/segment disclosures, a relevant FCC licensee statement, and the May 19 credit-facility guarantor roster. This is a bounded public-record audit, not a certified group chart or legal opinion.

## Finding

The latest located SEC-filed Exhibit 21.1 is attached to Space Exploration Technologies Corp.’s May 20, 2026 Form S-1. It identifies four significant subsidiaries: X Corp. (Nevada), X.AI LLC (Nevada), CTC Property LLC (Wyoming), and SpaceX Services Inc. (Texas). The earlier March DRS and May 7 DRS/A list the same four entities with “United States” as jurisdiction. The S-1/A filings of June 1 and June 3 do not include a replacement Exhibit 21.1; neither the August 4 Form 10-Q nor the August 14 Form 8-K does so. The SEC filing list reviewed on October 15 showed no later subsidiary schedule.

The four-name schedule is not a complete subsidiary census or an asset/title chart. FCC order DA 26-471 separately describes Space Exploration Holdings, LLC and SpaceX Services Inc. as wholly owned SpaceX subsidiaries holding multiple Commission licenses and authorizations. Thus, an entity omitted from Exhibit 21.1 is publicly identified elsewhere as a wholly owned subsidiary and license holder. The omission cannot be used to infer that Space Exploration Holdings LLC does not exist, is not a subsidiary, or lacks relevant assets or rights.

The filings support describing Starlink as the issuer’s Connectivity business/offering, not as a named legal person in this schedule. They do not establish that no Starlink-named entity exists. The 10-Q consolidates the issuer and wholly owned subsidiaries and reports three operating segments—Space, Connectivity and AI—but the segment presentation does not allocate assets, contracts, licenses, or liabilities to specific legal entities.

## Entity and role distinctions

- **Issuer / parent:** Space Exploration Technologies Corp. is the SEC registrant and borrower under the May 19 credit facility. Its consolidated 10-Q uses “Company” or “SpaceX” for the issuer and its wholly owned subsidiaries collectively.
- **FCC-related legal persons:** The FCC names Space Exploration Holdings, LLC and SpaceX Services Inc. as wholly owned subsidiaries and license holders. SpaceX Services Inc. also appears on the SEC significant-subsidiary list. The FCC statement does not map every license to each entity or identify the owner of every satellite, gateway, customer terminal, contract, or other operating asset.
- **Facility guarantors:** X Corp., X.AI LLC, and CTC Property LLC are guarantors under this particular agreement. SpaceX Services Inc. is not listed as a guarantor on its execution pages. These financing roles differ from SEC significance and FCC licensee roles.
- **Starlink:** The 10-Q uses “Starlink offering” for the Connectivity segment. This supports a service/business description only; the reviewed evidence does not provide a complete Starlink legal-entity, beneficial-ownership, asset, or contract map.
- **Cursor:** The August 14 filing places Cursor under the issuer as an AI-related subsidiary after the reporting period. Its inclusion in the wider corporate group does not make it part of a commercial Starlink target.

## What this evidence does and does not show

**Established from the reviewed public filings:** the four entities and jurisdictions listed in the May 20 Exhibit 21.1; Space Exploration Holdings LLC’s separate identification as a wholly owned subsidiary and FCC license holder; the issuer’s consolidated three-segment presentation; the distinct borrower, guarantor, and FCC-licensee roles summarized above; and the post-quarter Cursor subsidiary transaction.

**Not established:** a complete domestic or foreign subsidiary census; direct versus indirect parentage for every subsidiary; beneficial ownership registers; any Starlink-named entity’s existence or nonexistence; entity-by-entity ownership of satellites, gateways, spectrum rights, customer equipment, software, intellectual property, contracts, or data; the commercial-only target boundary; lien priority, creditor consent, or transferability; and a valuation or statutory-transfer opinion.

The public records therefore narrow the entity gap but do not close Item 1. A defensible acquisition schedule still needs an authenticated entity chart and asset/right/contract crosswalk, with explicit exclusions for other SpaceX businesses, Starshield and classified programs. The May 19 guarantor list and the FCC licensee statement are distinct cross-checks, not substitutes for title and contract schedules.

## Legislative relevance and next evidence

This audit makes no bill edit. The evidence supports a drafting requirement that any eventual transfer schedule identify legal persons and each included commercial asset, right, license, contract, and liability individually, with an explicit excluded-business schedule and a process for later-discovered entities. It does not supply the schedule itself or justify treating all subsidiaries of the issuer as acquisition targets.

Next evidence needed for Item 1 is an authenticated, current entity chart; member/share registers or equivalent ownership records; asset-by-asset title and financing schedules; and contract, customer, regulatory, and government-rights schedules that distinguish commercial Starlink operations from shared or excluded SpaceX operations. Those records are not present in the reviewed public filings. No third-party confirmation, paid registry search, legal opinion, or professional review was obtained in this pass.
