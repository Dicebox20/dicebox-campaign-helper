**Cutoff:** 2026-10-15 UTC. **Claim:** `STARTAKE-20261015-item2-cica-4b71ad`, confirmed saved in canonical tracker v102. **Method:** one bounded direct primary-source federal procurement-rule review, not Deep Research, not independent legal advice. **Perimeter:** possible congressional compensated acquisition of *commercial Starlink communications operations and infrastructure only*; unrelated SpaceX assets and Starshield/classified operations not presumptively included.

## Core finding

Existing competitive-procurement statutes and FAR rules govern ordinary **agency procurement of supplies and services**, including satellite broadband and continuity services. They do **not themselves authorize eminent domain, corporate nationalization, equity vesting or an enterprise-wide compulsory acquisition**. Conversely, the possibility that Congress might legislate a specially tailored purchase/condemnation scheme is **not blanket permission to waive otherwise applicable procurement competition** for separate transition, service, launch, ground-support and replacement purchases. Which law applies depends on the precise contemplated transaction, agency authority, procurement instrument and terms of enacted legislation; no Starlink-specific acquisition act, solicitation or justification is documented.

## Transaction-route comparison and contrary interpretations

| Hypothetical route | Competition analysis | Separate unresolved predicates |
|---|---|---|
| New agency contract purchasing Starlink broadband/service capacity | Normally FAR competition rules or valid exception; potential sole source requires J&A and approvals where applicable. | Agency needs statement, provider capability matrix, qualified marketplace, pricing, notice/J&A, appropriations and incumbent contract status. |
| Extension or order on an existing government contract | FAR Part 6 applicability depends on order vehicle, contract scope, and already competed terms; fair-opportunity rules may separately apply. | Original instrument, remaining scope, ordering rules, contract modifications, justification and funding. |
| Voluntary purchase of separable owned enterprise assets | Depends on express agency authority, appropriation, deal structure, and whether executed as procurement governed by procurement statutes. | Property legal title, shareholder/creditor consents, business vs services characterization, competition and fiscal-law counsel. |
| Express congressional compulsory acquisition / vesting | Ordinary FAR competition cannot itself authorize or invalidate an independent valid federal takings statute; legislative text determines special procedures and surviving obligations. | Actual valid enacted law, enumerated power/public use, due process, just compensation, ownership and foreign spectrum consents. |
| After-acquisition launch, network-operations, equipment and software support | Separate service/supply acquisitions often retain competition requirements and subcontract/contractor oversight. | Transition services, sole-source grounds, incumbent capability, replacement vendors, national security restrictions, market tests. |

**Adverse / alternative view:** A government buyer might argue that an integrated proprietary NGSO network has only one source for certain services or continuity components; opposing parties may show functional substitutes, improper artificial narrowing of agency specifications, or insufficient market research. The first argument may support a carefully justified exception **in a specified service contract**, but it is no substitute for independent nationalization authority or verification of uniquely adequate performance. Security and urgency exceptions also have statutory conditions and scrutiny; they are not self-declared by a project report.
