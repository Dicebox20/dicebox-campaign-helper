## Executive finding

Section 606(h) is a standalone penalty provision for willful conduct tied to the exercise of presidential authority under 47 U.S.C. §606: doing, causing, or suffering a prohibited act, or failing, causing, or suffering a failure to do a required act. It states a standard tier of up to one year of imprisonment and a $1,000 fine for an individual, with a $5,000 fine stated for a firm, partnership, association, or corporation; a separate tier requires intent to injure the United States or secure an advantage to a foreign nation and states up to 20 years and a $20,000 fine. The statute requires willfulness for the predicate conduct and an additional specified intent for the enhanced tier. [47 U.S.C. §606(h)](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&num=0&req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title47-section606); [1 U.S.C. §1](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&f=treesort&num=27186&req=18).

For conduct after 1 November 1987, Title 18’s general sentencing chapter applies to offenses defined by any federal statute, absent a more specific rule. On the better textual reading, §§3559 and 3571 supply the applicable offense grades and can raise the default maximum fines above §606(h)’s stated dollar figures: a standard one-year offense is ordinarily Class A misdemeanor; the 20-year tier is ordinarily Class C felony. Unless the misdemeanor results in death or the gain/loss alternative applies, the resulting grade-based fine caps are $100,000 for an individual and $200,000 for an organization in the standard tier, and $250,000 for an individual and $500,000 for an organization in the enhanced tier. This reading follows §3571’s “greatest of” formula and its requirement that an offense statute specifically reference and exempt itself from the otherwise applicable fine to preserve a lower cap. Section 606(h) contains no such reference. No reported federal decision directly construing §606(h) was located in the bounded search described below, so the fine interaction—especially the enhanced tier’s application to organizations—should be confirmed by counsel. [18 U.S.C. §§3551(a), 3559(a), 3571(b)–(e)](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/119-120/t18/s3571/).

Sections 501–502 do not replace §606(h)’s own penalty for the same §606(h) offense. Section 501 is a residual penalty for Chapter 5 offenses for which no other penalty is provided in that chapter; §606(h) supplies one. Section 502 instead requires a willful and knowing violation of a Commission-imposed rule or a covered international rule and states a separate per-day fine “in addition to any other penalties.” A §606(h) violation alone does not establish those separate §502 elements. [47 U.S.C. §§501–502](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&path=%2Fprelim%40title47%2Fchapter5%2Fsubchapter5).

## Stated penalties and general sentencing interaction

Section 3551(a) applies Title 18’s sentencing chapter to a defendant convicted of an offense described in any federal statute, except as otherwise specifically provided or for the listed exclusions. [18 U.S.C. §3551(a)](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/119-120/t18/s3551/) Section 3571 became applicable to offenses committed after 1 November 1987. [18 U.S.C. §3571 and effective-date note](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/119-120/t18/s3571/) Section 606(h) is a federal statutory offense and contains no provision displacing these general rules.

Because §606(h) does not specifically assign a letter grade, §3559(a) classifies the offense by the maximum authorized prison term: one year or less but more than six months is Class A misdemeanor; 10 years or more but less than 25 years is Class C felony. Section 3559(b) carries the incidents of that grade while preserving the maximum prison term specified by the offense statute. [18 U.S.C. §3559(a)–(b)](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/119-120/t18/s3559/).

| Tier / person | Maximum stated in §606(h) | Likely §3559 grade | General fine ceiling under §3571, if no death-resulting misdemeanor and no gain/loss alternative |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| Standard tier, individual | $1,000 fine; up to 1 year; or both | Class A misdemeanor | Up to $100,000 for an individual |
| Standard tier, organization | $5,000 fine stated for a firm, partnership, association, or corporation | Class A misdemeanor based on the offense’s one-year maximum; organization may receive a fine or probation, not imprisonment under §3551(c) | Up to $200,000 for an organization |
| Enhanced tier, individual | $20,000 fine; up to 20 years; or both | Class C felony | Up to $250,000 for an individual |
| Enhanced tier, organization | The exception applies to “any person” and states the $20,000 fine / 20-year tier; entity-specific application of that wording should be confirmed | Class C felony if the enhanced offense applies to the entity | Up to $500,000 for an organization |

The table states statutory ceilings, not expected fines. Section 3571(b)–(c) sets the greatest of the offense-specific amount, the applicable gain/loss amount under §3571(d), and the grade-based cap. For an organization, §18 defines the term as a person other than an individual. Section 3571(b)(4) and (c)(4) give higher fine caps for a misdemeanor resulting in death ($250,000 / $500,000); if gain or loss qualifies, subsection (d) permits up to twice gross gain or loss, unless imposing that fine would unduly complicate or prolong sentencing. Those conditional variants are omitted from the main table. [18 U.S.C. §§18, 3551(c), 3571(b)–(d)](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/119-120/t18/s3571/).

The best reading is that §3571’s general ceilings can exceed §606(h)’s stated $1,000 / $5,000 / $20,000 amounts for covered post-1987 conduct. Section 3571(e) preserves a lower fine only when the law defining the offense specifies a lower fine and, by specific reference, exempts the offense from the otherwise applicable §3571 fine. Section 606(h) does not make that reference. A contrary textual argument is that §606(h)’s specific dollar amounts should control as offense-specific maxima; the structure of §3571(e), which expressly requires a specific-reference exemption, weighs against that reading. No located §606(h) decision settles the issue. The historical 1934 enactment date does not by itself avoid §3571 for later conduct because the official effective-date note makes §3571 applicable to offenses committed after 1 November 1987. [18 U.S.C. §3571(b)–(e) and effective-date note](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/119-120/t18/s3571/).

The entity application of the enhanced tier merits separate counsel review. Section 1 of Title 1 includes corporate forms within “person”; §606(h)’s enhanced clause uses “any person,” while its ordinary clause spells out firms and corporations. Section 3551(c) authorizes organization probation or fines, and §3571(c) supplies organization fine ceilings. The statutory text does not expressly repeat the ordinary tier’s entity-specific $5,000 wording in the enhanced clause or expressly discuss corporate attribution of the required enhanced intent. The §3571 result shown above is therefore a strong statutory reading if the enhanced offense is charged and proved against an organization, not a settled case holding. [1 U.S.C. §1; 47 U.S.C. §606(h); 18 U.S.C. §§18, 3551(c), 3571(c), (e)](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/119-120/t18/s3571/).

## Sections 501 and 502

**Section 501.** It criminalizes willful-and-knowing violations or omissions elsewhere in Chapter 5 only where “no penalty (other than a forfeiture) is provided in this chapter,” with a subsequent-conviction provision. Because §606(h), within Title 47 Chapter 5, expressly provides a penalty for its own predicate, §501’s residual penalty does not displace or add a second penalty for the same §606(h) offense on the face of the statutory text. A separate violation of another Chapter 5 provision would require its own elements and penalty analysis. [47 U.S.C. §§501, 606(h)](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&path=%2Fprelim%40title47%2Fchapter5%2Fsubchapter5); [Title 47, Chapter 5, Subchapter VI](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&path=%2Fprelim%40title47%2Fchapter5%2Fsubchapter6).

**Section 502.** It requires a willful-and-knowing violation of a rule, regulation, restriction, or condition imposed by the FCC under Chapter 5, or a specified international rule, treaty, or convention. Its per-day fine is “in addition to any other penalties provided by law.” Section 606(h) alone does not supply a Commission rule or establish knowing violation of one. If the same facts also meet §502’s independent predicate, its text contemplates an additional penalty; charging, merger, and cumulative-sentence questions are outside this bounded statutory-text pass. [47 U.S.C. §502](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&path=%2Fprelim%40title47%2Fchapter5%2Fsubchapter5); [47 U.S.C. §606(h)](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&num=0&req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title47-section606).

## Amendment history

The official OLRC source credit traces §606 to the Communications Act of 1934 and lists amendments in 1942, 1947, and 1951, followed by the 1984 renumbering. The editorial notes distinguish two earlier subsection (h) histories: a December 1942 subsection (h) was added, then a July 1947 subsection (h) relating to wartime vessel protection was struck; the current penalties subsection (h) was added by §2 of the Act of 24 October 1951. Thus the current penalty text should not be attributed to the superseded 1942 subsection bearing the same letter. The official 2023 U.S. Code displays this history; the official 2024 Code and current OLRC text reproduce the current penalty subsection. [OLRC, 47 U.S.C. §606, Source Credit and Amendments](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&num=0&req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title47-section606); [2023 U.S. Code, §606 history](https://uscode.house.gov/download/annualhistoricalarchives/pdf/2023/2023usc47.pdf); [2024 U.S. Code, §606](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title47/pdf/USCODE-2024-title47-chap5-subchapVI-sec606.pdf).
