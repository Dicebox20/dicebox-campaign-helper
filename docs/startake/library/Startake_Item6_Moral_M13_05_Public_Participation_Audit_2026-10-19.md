## Executive finding

Bill v1.14 would create several distinct participation and accountability channels, but they do not provide the same rights. Section 201(c) expressly says the proposed Corporation is to be treated as an agency for Administrative Procedure Act (APA) and Freedom of Information Act (FOIA) purposes. If enacted as written, a strong reading is that the seven-member, Senate-confirmed Board also falls within the Government in the Sunshine Act’s covered-agency definition. Those statutes provide rulemaking comments, public observation of covered Board meetings, and access to nonexempt agency records under different conditions; neither public observation nor record access guarantees a chance to speak or influence every corporate decision. [5 U.S.C. §552](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/119-118/t5/s552/), [§552b](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/119-121/t5/s552b/), and [§553](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/119-121/t5/s553/)

The optional advisory-council language in §202(d) presents the main classification question. If the Bill creates or authorizes a standing council to advise the Corporation or Board, the better reading is that the Federal Advisory Committee Act (FACA) applies. A covered council would have charter, notice, public-meeting, and public-participation duties, subject to statutory limits. The Bill does not clearly say who establishes the council, when it exists, how it is composed, or how members of the public can submit input. This is a drafting ambiguity, not a finding about an existing entity. [5 U.S.C. §1001](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/119-121/t5/s1001/), [§1008](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/119-118/t5/s1008/), and [§1009](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/119-118/t5/s1009/)

Sections 205(c) and 205(e) add consultation duties beyond those statutory baselines. Section 205(c) requires public consultation before the affordability and rural-access plan; §205(e) requires the Board to consult affected customers and emergency-service users before adopting notice-and-review rules. Neither clause specifies a notice period, submission method, participant eligibility, required treatment of comments, or a response record. The named groups do not expressly include affected noncustomers, local governments, or international stakeholders. The phrase “public consultation” could be implemented broadly, but the text does not guarantee those groups a seat or a response.

## Findings

### 1. APA participation attaches to rulemaking, not every public-interest decision

Section 201(c) would expressly treat the Corporation as an agency for APA purposes. Under 5 U.S.C. §553, an agency generally must publish notice and give interested persons an opportunity to submit written data, views, or arguments when it conducts rulemaking subject to the section; the agency must consider relevant submissions and explain a rule’s basis and purpose. An interested person also has a statutory right to petition for issuance, amendment, or repeal of a rule. [5 U.S.C. §551(4)](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/119-121/t5/s551/) and [§553](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/119-121/t5/s553/)

That obligation does not reach every plan, operational choice, or Board discussion. Section 553 excludes specified subject areas, including military or foreign-affairs functions and certain public-property, loan, grant, benefit, or contract matters. Its notice-and-comment exception covers interpretive rules, general policy statements, and agency organization, procedure, or practice; an agency may also invoke the statutory good-cause exception with a published finding. Whether a particular §205(e) rule is a substantive rule requiring APA notice-and-comment or a procedural rule within an exception depends on its content and legal effect. The Supreme Court has confirmed that interpretive rules are exempt from notice-and-comment under the APA’s text, while the label cannot substitute for classifying the rule at issue. [5 U.S.C. §553(a)–(c)](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/119-121/t5/s553/); [Perez v. Mortgage Bankers Association, 575 U.S. 92 (2015)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-575/pdf/USREPORTS-575-92.pdf)

The Board’s §205(e) consultation duty is separately stated in the Bill. If an APA exception applies, that does not erase the Bill’s own express instruction to consult affected customers and emergency-service users. Section 205(c)’s “public consultation” requirement likewise stands as an Act-specific duty even where the plan is not itself an APA rule. The Bill should make the consultation procedures operational rather than leave these duties to implication. The Supreme Court has cautioned courts against adding procedural requirements beyond those Congress enacted in APA rulemaking, while recognizing that agencies can grant additional procedures; that principle does not prevent Congress from imposing specific consultation duties in this Bill. [Vermont Yankee Nuclear Power Corp. v. NRDC, 435 U.S. 519 (1978)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-435/pdf/USREPORTS-435-519.pdf)

### 2. The Sunshine Act likely covers the Board, but guarantees observation rather than public comment

The Sunshine Act covers an agency, as defined through FOIA, when it is headed by a collegial body whose majority is appointed by the President with Senate advice and consent. Section 201(c) supplies the FOIA-agency predicate; §202(a) provides for a seven-member Board appointed by the President with Senate confirmation. On those proposed terms, the better reading is that the Board is a Sunshine Act agency if the Bill is enacted. Section 552b defines a meeting as deliberations of at least the number of members needed to act that determine or result in joint conduct or disposition of official business. With the Bill’s four-member quorum, covered deliberations ordinarily begin at that threshold. This is a text-based inference about proposed legislation, not an agency-status ruling. [5 U.S.C. §552(f)](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/119-118/t5/s552/) and [§552b(a)](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/119-121/t5/s552b/)

The Act’s default is public observation of covered meetings, with advance public notice ordinarily at least one week before a meeting. It does not, by itself, give attendees a right to speak, submit comments, join the Board, or direct its votes. The agency may close a meeting only within statutory exemptions and after the prescribed majority vote, recorded vote, written explanation, and legal certification steps. The exemptions include properly classified information, protected commercial information, personal privacy, and premature disclosure likely to frustrate proposed agency action. A standalone Sunshine Act action can seek declaratory or injunctive relief, but a court may not invalidate Board action solely for a meeting violation in an action based only on §552b(h)(1). [5 U.S.C. §552b(b)–(h)](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/119-121/t5/s552b/)

The Board itself is a governing body, not a group whose function is to advise an agency; the Act’s meeting rules are therefore the more natural framework for Board sessions. If the Board authorizes a subdivision to act on its behalf, §552b’s definition also includes such a subdivision. The Bill does not specify remote attendance, public comment periods at Board meetings, or a meeting-participation procedure beyond observation. [5 U.S.C. §552b(a)](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/119-121/t5/s552b/); [5 U.S.C. §1001](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/119-121/t5/s1001/)

### 3. FOIA supplies a records channel, not a decision-participation right

Section 201(c) expressly treats the Corporation as an agency for FOIA purposes. FOIA generally requires an agency, on a sufficiently described request, to make agency records available to “any person,” subject to its procedural rules, fees, statutory exemptions, and exclusions. A requester may sue over agency records improperly withheld. The Supreme Court describes the threshold agency-record inquiry in terms of agency creation or acquisition plus agency control when the request is made. These rules support public understanding and informed participation, but do not require the Board to solicit or consider an individual requester’s policy views. [5 U.S.C. §552(a), (b), (f)](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/119-118/t5/s552/); [Department of Justice v. Tax Analysts, 492 U.S. 136 (1989)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-492/pdf/USREPORTS-492-136.pdf)

FOIA is not blanket access to all corporate information. It protects, among other things, properly classified material, confidential commercial information, personal privacy, law-enforcement material, and certain privileged or deliberative communications. Any reasonably segregable nonexempt portion must be released, and the amount deleted and exemption identified unless doing so would itself harm a protected interest. The statute generally uses “any person” and contains no general U.S.-citizenship qualifier in its request provision; it does specifically restrict intelligence-community agencies from releasing records under that provision to a foreign government or its representative. This textual observation does not establish that the proposed Corporation would be part of the intelligence community or that a particular foreign requester could obtain a particular record. [5 U.S.C. §552(a)(3), (b)](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/119-118/t5/s552/)

Section 206(a)’s required quarterly publication of reports, Board minutes, and material policy changes would be an affirmative Bill-specific disclosure duty. It is not equivalent to consultation or public comment. “Where lawful” redaction and identification of a legal basis are useful, but the Bill should clarify timing, record scope, partial release, protection of customer privacy and sensitive operational information, and how the publication rule interacts with Sunshine Act and FOIA exemptions. Section 206(d)’s public metrics and audit summaries also inform the public but do not create a route to propose or contest a Board policy.

### 4. FACA likely applies to a standing advisory council, but §202(d) is not explicit enough

FACA covers a council or similar group established or used to obtain advice for the President, an agency, or a federal officer, when established by statute or by the President or an agency. It excludes a committee composed wholly of full-time or permanent part-time federal officers or employees and certain National Academies committees. Bill §201(c) expressly treats the Corporation as an agency for APA purposes, and §202(d) contemplates an advisory council that may advise. If §202(d) establishes or authorizes a continuing council to advise the Corporation or Board, and the council includes non-federal members as its stakeholder language suggests, the better reading is that FACA applies. [5 U.S.C. §1001](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/119-121/t5/s1001/) and [§1003](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/119-120/t5/s1003/)

A covered council cannot meet or act before the required charter is filed. The charter identifies the council’s purpose, scope, reporting line, sponsoring agency, duties, estimated cost, and meeting frequency. FACA also generally requires open meetings, timely Federal Register notice, and permission for interested persons to attend, appear, or file statements subject to reasonable rules; advisory-committee records are publicly inspectable subject to FOIA. A meeting portion may be closed under the cross-referenced Sunshine Act exemptions through the required written determination. A covered council may advise, but unless a statute says otherwise, the decision remains with the responsible federal official or agency. [5 U.S.C. §1008](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/119-118/t5/s1008/), [§1009](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/119-118/t5/s1009/), and [§552b(c)](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/119-121/t5/s552b/)

When Congress considers legislation establishing or authorizing a committee, FACA identifies a defined purpose, balanced viewpoints and functions, independent advice, and supporting provisions as legislative-review guidelines. Bill §202(d) names employees, customers, emergency users, and affected communities, but does not define how members are selected, balance views, or avoid special-interest capture. That is a concrete design issue if Congress intends the council to be a participation mechanism. [5 U.S.C. §1004(b)–(c)](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/119-118/t5/s1004/)

The Supreme Court’s FACA decision is a meaningful contrary authority but does not establish a blanket exception for informal councils. In *Public Citizen v. Department of Justice*, the Court held FACA did not cover the Department’s solicitation of advice from the preexisting American Bar Association judicial-nominations committee, after considering FACA’s statutory context and the President’s appointment role. A council directly established or authorized by this Bill for the Corporation is materially different; the case does, however, reinforce that a one-off request for advice from an outside organization is not automatically a FACA committee. The result here depends on whether §202(d) creates a continuing body and how it is constituted and used. [Public Citizen v. Department of Justice, 491 U.S. 440 (1989)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-491/pdf/USREPORTS-491-440.pdf)

### 5. Non-U.S. stakeholders are not expressly excluded from general public channels, but receive no governance entitlement

The relevant participation terms are “interested persons,” “public,” and “any person”; the provisions do not on their face reserve APA comments, Sunshine Act observation, FOIA requests, or FACA meeting statements to U.S. citizens. That supports the inference that an overseas customer, noncustomer, local government, or other stakeholder may use an otherwise available public channel. It does not confer a council seat, voting power, guaranteed consideration of a particular submission, access to exempt or classified material, or control over U.S. corporate policy. Security, privacy, trade-secret, and meeting-closure rules continue to apply. [5 U.S.C. §552](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/119-118/t5/s552/), [§552b](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/119-121/t5/s552b/), [§553](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/119-121/t5/s553/), and [§1009](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/119-118/t5/s1009/)

Bill §207 would authorize declaratory or injunctive relief for a person adversely affected by a violation of §§203–205, subject to constitutional standing and other applicable requirements. The text could support an action over a failure to perform a §205 consultation duty where the plaintiff can show the required injury, but it does not expressly make every commenter an adversely affected person. Section 207 does not list §202(d) or §206 as covered provisions and does not provide damages. No conclusion is made about how a court would apply standing or review standards to a future dispute. (Bill v1.14 §207.)

## Adverse readings and unresolved facts

- Section 553’s default applies only to a covered agency’s rulemaking. A plan or corporate decision may fall outside “rulemaking,” and procedural-rule or other exceptions may apply. The text and effect of each future Board action will matter. ([5 U.S.C. §551(4)](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/119-121/t5/s551/); [§553](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/119-121/t5/s553/))
- Section 205(e) rules may be characterized as procedural because they govern notice, review, complaints, and restoration. Even then, the Bill’s express consultation requirement remains a separate statutory question. ([5 U.S.C. §553](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/119-121/t5/s553/))
- The Bill says an advisory council “may advise” but does not clearly say who establishes it or whether it is a continuing body. FACA coverage is likely if the Bill creates or authorizes a standing multi-stakeholder council; a series of ordinary public consultations without a committee would present a different case.
- No actual Board, advisory council, U.S. agency, customer, public meeting, policy rule, consultation, FOIA request, or stakeholder submission exists on the record reviewed.
- This audit does not resolve actual access to a specific record, eligibility for a specific council seat, the enforceability of a future consultation, or constitutional standing in a hypothetical case.

## Recommended next step

Have legislative counsel decide and state the intended participation model before a later Bill revision. The next drafting pass should specify (1) whether a standing advisory council will exist and whether FACA applies; (2) how §205(c) and §205(e) reach customers, affected noncustomers, local governments, and international stakeholders; (3) notice periods, accessible submission channels, meeting participation, comment summaries, and Board responses; (4) how public minutes and records are published with lawful redaction and segregable release; and (5) whether §207 should expressly support enforcement of the participation duties. This report supplies legal design questions only; it does not amend Bill v1.14 or record formal acceptance.
