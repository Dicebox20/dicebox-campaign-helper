## Executive findings

Official FCC orders identify **Space Exploration Holdings, LLC** as the grantee/licensee for the first-generation Starlink space-station records using call signs **S2983/S3018** and the second-generation Starlink records using **S2992/S3069**. The current public-order record includes a 2026 conditional modification grant for Gen1 and a 2026 partial grant/partial deferral for Gen2. These order-level records identify an FCC authorization holder; they do not establish who owns any satellite, whether every authorization remains effective today, or whether every requested operation is permitted.

The latest located Gen1 action is the grant, with conditions, of `SAT-MOD-20250805-00200` on 9 April 2026. The official notice identifies S2983/S3018 and a validity range through 29 March 2033. The underlying application sought operating flexibility for the existing 4,408-satellite Gen1 system and said it did not seek to increase that system’s authorized total.

The Gen2 order `DA 26-36` identifies S2992/S3069 and Space Exploration Holdings, LLC. It grants in part and defers in part, with conditions, and authorizes **15,000 Gen2 satellites** in listed orbital shells. That number is an authorization ceiling, not a count of satellites built, deployed, operational, or owned.

FCC 25-11’s earlier regulatory-fee table lists Space Exploration Holdings, LLC with all four call signs and 11,908 authorized NGSO space stations. That older fee-table figure predates the January 2026 Gen2 order and cannot be treated as a current 2026 Starlink total or a live authorization export.

Recent FCC public notices also record several temporary Gen2 Starlink authority actions. The notices retrieved do not provide enough current ICFS status and term information to determine whether each temporary authority remains effective at this cutoff. They are recorded below as docket events, not as verified current permissions.

No official FCC order or notice located in this bounded search records an assignment or transfer of control of the four Starlink space-station call signs. **That is not evidence that no such application or record exists.** The public ICFS search page was inaccessible in this retrieval; therefore, the holder inventory is incomplete, and transfer-history completeness and present-day status remain unresolved.

### Temporary-action detail

- DA 26-101 lists `SAT-STA-20260107-00013` for S3069/2992 with a 26 January 2026 action date and `SAT-STA-20260107-00014` with a 28 January 2026 action date. Both are identified as granted with conditions.
- DA 26-431 lists `SAT-STA-20260406-00153` and `SAT-STA-20260406-00154`, associated with S3069/2992, as granted with conditions on 30 April 2026.
- DA 26-582 lists `SAT-STA-20260527-00215`, associated with S2992/3069, as granted with conditions on 5 June 2026. It separately records `SAT-STA-20260401-00150` as an ICFS-listed grant reflecting operations pursuant to 47 C.F.R. §1.62.
- The earlier 14 October ICFS sample also listed temporary Gen2 proceedings `SAT-STA-20250929-00309` and `SAT-STA-20250929-00310`. Their current status was not revalidated here.

These notice entries demonstrate why a current ICFS docket view is necessary: a filed application, a conditional grant, an STA, and an authorization with an unknown end date cannot be collapsed into one “current license” status.

## Transfer-record findings and scope boundaries

The accessible orders and notices reviewed for these call signs did not disclose a Starlink space-station assignment or change-of-control decision. Because a live ICFS query and complete record export were unavailable, this is a **search limitation**, not a finding that no filing or transfer exists. The previous FCC transfer-continuity report already analyzes 47 U.S.C. §310(d) and 47 C.F.R. §§25.117 and 25.119; this inventory does not repeat or revise that legal analysis.

A significant near-match is DA 26-471, which approves an assignment involving EchoStar AWS-4, AWS-H and unpaired AWS-3 spectrum licenses and several earth-station licenses to SpaceX through a two-step transaction. The transaction is not identified as an assignment or transfer of the Starlink space-station authorizations under S2983/S3018/S2992/S3069. It is excluded from the Starlink space-station inventory and does not establish transfer of those authorizations.

The same FCC applicant name appears on other, non-Starlink space-station matters. SAT-LOA-20241218-00288 / S3216 concerns Starship spacecraft and variants; SAT-LOA-20260108-00016 concerns the separately named Orbital Data Center system. Those records are not counted as Starlink. FCC applicant identity alone does not make a distinct satellite system part of the Starlink perimeter.

This report does **not** establish: a complete FCC license or authorization census; current legal holder at every relevant date; satellite, terminal, ground-station, contract, intellectual-property, or other asset title; all transfer filings; complete spectrum rights; operational permission in any country; fleet size or deployment; or compliance with FCC authorization conditions. It makes no deorbit-cost, valuation, or operating-compliance finding.
