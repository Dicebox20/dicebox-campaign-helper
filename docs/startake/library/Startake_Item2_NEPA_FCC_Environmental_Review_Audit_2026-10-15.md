## Result

**Neither 'buying Starlink always triggers a fresh EIS' nor 'existing FCC satellite authorizations eliminate all environmental review' is supported.** NEPA review turns on an identified proposed **federal agency action**, whether it is a **major Federal action** subject to sufficient federal control and discretion, the threshold exceptions/categorical exclusions, and reasonably foreseeable environmental effects. Under **42 USC §§4332, 4336, 4336e**, an agency must establish the action and appropriate level of review; an EA/EIS is not mechanically required for every federally associated transaction. An enacted acquisition statute with nondiscretionary vesting might create different NEPA and judicial-review questions than discretionary agency purchases, federal subsidies, new ground-station construction, station modifications, or launches. **No actual Starlink acquisition statute/agency decision is available to classify.**

FCC environmental processing is a **separate review interface** for applications and facility changes. **47 CFR §§1.1306–1.1308** generally categorically exclude actions outside the §1.1307 triggers, while site-specific conditions (wilderness/refuge, threatened species, protected historic properties, floodplain or other special sites), human exposure to RF radiation, and extraordinary circumstances can require an environmental assessment. An FCC approval, case-specific NEPA analysis, and FAA review of rocket launches are not substitutes for asset title, other agency permissions or environmental compliance in other jurisdictions. The January 9, 2026 FCC Starlink Gen2 order **DA 26-36** indicates the agency addressed environmental concerns and referred to FAA launch review, but that order neither resolves environmental review for a future acquisition transaction nor proves that every future launch, ground facility and changed operation is covered.

## Opposing interpretations and conditional analysis

- **Overbroad pro-acquisition claim:** 'The satellite network is already licensed; NEPA cannot delay federal acquisition.' **Response:** existing grants relate to specified actions; new discretionary governmental purchases, ground buildouts, launch decisions and modifications must each be screened for NEPA applicability and other environmental obligations. A qualifying categorical exclusion may resolve a given action without EA/EIS; that is not a universal exemption.
- **Overbroad anti-acquisition claim:** 'Federal purchase requires a constellation-wide EIS in all circumstances.' **Response:** NEPA has a statutory major-action threshold, exceptions and categorical exclusions. No concrete action/agency/cost/site and environmental finding exists to classify; outcomes vary by the acquisition structure and implementation stages.
- **Ownership is not permission:** Title transfer, FCC licence modification, FAA launch oversight, environmental consultation, private land use, foreign gateways and spectrum rights are separate gates. NEPA itself does not authorize purchase, supply compensation, guarantee operations or determine treaty obligations.
- **Current-law caveat:** NEPA and FCC procedural rules have been amended over time. Any transaction-stage report must compare current official text and agency-specific regulations with proposed/actual legislation; an older CFR or an agency-indexed order is not sufficient to certify later regulations.
