## Executive finding

A public Starlink operator's legal duties are **structure- and statute-dependent**, not created merely by declaring the network "public." A congressionally chartered government corporation can be subject to public-records and constitutional restrictions, financial reporting and auditing. However, the *specific* design must reconcile the prospective charter, the statutory definitions/coverage of each law, contractors, foreign customers, classified exceptions, and direct network-operation policies. Three important qualifications emerged: (1) the **Government Corporation Control Act** has a defined statutory list of covered corporations, so a newly designed entity needs its specific coverage addressed, not assumed; (2) **FOIA** is a records-disclosure regime with national-security and commercial confidentiality exemptions, not unrestricted disclosure of subscriber data or network secrets; and (3) **Privacy Act** coverage is narrower than universal consumer privacy, including the statutory definition of covered "individual" and the rule for agency "systems of records." Both public protection and risks of government abuse remain live considerations.

## 2. Scope-correct legal assessment and contrary views

### Federal charter versus agency-like ownership

- **Verified legal anchor:** Section 9102 requires specific legal authorization for an agency to establish/acquire a corporation that acts as an agency. Chapter 91's enumerated government corporations should be evaluated against the **proposed** operator's form; chapter coverage cannot simply be assumed from a hypothetical future name.
- **Necessary drafting inference, not enacted law:** Congress might create a corporation, place an acquired business within an existing agency, or procure public-service guarantees from a private operator. Each route yields different audit, records, workforce, procurement and judicial-review consequences. The required statutory text is *not yet available*.
- **Counterargument preserved:** Commercial-scale satellite operations may need independent hiring, contracting, engineering and service flexibility. A heavily prescriptive charter could inhibit responsiveness. Conversely, broad autonomy could undermine accountability; both design risks must be compared with evidence.

### Transparency is not unrestricted disclosure

- **Verified legal anchor:** FOIA's agency definition includes government corporations, but exemptions matter. Particularly sensitive are customer-identifying data, trade secrets, cyber architecture, satellite-control credentials and properly classified information.
- **Design inference:** Publish annual service-equity/outage statistics, budgets, aggregate law-enforcement access metrics, procurement audits and board conflicts **while segregating exempt security/customer records**. Do not require publication of secrets to achieve accountability.
- **Open test:** Define FOIA record custodians, classification/redaction procedures, appeals, IG access and nonpublic secure review for operational incidents.

### Privacy and constitutional expression

- **Verified legal anchor:** Section 552a applies to agency-controlled systems of records and a legally defined set of individuals; it does not supply a global standalone privacy code. Lebron provides a strong constitutional warning against governmental viewpoint suppression via a nominally corporate operator when the Court's conditions are met.
- **Design inference:** Require viewpoint-neutral access rules subject to narrowly tailored lawful security controls, separated content/network functions, independent complaint/appeal mechanisms, and a clear statutory prohibition on political interference. Differentiate ordinary routing/security abuse prevention from discriminatory refusal of public communications; obtain counsel's service-specific First Amendment analysis.
- **Open test:** Identify global user categories, data location and lawful interception requests; evaluate foreign legal regimes, government demand review, U.S. constitutional rights and any remaining private-provider rules. Government ownership can increase oversight **and** create surveillance/censorship risks.

### Financial and cyber accountability

- **Verified anchor:** 31 U.S.C. §§ 9105–9106 and GAO 2025 Green Book/2026 FISCAM supply a framework of financial reporting, independent auditing and controls where applicable.
- **Design inference:** Mandate independent penetration/cutover tests; a confidential classified-security review; disaster-recovery exercises; software update separation from Starshield; incident/public outage metrics; independent audit and IG authority over contractors.
- **Open test:** No Starlink audit of fleet command and control, software source, government contractor permissions, employee clearance segregation or operational continuity plan was reviewed. No blanket FISMA certification is claimed.
