## Finding

The proposed bill already contains unusually direct language addressing both statutory questions. Section 201(a) itself establishes the named, wholly owned federal corporation. If enacted, that satisfies the specific-law authorization demanded by 31 U.S.C. § 9102; a separate agency-created charter or generic implied power is unnecessary on this design. Section 201(c) then expressly directs that, for Chapter 91, the Corporation be treated as a wholly owned Government corporation. That entity-specific rule likely supplies Chapter 91 coverage even though the current § 9101(3) list does not name the proposed Corporation. Section 206(b)'s separate reference to a Chapter 91 audit confirms the bill's intended coverage. (Integrated Working Bill v1.2 §§ 201(a), (c), 206(b); [31 U.S.C. § 9101](https://usc-cdn.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&num=0&req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title31-section9101) and [§ 9102](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=%28title%3A31+section%3A9102+edition%3Aprelim%29); [GAO B-278820, at 4–5](https://www.gao.gov/assets/b-278820.pdf).)

**Recommended drafting disposition:** retain §§ 201(a) and 201(c). For codification clarity, add a conforming amendment to § 9101(3) naming the Corporation. This is a clarity improvement, not a demonstrated defect in the existing bill text. No controlling decision was identified that decides whether a Chapter 91-specific deeming clause, without a parallel addition to § 9101(3), is sufficient for a newly created entity; counsel should confirm that interaction before bill freeze.

## Question and boundaries

The question is whether the proposed charter: (1) specifically authorizes establishment of a corporation to act as an agency, as § 9102 requires; and (2) places the new corporation within Chapter 91's wholly owned Government corporation category, which § 9101 defines through named entities.

This pass does not audit the separate acquisition authority, title, compensation, valuation, appropriations, or operating powers; does not revisit Board or CEO appointment/removal law; and does not assess full Chapter 91 implementation. Other SpaceX businesses and Starshield are outside the presumed perimeter. No bill text was changed.

## Governing text and proposed charter

### 1. Chapter 91 classification

Section 9101(1) defines a “Government corporation” for Chapter 91 as a mixed-ownership or wholly owned Government corporation. Sections 9101(2) and (3) then define those categories through enumerated entities. The current wholly owned list ends at subparagraph (P), the International Clean Energy Foundation; it does not name the proposed United States Public Satellite Communications Corporation. The text is not framed as a general ownership test under which any corporation with federal ownership automatically becomes a Chapter 91 corporation. ([31 U.S.C. § 9101](https://usc-cdn.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&num=0&req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title31-section9101); official preliminary 2026 Code text states it contains laws in effect on 28 September 2026.)

### 2. Specific-law requirement

Section 9102 states that an agency may establish or acquire a corporation to act as an agency only by or under a federal law specifically authorizing that action. It restricts agency-created corporations; it is not itself acquisition, condemnation, or compensation authority. ([31 U.S.C. § 9102](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=%28title%3A31+section%3A9102+edition%3Aprelim%29).)

### 3. Bill v1.2

Section 201(a) says: “There is established a federally chartered, wholly owned Government corporation, the United States Public Satellite Communications Corporation,” and states that all ownership interests are held by the United States. Section 201(c) provides that, “[f]or purposes of chapter 91 of title 31,” the Corporation “shall be treated as a wholly owned Government corporation.” It separately treats the Corporation as an agency for the APA, FOIA, Privacy Act, federal records law, and federal information-security requirements. Section 206(b) requires annual financial statements to be independently audited under Chapter 91. The proposed bill therefore contains both an express act of creation and a Chapter 91-specific classification instruction. ## Analysis

### A. Section 9102 is satisfied by the proposed Act itself

If Congress enacts § 201(a), the Corporation will be established directly by a federal law that names the entity, fixes federal ownership, and defines its mission and operating authority. That is more specific than an agency relying on broad ancillary authority to incorporate a private entity. Section 9102 does not appear to require a second statute when the enactment itself directly establishes the corporation. ([31 U.S.C. § 9102](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=%28title%3A31+section%3A9102+edition%3Aprelim%29).)

GAO applied § 9102 in B-278820, concluding that the FCC lacked specific authority to direct another organization to create private corporations that would carry out FCC program functions. GAO reasoned that generic Communications Act authority did not supply the required specific authorization and that an agency could not avoid the rule by directing another organization to act as incorporator. (GAO, *Comments on the FCC’s Implementation of Section 254(h) of the Communications Act of 1934*, B-278820, at 4–5 (Feb. 10, 1998).) The important distinction is that the proposed bill does not rely on a general agency power or a private third-party incorporation workaround: Congress itself establishes the named Corporation. GAO's decision is an agency legal opinion on different facts, not a judicial holding on this bill.

Section 9102 should not be read as acquisition authority. Any transfer of Starlink assets, licenses, contracts, or equity remains dependent on the bill's separate acquisition provisions, valid permissions, compensation rules, and other applicable law.

### B. Section 201(c) likely supplies Chapter 91 treatment

Under current § 9101, federal ownership alone does not place a new corporation in the enumerated wholly owned category. Standing alone, § 201(a)'s “wholly owned Government corporation” label could leave the relationship to § 9101 unclear because the proposed name is absent from § 9101(3). ([31 U.S.C. § 9101(1), (3)](https://usc-cdn.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&num=0&req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title31-section9101).)

Section 201(c), however, goes further: it directs that this named Corporation be treated as a wholly owned Government corporation **for purposes of Chapter 91**. The better reading is that Congress is specifically extending Chapter 91 treatment to the new entity, not merely declaring that it is wholly owned as a matter of corporate ownership. Section 206(b)'s Chapter 91 audit clause reinforces that reading. This is a statutory-text inference from the proposed language; no direct judicial decision on this exact combination of § 9101's enumerated list and a later entity-specific deeming clause was identified.

The strongest counterpoint is that § 9101 defines the statutory category by a list, and § 201(c) does not amend that list. A reader could therefore ask whether “shall be treated as” independently adds the Corporation to the § 9101(3) definition, or whether a conforming amendment is required. Because Congress may legislate a specific rule for a named entity, § 201(c) likely controls for this Corporation if enacted. Adding the name to § 9101(3) would remove the drafting and codification question without changing the intended policy.

### C. Coverage has downstream consequences

If § 201(c) applies, Chapter 91 treatment is consequential. The chapter contains business-type budget and congressional budget provisions, financial-statement audits, management reports, accounting rules, and restrictions concerning obligations. Sections 9103 and 9104 address wholly owned corporation budgets and congressional action; §§ 9105 and 9106 address audits and management reports; § 9108 addresses obligations. Section 9109 supplies a separate route under which a wholly owned corporation may be excluded from the chapter after a presidential recommendation and congressional approval. ([31 U.S.C. § 9103](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&num=0&req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title31-section9103), [§ 9104](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&num=0&req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title31-section9104), [§ 9105](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&num=0&req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title31-section9105), and [§ 9106](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&num=0&req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title31-section9106); [§ 9108](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&num=0&req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title31-section9108); [§ 9109](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&num=0&req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title31-section9109).) This report does not determine the Corporation’s detailed compliance with each provision or whether a later § 9109 process would be sought.

## Drafting recommendation

Keep Bill v1.2 §§ 201(a) and 201(c). For a clean match between the bill and § 9101’s enumerated definition, consider adding a conforming amendment along these lines:

> **CONFORMING AMENDMENT TO CHAPTER 91 OF TITLE 31.—** Section 9101(3) of title 31, United States Code, is amended by adding at the end:  
> “(Q) The United States Public Satellite Communications Corporation established by section 201 of the Starlink and Stripes Act.”

This candidate is based on § 9101(3)'s current final subparagraph (P) as displayed in the preliminary 2026 Code. Recheck the list and lettering against the Code in effect when the bill is introduced. Retain § 201(c)'s express Chapter 91 treatment even if the conforming amendment is adopted. No separate § 9102 clause appears necessary where § 201(a) itself directly establishes the Corporation by statute.
