# Item 7 / original legal scope 15.02 — Transition-service levels and funded exit plan

**Date:** 18 October 2026 UTC  
**Claim:** `STARTAKE-20261018-WORK-LEGAL-15-02-TRANSITION-SLA-7c4e91`  
**Disposition:** Bounded direct-primary-source audit complete; §401(c) was narrowly amended in Bill v1.14. Formal Item 7 acceptance, original scope 15.02 acceptance, parent-topic acceptance, and congressional readiness remain open.

## Finding

Bill v1.13 §401(c) already required a transition-services agreement to specify measurable service levels, price, duration, minimum staffing, support access, enforceable remedies, and a funded exit plan. Sections 401(a) and 401(d), 402(b) and (d)–(e), and 303(b) supplied important dependencies: a technical and transition plan, a critical-service register, approved cutover tolerances and recovery objectives, site/service tests and alternatives, and a continuity-reserve decision record. Those provisions did not require §401(c)'s service levels to carry a common service-specific measurement, reporting, exclusions, escalation, and records schedule. They also did not expressly define the cost/funding disclosure needed to make the §401(c) exit plan auditable.

Current public standards support making those terms concrete without choosing their values. Federal performance-based service-contract rules call for measurable standards and a method of assessment; federal quality-assurance planning identifies the work to monitor and the method; and the optional federal continuity clause addresses training, responsibility-transfer dates, staffing continuity and phase-out costs in particular circumstances. GAO cost-estimating guidance supports documenting the estimate's baseline, assumptions, data, method and risk. The public Starlink Priority SLA illustrates how measurement windows, outage definitions, exclusions and remedies interact, but applies only to specified plans and terminals. It does not establish a suitable mission threshold, agency obligation, or transition term.

I therefore amended only proposed §401(c) in Bill v1.14. The amendment requires a schedule for each covered service and a defined exit-plan funding record; it ties critical-service schedules to §402(b), applies §303's available-funds rules, and bars the plan from describing unavailable future funds as funded. The bill states no uptime percentage, service price, staffing count, cost estimate, duration or appropriation. Those values remain for the relevant customer, operator and authorized officials to establish from verified obligations, missions and cost records before the related obligation. The amendment creates no appropriation, vendor-access power, supplier takeover authority, service-independence finding or new remedy outside the agreement.

## Amendment and drafting choice

Bill v1.14 changes only operative §401(c). In addition to retaining all existing subjects, it requires each covered service to have a schedule with scope; measure, unit, period, source and method; exclusions and outage/degradation treatment; reporting/records; incident escalation and response/restoration/cure periods; and remedies tied to measured performance. For critical services, the schedule is linked to §402(b) tolerances and separates provider-network metrics from site/end-to-end mission outcomes. The agreement must make the relevant records available to the Secretary and authorized assessors subject to §206(a) and other applicable law.

The exit-plan content must state triggers/notice, deliverables, lawfully available access, training/support, responsible parties, transfer milestones, staffing qualifications/coverage when needed, and completion/acceptance/continuity criteria. It must document exit/termination/transfer estimates and basis; identify legally available funding, account, purpose and period for committed obligations; and separately show contingent or future-appropriation dependencies. An estimate, reserve designation, or expected appropriation is not budget authority. Section 303 continues to control actual obligations; no new funds, availability period, or debt authority are created.

The amendment intentionally selects no uptime, latency, availability, restore-time, price, term, or staff-count value. It does not incorporate the Starlink public SLA, FAR contract clause, or its 90-day maximum by reference. It does not presume vendor cooperation, transferable labor, proprietary access rights, a successor supplier, or public/operator independence. Those matters must be supported by contract terms, existing law, verified dependency records and witnessed tests before the relevant obligation or cutover.
