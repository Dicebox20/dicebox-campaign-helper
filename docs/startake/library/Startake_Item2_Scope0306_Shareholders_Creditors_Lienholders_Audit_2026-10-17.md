# Item 2 Original Scope 03.06 — Shareholders, Creditors and Lienholders Audit

**17 October 2026 UTC. Research pass complete; formal verification and acceptance remain open.**

## Result

Bill v1.9 already protected creditor and claimant interests at a high level: §101 required an owner-by-interest schedule and said the schedule itself did not release a lien; §102(b) did not bind a counterparty without agreement; §104(1) required creditor treatment to be resolved; and §§105(d), 106(c) addressed notice, judicial entitlement, disputed proceeds and double recovery. Two public-record facts supported a narrow drafting clarification: the current amended revolving credit agreement contains a Starlink-specific restriction on transferring Starlink or material Starlink assets outside the SpaceX group, and the latest reviewed quarterly filing disclosed other equipment-backed financing without identifying the collateral by business or asset.

Bill [v1.10](/Startake/The_Starlink_and_Stripes_Act_Integrated_Working_Bill_v1.10_2026-10-17.md) therefore clarifies only §§101(c), 104(1) and 106(c). A schedule cannot satisfy a required contract notice, consent or waiver or release/subordinate a lien; the closing condition expressly covers any action required by governing instruments; and a condemnation court determines entitlement and priority under applicable law. These are conditional safeguards: they apply only when governing law or an instrument already requires the action. The amendment creates no general lender or shareholder veto, lien priority rule, automatic release or transfer, new acquisition authority, or compulsory share transfer.

## Findings

### Negotiated transfers and creditor contracts

The Act authorizes negotiated purchase, lease, license or voluntary-entity equity acquisition in §102(a). Section 102(b) says transition agreements must be made by agreement and do not obligate a counterparty without one. Section 105(c) excludes compulsory transfer of corporate shares. These provisions preserve negotiation and shareholder/entity-law questions for a specific target; no target corporation, share class, stockholder agreement, cap table, transfer restriction or proposed vote was identified by this bounded review.

The May 19, 2026 Amended and Restated Credit Agreement filed as SEC Exhibit 10.10 contains a negative-lien covenant (§7.01), identifies a Schedule 7.01 titled “Existing Liens,” and includes a Starlink-specific fundamental-changes covenant (§7.03(c)). On its face, §7.03(c) restricts disposing of Starlink or a Material Starlink Asset to a person outside the borrower/subsidiary group and restricts a spin-off or other transaction that would place Starlink or a Material Starlink Asset outside that group, subject to specified exceptions. The same agreement defines Starlink Financing Assets broadly for financing purposes. The filed agreement is a financing instrument, not an ownership chart or definitive acquisition perimeter. The schedule's existence does not establish which specific assets, if any, are listed or whether a particular asset is encumbered. ([SEC EDGAR, May 19, 2026 Amended and Restated Credit Agreement, §§1.01, 7.01, 7.03(c), Schedule 7.01](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1181412/000162828026039276/exhibit1010-sx1a1.htm))

The SEC's Form 10-Q for the quarter ended 30 June 2026 says the March 2026 $20 billion bridge loan was repaid in full in June using proceeds from the SpaceX Notes. It reports a $5 billion amended revolving credit facility, no borrowings under that facility during the quarter, and no borrowings outstanding at quarter end; the facility's May 2026 amendment extended its stated maturity to 19 May 2031. Accordingly, the bridge loan is historical for this review, while the unused revolving commitment and its covenants remained relevant on the reported date. The same Form 10-Q identifies $25 billion of senior unsecured SpaceX Notes and reports a covenant restricting additional liens above 7.5% of consolidated assets, plus a fundamental-changes covenant. It also reports other financing arrangements generally collateralized by specific machinery/equipment and separately discusses failed sale-leaseback debt associated with AI infrastructure. It does not provide a Starlink-by-asset collateral map. ([SEC Form 10-Q, 30 June 2026, Note 9 and liquidity discussion](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1181412/000162828026052535/spcx-20260630.htm))

**Inference from those public instruments:** a negotiated transfer, entity separation, asset pledge, or transfer of control could engage existing lender covenants, notice requirements, default provisions, or consent/waiver procedures. Whether it does depends on the precise seller, buyer, assets, equity, timing, applicable definitions, exceptions, outstanding instruments and any executed consent or waiver. The sources do not establish that a proposed transaction exists, that any Starlink asset is subject to a lien, that consent is invariably required, or what lender threshold or payoff amount would apply. The Bill should require transaction-specific resolution, not presume a covenant breach or dictate a new lienholder priority.

The public filings identify types of creditor restrictions and financing, not the actual legal and beneficial owner of each Starlink asset, the debtor named in each filing, the collateral schedule, lien perfection, priority, payoff, release, guarantee, share-transfer approval or closing deliverable. The contents and present effect of the relevant Schedule 7.01 and asset-specific financing documents were not established here. No search of every state UCC, real-property, intellectual-property or account-control record is claimed. Those facts remain an Item 1 / transaction-specific diligence gate.

### Federal procedure and distribution of proceeds

Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 71.1 applies to federal eminent-domain proceedings involving real or personal property. It requires joinder of known or reasonably ascertainable persons claiming interests before a compensation hearing; a defendant must state the nature and extent of the claimed interest, and a defendant may present compensation evidence and share in an award even if the defendant did not previously appear or answer. The rule also directs courts to expedite distribution of deposited funds and ascertainment/payment of compensation. These rules provide procedural notice and participation protections; they do not themselves state a universal relative-priority formula for every lien or security interest. ([U.S. Courts, Federal Rules of Civil Procedure, Rule 71.1(c), (e), (j)](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/restyled_federal_rules_of_civil_procedure.pdf))

Title 40 U.S.C. §3114 is limited to a declaration of taking for land. It vests the specified land interest and just-compensation right upon filing and deposit, permits the court on application to pay deposited money to interested parties, and authorizes just and equitable orders concerning encumbrances and liens. The bill already limits use of Title 40 procedures to qualifying real-property interests in §105(f), bars a personal-property quick take in §105(e), and applies Rule 71.1 in §105(d). The existing law therefore supplies a real-property lien-order tool and a general condemnation procedure, while §106(c) states the proposed allocation safeguard. ([40 U.S.C. §3114(b)–(d), official House U.S. Code](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&num=0&req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title40-section3114))

### Bill review and narrow revision

The existing text [v1.9](/Startake/The_Starlink_and_Stripes_Act_Integrated_Working_Bill_v1.9_2026-10-17.md) was substantively protective but did not expressly say that an acquisition schedule cannot satisfy or waive a private covenant, or that the closing record must show the specific notices, consents, waivers, payoffs, releases or assumptions required by the governing financing and equity instruments. Section 106(c) instructed a court to determine entitlement among owners and encumbrance holders, but did not expressly state that relative priority is determined under applicable law. These are clarity and implementation gaps; they do not show that the bill already had power to release liens or override contracts.

The v1.10 changes are:

1. **Section 101(c):** specifies that listing an interest does not transfer title, release/subordinate a lien, or satisfy a required regulatory or contractual notice, consent or waiver.
2. **Section 104(1):** adds lien priorities and requires completion of any notice, consent, waiver, payoff, release, assumption or other transaction action already required by applicable law or the governing instrument.
3. **Section 106(c):** directs the court to determine entitlement and priority under applicable law while retaining the disputed-proceeds protection, access to undisputed deposits, and bar on double recovery.

No amendment was made to §102 or the condemnation notice procedure. This pass does not create compulsory share acquisition, a right to force a lender to consent, a federal payment waterfall, a lien-free transfer, a presumption that existing liens are invalid, or a new taking. The edits preserve contractual and property rights unless a later, specific law expressly changes them.
