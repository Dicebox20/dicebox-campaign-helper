## Principal findings

The Contract Disputes Act of 1978 (CDA), now 41 U.S.C. chapter 71, supplies a dispute-resolution path for covered **federal procurement contracts**, not an authorization to purchase or condemn Starlink's satellites, corporate shares, spectrum operating permissions, software or infrastructure. It would become relevant to existing Starlink-related government service/support contracts only after establishing actual contractual coverage, parties, claim accrual and incorporated terms. Neither the preceding FAR termination research nor the general Tucker Act inverse-takings research establishes an actual Starlink CDA claim.

* FAR 33.206 and 41 U.S.C. §7103 generally require a contractor's **written claim to the contracting officer** within six years of accrual, subject to applicable statutory/regulatory qualifications. FAR 33.207 requires certification of **contractor claims exceeding $100,000**. A written claim, certification, and the contracting officer's final decision are procedural questions distinct from negotiated payment for a business.
* Under 41 U.S.C. §7104, a contractor generally may appeal a contracting officer's decision to an agency **board of contract appeals within 90 days of receipt**, or alternatively file a **de novo** action in the U.S. Court of Federal Claims **within 12 months** of receipt. The TVA-specific district court exception requires separate treatment. These alternative paths should not be conflated with the Tucker Act's general six-year claims period.
* Under 41 U.S.C. §7107, a contractor may generally seek Federal Circuit review of an agency-board decision **within 120 days** of receipt. Legal conclusions and factual findings receive distinct statutory review treatment.
* FAR 33.213 and 41 U.S.C. §7103(g) address agency power to require contract performance while a dispute is pending; this is not proof that Starlink would remain technically or commercially able to operate during acquisition, or that the government can impose an indefinite duty beyond the covered contract.
* FAR 33.204 promotes negotiated resolution and voluntary alternative dispute resolution, whereas FAR 33.209 flags fraud-related limits; neither provision resolves third-party customer, creditor, foreign-government or satellite-property rights.

## Contradictory readings preserved

1. **Claim: the CDA lets the federal government seize or retain Starlink itself during a service dispute.** Incorrect. The CDA regulates covered procurement contract claims; any company acquisition requires separate enacted authority, acquisition instruments and constitutional treatment.
2. **Claim: a terminated Starlink contract means an immediate 90-day federal court filing deadline.** Incorrect. Ninety days ordinarily governs *agency board* review of a contracting officer's decision; a direct Court of Federal Claims action generally has a different 12-month route. The original written-claim timing has a six-year rule subject to qualifications.
3. **Claim: an asset purchaser automatically acquires Starlink's CDA claims, or government service can be uninterrupted while litigation is pending.** Unsupported. Actual claims/contract-party status, FAR 42.1204 novation where applicable, performance clauses, remedies and transition capability require documentary proof.
4. **Claim: termination settlement entitlement is automatically identical to Fifth Amendment compensation for all Starlink assets.** Incorrect. Covered procurement settlement and independent property-taking claims require separate legal and financial analysis.
