## Research finding

An agency's right to terminate an **existing service contract** does not confer substantive authority to acquire a contractor's satellite fleet, equity, IP or communications network. Termination, assignment/novation, asset purchase and compulsory acquisition are different legal and economic events. Under the contractually incorporated provisions, the government may terminate for convenience and owe a settlement for previously performed work and permitted costs; an improper default/for-cause termination may be converted into convenience treatment. Termination does not itself establish continuity of broadband service for public safety, military or civilian users, and could instead interrupt service if replacement contracts and handover are not independently arranged.

## Transaction scenarios, contrary cases and obligations

1. **Commercial broadband contract terminated for convenience:** Confirm whether FAR 52.212-4 applies; identify completed service proportion, permitted reasonable termination charges, payments, notice and continuity duties. A blanket assertion that the government owes the entire remaining subscription value would be unsourced.
2. **Noncommercial fixed-price equipment/support contract terminated:** If 52.249-2 actually applies, account separately for delivered/accepted supplies, terminated work costs, subcontractor settlements, limited profit, loss-contract adjustments, inventoried government property and proposal/appeal deadlines. Do **not** equate government title to termination work products with title to an unrelated operating satellite constellation.
3. **Termination for cause/default:** Confirm specific failure, required notices/opportunity to cure, excusable delay, contractual remedies and wrongful-termination conversion. Default is not an acquisition shortcut.
4. **Novation rather than termination:** FAR 42.1204 already separately researched; novation may preserve existing contracts where permitted. This pass does not assert any actual Starlink novation or transfer of contracts.
5. **Statutory business purchase/condemnation:** Requires substantive enacted authorization, appropriations, property rights and constitutional compensation independent of a procurement-contract termination clause. Government may still need bridge contracts, spectrum permissions and software access.
