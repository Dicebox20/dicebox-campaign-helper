## Findings — evidence and limits

1. **Equipment sale versus rental must not be conflated.** Starlink's operator-published Terms of Service in publicly indexed jurisdiction-specific documents state that title to a purchased Starlink Kit and accessories transfers to the customer at delivery; rented kits remain Starlink property without title transfer. These establish a contract model in those published countries, **not a verified current Canadian contract**. For the acquisition inventory, purchased customer-owned terminals cannot simply be booked as company-owned assets, while rented kits may be company-retained assets, subject to proof of the actual Canadian owner and customer order. [S01–S03]
2. **Embedded software is a separate interest.** The operator documents describe software copies and updates as licensed, not sold, using nonexclusive, limited, revocable and nontransferable terms. Ownership of physical equipment therefore cannot by itself establish a right to acquire software intellectual property, source code, sublicensing authority or a going-concern software licence. The identity of the Canadian software licensor and any change-of-control terms remain unverified. [S01–S03]
3. **Customer transfer is conditional.** The operator documents allow certain customer-initiated transfers for purchased equipment subject to standing, capacity and waiting-period requirements; rented equipment does not carry the same transfer path. A customer transfer procedure is **not** evidence that Starlink may assign an entire Canadian subscriber book or network contract to a public acquirer without customer notice, consent or other governing requirements. [S01–S03]
4. **Country mismatch is critical.** The retrieved operator source `DOC-1354-58366-77` expressly names Ecuador's **Starlink Ecuador STAREC C. Ltda.**; `DOC-1119-56469-78` expressly names Irish **Starlink Internet Services Limited** and Estonia; `DOC-1540-49184-72` addresses Argentina-specific pricing. None can establish **which legal entity signs Canadian retail contracts**, whether that entity is SpaceX Canada Corp. (formerly Tibro Canada Corp.), Space Exploration Technologies Corp., or another affiliate. The existing CRTC 2019/2020 dockets and Canadian corporate registry evidence establish regulatory/corporate identities, not executed Canadian subscriber-contract counterparties. [S01–S05]
5. **No actual Canadian executed contract or inventory retrieved.** We did not obtain a Canadian-country-selector authenticated current ToS, Canadian order/invoice for retail or business plans, reseller terms, serial-number kit ownership ledger, rental receivables, software ownership/licence agreement, source-code rights, or representative executed enterprise/service-level agreement. Nor is there a Canadian entity-specific assignment/novation clause or customer notification matrix in the material reviewed. The asset and liabilities schedule for a hypothetical Starlink-only acquisition remains unverified. [S01–S05]
6. **Perimeter and remedies.** These operator policies support an **analytical classification** of possible property interests (customer-owned sold terminal; operator-owned rental terminal if proved; separately licensed software and ongoing service rights). They do not establish the claimant against compensation, governing law of each Canadian agreement, owned inventory quantity/value, enforceability of an assignment, or inclusion of noncommercial SpaceX/Starshield assets.
