## Executive findings

The Worker Adjustment and Retraining Notification Act (WARN), 29 U.S.C. chapter 23, can impose advance notice duties when a **covered business enterprise** orders a qualifying plant closing or mass layoff. This is a condition-specific employment-protection requirement, **not authority to condemn, purchase, or transfer Starlink property**. The WARN inquiry depends on the actual employing legal person, covered domestic employment site(s), worker counts, projected employment losses, sale date and who orders any affected closure or layoff. None of those Starlink-specific factual predicates was authenticated in this pass.

**Sale-of-business rule:** Under 29 U.S.C. §2101(b)(1), the seller ordinarily bears WARN notice responsibility for qualifying events **up to and including** the effective date of sale; afterward the purchaser bears it. Non-part-time employees working for the seller on the effective date are deemed employees of the buyer **immediately after** that date for WARN purposes. This deemed succession prevents treating a seamless sale as a technical termination, but does **not independently create a public-sector job, require permanent retention, convey a collective bargaining agreement, or extinguish liability for subsequent qualifying layoffs**. The exact closing date, transfer structure and potential same-day reduction matter.

**Who is covered:** The employer threshold generally is 100 or more qualifying non-part-time workers, or 100 or more total employees collectively working 4,000 hours weekly excluding overtime. A plant closing generally involves 50 or more non-part-time employment losses at a single site, while a mass layoff generally requires 50–499 qualifying losses representing at least 33% of active workers, or 500+ losses without the 33% requirement, within the relevant counting window. A 60-calendar-day advance notice ordinarily goes to affected employees/representatives, the state dislocated-worker unit, and local chief elected official; detailed exceptions and aggregation rules exist. A hypothetical purchase with no qualifying employment losses **is not automatically a WARN event**.

**Public-entity distinction:** DOL identifies **regular federal, state, local, and tribal public-service agencies** as outside WARN's business-enterprise definition, while separately governed, independently managed public/quasi-public businesses conducting commercial activities can be covered based on their factual organization and functions. A federally chartered telecommunications corporation must therefore be analyzed separately from a normal federal agency; federal ownership alone neither proves coverage nor exclusion. The acquiring entity's personnel statutes, labor relations, civil-service and security rules require separate review, and no governmental hiring obligation is inferred from WARN alone.

**Remedies:** Under §2104, a violating covered employer may owe statutory back pay and benefits for each day of violation, up to 60 days, subject to offsets and defenses; there is also a limited local-government-notice penalty. The Act provides civil judicial remedies, not an injunction preventing the shutdown under this chapter. These are WARN remedies, **not** Fifth Amendment property compensation or Tucker Act forum rules.
