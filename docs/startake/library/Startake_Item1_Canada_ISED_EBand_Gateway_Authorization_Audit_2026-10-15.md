## Findings and evidence

1. **Specific authorization decision.** ISED's April 21, 2026 *Decision on Request Received from SpaceX Canada Corp. for Use of E-band Frequencies in Canada* states that SpaceX Canada Corp. requested 71–76 GHz downlink and 81–86 GHz uplink communications, referencing ITU filing **USASAT-NGSO-3X**, between Starlink spacecraft and gateway earth stations in **Janet, Alberta** and **Saint-Laurent, Quebec**. Decision D1 states that ISED **will temporarily authorize** such communication on a **no-interference, no-protection (NINP)** basis, subject to coordination with fixed-service (FS) licensees; future Canadian consultations and potential WRC-27 decisions may alter the arrangement. The decision is source evidence of ISED's determination, not itself a copied site-approved licence, individually numbered authorization or fee-paid Granted-status SMS certificate.
2. **Prior consultation and preexisting services.** ISED's January 22, 2026 *Notice of Request* names the same company, frequency bands, filing and gateway purpose, and reports use of separate Ku- and Ka-band frequencies in Canada. Neither notice nor final decision establishes legal ownership of the sites or the current holder of all preexisting site approvals.
3. **Individual site and licence gate.** ISED's *Site-approved earth station spectrum licences* guidance says gateway/feeder stations in FSS are within the site-approved class and multiple approved sites may share one spectrum licence. The *Apply for a satellite spectrum licence* and *Apply for an earth station site approval* pages distinguish the licence from specific site approval; site approval is sought after the site-approved licence is granted. ISED application guidance says authorizations move to **Granted** after fee payment; it also notes a third-party satellite authorization requires an agreement to communicate with that satellite. This describes general procedural eligibility, **not** proof the two SpaceX sites have specific granted SMS entries or an enforceable contract.
4. **Available registry route but actual record outstanding.** ISED's *Satellite spectrum authorizations services* page directs public users to a **Licence search** function to view/print licences and approvals without logging in. The reviewed official HTML did not disclose a matched licence number, SMS grant extract, operator field, gateway site coordinates, or transfer instrument for the two named sites. No inference of a complete licence census is justified.
5. **Different agencies and rights.** The ISED decision governs Canadian spectrum communication rights; it does not prove Canadian CRTC BITS provider licensing, corporate ownership of SpaceX Canada Corp., Starlink spacecraft/ground-facility title, spectrum property title or consent to assign rights in a proposed Starlink-only acquisition. The 2020 CRTC licence in the previous research pass names **Space Exploration Technologies Corp.**, while this ISED decision names **SpaceX Canada Corp.**; this is a documented identity distinction, not proof of succession.

## Contrary evidence, interpretation, and confidence

- A public **decision to temporarily authorize** a service does not furnish an individually numbered licence, site approval, current registered licensee, renewal history or evidence of continuing compliance. Accordingly treat 2026 decision as authoritative *policy action*, not a complete licence census.
- Granting NINP access cannot be described as exclusive/protected property rights. Fixed-service coordination and potentially changed post-WRC-27 Canadian policy expressly limit continuity.
- The two named sites are not necessarily all Canadian Starlink gateway locations. No completeness assertion follows from a two-site request.
- Named applicant **SpaceX Canada Corp.** does not establish registered land/equipment owner; satellite licence holder may legally be another person when the necessary agreement exists.
